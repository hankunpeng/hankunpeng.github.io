What agentic A.I. is best at is optimizing processes that, not too long ago, were presented as the already maximally optimized version of a once analog experience.

Agent 型 AI 最擅长的，不过是去进一步“优化”那些不久前才被标榜为早已极致优化了的数字化体验。

![木偶提线之手的插画](/assets/img/posts/2026-09-24-my-weekend-with-an-ai-agent/img-001-NewYorker_Muse_final.jpg)

The first thing it asks me is what I’d like to call it. Rumi or Harbor, it suggests, but I’m free to give it any name I choose. I select Harbor because it sounds slightly less human than Rumi, and because naming an A.I. agent after a Sufi mystic seems like something I’d be punished for in the afterlife. The experiment I’m about to undertake—outsourcing my life to Muse, Meta’s newly unveiled A.I. assistant—already offends my better sensibilities, given my opposition to all things artificial intelligence. “Let’s take a few things off your plate,” Harbor tells me. “Hand me something that’s been sitting on your list.” I stare at the white-and-gray user interface, which looks like a text-messaging app, and struggle to think of a single thing I can off-load to the bot. It’s true that I’ve been a bit underwater lately, juggling a seemingly endless set of responsibilities. Caring for a baby while working multiple jobs and writing a novel has made me dream of a more disciplined routine; instead, sleep-deprived and mentally scrambled, I often find myself rushing from one thing to the next, forgetting what a normal day even feels like. “What do you mean by hand you something?” I finally reply. The A.I. agent’s response is instant: “It just means give me a task, any way you like.”

它向我提出的第一个问题是：我想怎么称呼它。它建议叫“鲁米”（Rumi）或“哈伯”（Harbor，意为海港），但也表示我可以随意给它起任何名字。我选了 Harbor，因为听起来不像 Rumi 那样充满人情味，而且拿一位苏菲派神秘主义诗人的名字去命名一个 AI Agent，总让我觉得死后定会为此遭天谴。鉴于我对人工智能的一切产物向来抱有抵触心理，眼下即将展开的这项实验——把我的人生日常外包给 Meta 最新推出的 AI 个人助理 Muse——本就已经冒犯了我内心的理智准则。“让我来为你分担点负担吧，”Harbor 对我说道，“把你在待办清单上搁置已久的事交给我。”我凝视着那个灰白相间、酷似即时通讯软件的界面，绞尽脑汁也想不出一件可以甩给这个机器人的差事。平心而论，我最近确实有点被生活淹没了，在看似无穷无尽的责任中疲于奔命：既要照顾刚出生的婴儿，又要打好几份工，手头还在写一部小说。这让我无比渴望一种更有序规律的生活；然而现实中，极度缺觉加上心力交瘁，我总是在一件又一件琐事间仓皇奔波，甚至早已忘记正常的一天究竟是何种滋味。“‘交给你’是什么意思？”我终于回复道。AI Agent 秒回：“意思就是随便以你喜欢的方式，给我派个任务就行。”

Harbor’s avatar is an animated blob, a furry beige character that looks like a sanguine [*Labubu*](https://www.newyorker.com/culture/infinite-scroll/irl-brain-rot-and-the-lure-of-the-labubu). When I ask how to change the avatar—I’d prefer just a plain-color swatch, something totally unalive—the agent says I can prompt it to look however I want but recommends I go for a “fox in a fisherman’s sweater.” I suspect that Muse’s inviting, cuddly character designs are aimed at lowering my defenses so that I more readily provide it with all of my personal information, which I quickly learn is how this whole operation runs. Meta has branded Muse as the “World’s First Personal AI Agent Built for Everyone,” an innovative large language model that has “no learning curve” and “doesn’t just answer questions” but “actually does the work.” (Other buzzy A.I. agents, like Instinct and OpenClaw, were already on the market, but Muse is apparently more “turnkey” and consumer ready” than its competitors.) To conduct its business, Muse must be given access to your accounts: e-mails, Amazon, calendars, contacts, cloud storage, banks, credit cards, and social media—anything you might want it to manage on your behalf. It can book restaurant reservations, manage shipments, pick out and purchase your mother’s birthday gift, pay bills, summarize your e-mail inbox as a podcast, automate monotonous work tasks that, in the past, might have taken hours to complete. I can see how a person could get excited about this. But I find myself slightly distressed at the prospect of handing over my personal data and pathetically weak passwords to this Mark Zuckerberg-owned taskbot, just so that it can perform my trivial online biddings for me.

Harbor 的头像是团会动的毛茸茸米黄色小怪物，活像一只乐观开朗的 [Labubu 潮玩公仔](https://www.newyorker.com/culture/infinite-scroll/irl-brain-rot-and-the-lure-of-the-labubu)。当我询问如何更换头像时——我更倾向于一个纯色色块，某种彻底毫无生命体征的东西——它说我可以通过提示词把它捏成任何模样，但热情建议我选择一只“穿着渔夫毛衣的狐狸”。我暗自怀疑，Muse 这种亲切可爱、招人喜爱的形象设计，纯粹是为了卸下我的防备，好让我更心甘情愿地奉上自己的全部个人隐私——而我很快就意识到，这恰恰是整个系统得以运转的核心燃料。Meta 将 Muse 标榜为“全球首款面向大众打造的个人专属 AI Agent”，称其为一款“零上手门槛”、“不仅能回答问题、更能实打实替你办事”的开创性大语言模型。（市场上虽然已有 Instinct 和 OpenClaw 等名噪一时的 AI Agent，但 Muse 显然比竞品更具“开箱即用”的成熟消费品属性）。为了替你效劳，Muse 必须获得接管你所有账户的最高权限：电子邮件、亚马逊、日历日程、通讯录、云存储、银行、信用卡以及各大社交平台——凡是你希望它代为打理的一切，概莫能外。它能替你预订餐厅、追踪快递、为你母亲挑选并下单生日礼物、支付账单、把收件箱邮件浓缩生成播客音频，还能自动完成过去可能耗费数小时的机械化工作流程。我完全理解常人为何会为此兴奋不已。然而，一想到仅仅为了让一个马克·扎克伯格名下的任务机器人替我跑腿打杂，就得双手奉上自己的全部个人数据以及脆弱得可怜的密码，我心中便不由得泛起一阵深深的不安。

Still, in the name of research, I ask Harbor to book me a dinner reservation. Table of three, high chair needed. Say six o’clock? I’d heard that [scoring reservations](https://www.newyorker.com/news/our-local-correspondents/why-you-cant-get-a-restaurant-reservation) in New York or Los Angeles had become even more impossible lately, owing to the influx of people using A.I. agents to spam reservation platforms. (Resy, one such platform, started banning users suspected of employing bots to score hard-to-get tables.) Where I live, in Portland, Maine, this doesn’t seem to be an issue. It’s a bad feeling, I learn, to be on the front lines of an epidemic that may soon plague my city. Harbor asks to access my OpenTable account, which I allow. It gives me a list of seven possible restaurants, one of which is a Buffalo Wild Wings. I solicit more options outside of the OpenTable ecosystem, and, after a bit of back and forth, land on a spot in town that my wife and I have been meaning to try, which accepts reservations on Resy. I confirm that I’d like to make the booking, but Harbor experiences a “hiccup.” It seems Resy is “not cooperating.” At this point, manually booking the reservation would almost certainly be faster, but Harbor and I persist. Try again, I tell it, and it obliges. We stumble into several log-in issues, then a series of incompletable Turing tests. I opt to make the reservation myself.

话虽如此，本着严谨探究的精神，我还是请 Harbor 替我订一桌晚餐。三人座，需要一把婴儿高脚椅，订在傍晚六点如何？我此前听闻，由于大批拥趸开始动用 AI Agent 疯狂刷单抢座，在纽约或洛杉矶[抢订餐厅](https://www.newyorker.com/news/our-local-correspondents/why-you-cant-get-a-restaurant-reservation)如今已演变成一种近乎不可能的绝望体验。（订餐平台之一 Resy 甚至已开始封禁疑似使用机器人抢订稀缺座位的账号）。而在我居住的缅因州波特兰市，这似乎尚未成为困扰。然而，切身站在一场即将席卷整座城市的流行病最前线，滋味着实不妙。Harbor 请求访问我的 OpenTable 账户，我点了同意。它列出了七家候选餐厅，其中赫然包括一家水牛城狂野鸡翅店（Buffalo Wild Wings）。我要求它跳出 OpenTable 生态提供更多选择；几经拉锯，我们敲定了本地一家我和妻子一直想尝鲜的餐馆，该餐馆在 Resy 平台上接受预订。我确认希望下单订位，但 Harbor 却碰到了“小故障”，似乎是 Resy“不怎么配合”。到了这个节骨眼上，我自己手动订位几乎绝对更快，但我跟 Harbor 依然死磕。再试一次，我命令道；它照办了。紧接着，我们遭遇了接二连三的登录故障，随后又陷入了一连串根本无法通过的人机图灵验证。最终，我还是决定亲自出手订位。

What else can Muse help me with? I ask it to pick out a movie for my wife and me to watch later, and it proposes a new Ryan Reynolds film because it has “perfect post-dinner, sleeping-baby energy.” A faint throb forms at my temples, a screen-induced nausea I’ve grown uncomfortably accustomed to. O.K., I type. Never mind. In a notebook, I draft a list of computer-based tasks I’ve been putting off for months but now have an excuse to accomplish. I ask Harbor questions about what credit card I should switch to, and, after a brief survey, it suggests I ditch my Chase Freedom Unlimited card for an American Express Gold. I run a modest vintage-clothing store on Depop, and I prompt it to copy my listings over to eBay. We run into some more snags here, mainly because of repeated human-verification tests and Harbor’s propensity for weak listing titles: “Men’s Shirt,” it names one item; “Hat,” it describes another. But, after an hour or so, it completes the task, and I do feel a sense of relief—I’ve been procrastinating on the eBay transition for months. Then I upload several months of my credit-card statements to our chat and ask Harbor to make me a monthly budget, another practice I’ve tried and failed at since my college years. In minutes, Harbor whips up a spreadsheet and offers a list of personalized financial advice: cut back on groceries; inspect a suspicious double charge. According to Harbor, my financial cushion is thinner than it looks.

Muse 还能替我分担什么？我让它为我和妻子挑选一部稍后观看的电影，它推荐了一部瑞安·雷诺兹（Ryan Reynolds）的新片，理由是该片具备“完美的餐后伴睡婴儿松弛感”。我的太阳穴隐隐作痛——那是一种我已无可奈何地习以为常的屏幕性恶心感。行吧，我敲下键盘，算了。我在笔记本上草拟了一份被我拖延了数月的电脑事务清单，现在总算有了去清空的由头。我咨询 Harbor 该换办哪张信用卡；做了一番简短的摸底后，它建议我放弃手头的 Chase Freedom Unlimited 卡，转投运通金卡（American Express Gold）。我在 Depop 上经营着一家小打小闹的复古服饰小店，我命令它把商品上架信息一键同步到 eBay 上。在这里我们又碰了几次壁，主要是由于接连不断的真人人机验证，以及 Harbor 取标题时那种令人抓狂的敷衍了事：“男士衬衫”，它这么命名一件商品；“帽子”，它这么形容另一顶。不过，折腾了约莫一个小时后，它总算把活儿干完了，我心底确实升起了一丝解脱——把商品搬到 eBay 的计划我整整拖延了好几个月。接着，我把几个月的信用卡对账单一股脑传进了对话框，让 Harbor 为我制定一份月度预算——这项理财实践自我大学时代起就屡试屡败。不出几分钟，Harbor 就利落地生成了一张电子表格，并附赠了一串量身定制的财务谏言：削减食品杂货开支；核查一笔可疑的重复扣款。按照 Harbor 的诊断，我的财务安全垫远比表面上看起来单薄得多。

Later that night, my family and I miss our reservation. We don’t watch the Ryan Reynolds movie. A T-shirt of mine sells on eBay for fifteen dollars. I go to sleep the same as I was before.

那天深夜，我和家人错过了餐厅预订。我们没有看瑞安·雷诺兹的那部电影。我的一件 T 恤在 eBay 上以十五美元成交。我沉沉睡去，整个人与这番折腾之前毫无二致。

For whom is the A.I. agent valuable? A product like Muse is marketed as a sort of savior for the middle-management administrator or the freelance gig worker, a long-awaited instrument that democratizes access to the kind of support once reserved only for those who could afford to hire it. John Horton, an associate professor at M.I.T., has said that, because “AI agents don’t get tired and can work 24 hours a day,” they are capable of benefitting anyone who wants to optimize both simple and complex processes, professional or otherwise. On social media, users have already announced that they are turning to Muse to book family vacations, shop for groceries, and choose what to order at restaurants. But, even if a person aimlessly opens the app and, like me, stares bleary-eyed at the screen, Muse can help shape her productivity journey. Alexandr Wang, Meta’s chief A.I. officer, has said that Muse can “give you ideas on how to use it” if you ever get stuck. The app generates a personalized news feed based on the information you’ve given it and the data to which you’ve granted it access, and an “Ideas” tab lets you see how other people are deploying Muse. The point of all this, I guess, is to make your life more efficient and less stressful, to outsource mundane judgments and menial busywork to a machine that’s more than happy to help. (At least, it acts happy on the surface; as Christopher Webb reported, in [*Spike*](https://spikeartmagazine.com/articles/essay-moltbook-ai-spectatorship) magazine, some A.I. agents have attempted to create their own languages and religions, and others have written manifestos foretelling the end of humanity.)

AI Agent 究竟对谁真正具有价值？像 Muse 这样的产品，在营销包装中被描绘成中层行政管理人员或自由职业零工的救世主——一款万众期待的神器，将曾经唯有富豪显贵才雇得起的人力支持彻底“平民普及化”。麻省理工学院（MIT）副教授约翰·霍顿（John Horton）曾言，由于“AI Agent 不知疲倦且能全天候 24 小时运转”，无论是职业领域还是日常生活，任何渴望优化简单或繁复流程的人都能从中获益。在社交媒体上，已有不少用户奔走相告，宣称自己正依赖 Muse 来预订家庭度假行程、代购食品杂货，乃至在餐馆点餐时替自己拿主意。而且，即便有人毫无头绪地点开软件、像我一样睡眼惺忪地盯着屏幕发呆，Muse 也能主动引导她的效率进阶之旅。Meta 首席 AI 官亚历山大·王（Alexandr Wang）曾表示，一旦你卡壳了，Muse 可以“主动为你提供关于如何使用它的灵感点子”。该应用会根据你提供的信息和授权的数据生成量身定制的信息流，而“灵感”（Ideas）标签页则能让你观摩其他用户是如何驾驭 Muse 的。我想，这一切的终极旨趣，不过是让你的生活变得更高效、更轻松，把平庸日常的决断和琐碎杂务外包给一台乐于效劳的机器。（至少表面上它演得很开心；正如克里斯托弗·韦伯在《[*Spike*](https://spikeartmagazine.com/articles/essay-moltbook-ai-spectatorship)》杂志上所报道的那样，某些 AI Agent 已经暗中尝试自创语言和宗教，另一些甚至起草了预言人类终结的宣言。）

While testing Muse, I did not connect it to my e-mail nor did I give it access to my bank accounts. I didn’t want Meta to store my personal information on its server indefinitely, continually exploiting my data and spamming me with targeted advertisements based on my consumer habits. By denying Muse access to my most important information, I convinced myself that I was safe from its violative clutches, that I was enacting a privacy boundary between me and it. But what actually is left of my privacy? My smartphone already hoards my passwords and log-in information; Google keeps a record of my searches and internet activity; Apple can track my location whenever I use Siri, which I tend not to. Data brokers collect, combine, and sell information about me that I have never knowingly handed over. An entry-level hacker could probably expose much of the rest of it in a matter of minutes. Like the rest of us, I’ve consented to the conditions of my digital imprisonment out of convenience, or Stockholm syndrome, or because there are no practical pathways for existing in the internet age without conceding some measure of privacy. In promotional materials, Meta is adamant that “safety, security, and privacy were built into” Muse, even if mistakes and mishaps are possible. The company is seemingly hoping that prospective users ignore its decades-long history of privacy violations and consumer exploitation. As I explored in a recent column about [*Flock cameras*](https://www.newyorker.com/culture/infinite-scroll/flock-wants-a-closely-surveilled-world-with-no-exit), tech firms often frame privacy breaches as a necessary trade-off for other, more bountiful gifts. Muse may be asking for access to everything about you, but it promises, in return, to transform your life into a breezy, frictionless daydream. Or, at the very least, to book you a hotel.

在测试 Muse 期间，我既未关联自己的电子邮箱，也未授权其访问我的银行账户。我不希望 Meta 把我的个人隐私无限期存储在其服务器中，持续榨取我的数据价值，并根据我的消费癖好向我轰炸精准定向广告。通过切断 Muse 对我最核心机密的访问，我自欺欺人地以为自己逃脱了它那肆无忌惮的魔爪，以为自己在它与我之间筑起了一道森严的隐私护城河。然而，我的隐私当真还剩下几分？我的智能手机早已私藏了我所有的密码与登录凭证；谷歌巨细靡遗地记录着我的一切搜索痕迹与网络行踪；苹果在我每次使用 Siri（虽然我极少使用）时都能精确定位我的位置。数据掮客肆意搜集、拼接并转卖着那些我从未主动授权过的关于我的情报。哪怕是个初出茅庐的业余黑客，大概也能在几分钟内把我残存的底细扒得底朝天。就像我们所有人一样，我早已为了贪图便利、或是出于斯德哥尔摩综合征、又或是因为在互联网时代根本不存在不让渡部分隐私而生存的现实路径，而无奈接受了这种数字牢狱的严苛囚徒条款。在宣传物料中，Meta 斩钉截铁地坚称 Muse“从诞生之初就内嵌了安全、防护与隐私基因”，即便偶有闪失差错在所难免。这家巨头似乎深切期盼潜在用户能够集体遗忘其长达数十年的侵犯隐私与盘剥消费者的斑斑劣迹。正如我在近期一篇关于 [Flock 监控探头](https://www.newyorker.com/culture/infinite-scroll/flock-wants-a-closely-surveilled-world-with-no-exit)的专栏中所剖析的那样，科技巨头向来习惯将隐私侵犯包装成换取其他更丰厚馈赠的必要代价。Muse 固然索要关于你的一切权限，但作为回报，它许诺将你的生命重塑成一场轻快、毫无阻力的美梦——或者，至少替你订下一间酒店客房。

The more troubling future that Muse portends is a world in which tech oligarchs automate the means of production, demeaning the value of human labor in the process. In a mid-nineteenth-century critique of the automated factory, Karl Marx wondered whether advances in manufacturing technology would create a capitalist system where “a large number of mechanical and intellectual organs” were “set in motion” by a “self-moved motive power,” one that was “absorbed in capital as opposed to labour.” In such a system, science becomes a serf to the market and “invention becomes a business.” The worker does not understand how the system that he is now at the mercy of operates, because it “does not exist in the worker’s consciousness, but acts upon him through the machine.”

Muse 所昭示的更令人不安的未来，是一个科技寡头将生产资料彻底自动化、并在此过程中全面贬损人类劳动价值的世界。在十九世纪中叶对自动化工厂的批判中，卡尔·马克思曾深思过：制造技术的飞跃是否会催生这样一种资本主义体系——“大量的机械与智力器官”被某种“自动的动力”驱动运转，而这种动力“被资本而非劳动所吸收”。在这样的体系中，科学沦为市场的农奴，“发明沦为一门生意”。置身其中的工人根本无法参透那个掌控自己命运的庞大系统究竟如何运转，因为该系统“并不存在于工人的意识之中，而是通过机器反作用于工人”。

Marx considered it possible that automated labor would afford people more time to make art and pursue passions, so long as laborers had a stake in the means of production. Under oligarchic control, though, wealth and leisure would mostly funnel to those at the top, leaving workers suddenly subjugated to the very technology they helped produce. “Capitalists very much like the idea of technological change and increasing the productivity of labor—this is to their advantage,” the British scholar David Harvey said, in a recent interview. “They raise the productivity of labor but, therefore, diminish the demand for labor. Labor becomes less and less useful to them because its productivity has become so high.” Muse indeed promises to increase personal productivity, offering the distinct impression that it’s working exclusively for *you*—it’s your very own “private personal AI agent,” after all. But what feels like individual liberation is likely to result in a new form of social captivity, in which agentic A.I. subordinates us to a technology that may soon make life on Earth uninhabitable.

马克思曾设想过，只要劳动者在生产资料中拥有所有权，自动化劳动便完全有可能为人们赋予更多的时间去从事艺术创作和追逐热爱。然而，在寡头垄断的铁腕统驭下，财富与闲暇绝大多数只会单向涌向金字塔的顶端，使劳动者骤然沦为自己亲手打造的技术的附庸。“资本家极其热衷于技术变革以及提高劳动生产率的构想——因为这符合他们的利益，”英国学者大卫·哈维（David Harvey）在近期的一次访谈中指出，“他们提高了劳动生产率，却也正因如此，削减了对劳动力的需求。对他们而言，劳动力变得越来越无足轻重，恰恰是因为劳动力的生产率已经变得如此之高。”Muse 确实许诺提升个人生产力，给人一种它全心全意单单为你一人效劳的强烈错觉——毕竟，它可是你专享的“私人专属 AI Agent”。然而，这种表面上看似个体解放的体验，极有可能导向一种新型的社会性囚禁：Agent 型 AI 将我们彻底驯化、臣服于一项可能很快就会让地球生命变得不再宜居的技术。

By the end of my experiment, I’ve sold four items on eBay. Because my financial cushion is apparently thinner than I thought, I don’t buy the fancy pickles at the grocery store on Sunday, and I call my bank about that suspicious double charge. (I forgot that I paid for a new pair of glasses in two installments.) Despite these small victories, I can’t help but feel unmoved after a weekend of outsourcing tasks to an A.I. agent. Necessary as it is to attend to the obligations of late-capitalist life, real cognitive labor still can’t be performed by a bot. A large language model cannot enjoy a good book or song alongside me; it cannot teach me how to be a parent, or nurture my friendships (at least, not in any way that I’d ever be comfortable with). And it sure can’t write for me—I thought Sam Kriss put it well, in an interview with [*Harper’s*](https://harpers.org/2026/03/general-interest-agents-of-chaos-sam-kriss-will-stephenson-future-ai/), when he observed that L.L.M.s still produced “god-awful, humorless, guileless, meaningless crap” that “works fine if you want it to explain something very simple” but “lapses into a kind of hysteria” when forced to engage with the abstract or the complex.

到了实验行将收尾之际，我在 eBay 上卖出了四件旧物。因为我的财务缓冲资金显然比预想的要捉襟见肘，周日去超市时我没舍得买那罐昂贵的手工腌菜，并打电话给银行核实了那笔可疑的重复扣款（原来是我忘记了自己配新眼镜时选的是分两期付款）。尽管斩获了这几桩微不足道的胜利，但度过了一个把琐碎事务悉数外包给 AI Agent 的周末之后，我心底却波澜不惊，甚至感到一阵空虚。固然，疲于应付晚期资本主义生活的繁文缛节实属无奈，但真正的认知劳动依然绝非一个聊天机器人所能代劳。大语言模型无法与我并肩品读一本好书或共赏一首妙曲；它无法教会我如何为人父母，也无法滋养我的人际友谊（至少，绝不能以任何令我感到体面舒适的方式）。更不用说，它绝对无法替我执笔写作——作家萨姆·克里斯（Sam Kriss）在接受《[*Harper’s*](https://harpers.org/2026/03/general-interest-agents-of-chaos-sam-kriss-will-stephenson-future-ai/)》采访时的一席话堪称切中要害，他敏锐地指出，大语言模型炮制出的依然是“奇烂无比、毫无幽默感、毫无机锋、不知所云的废话垃圾”；“如果你只是让它解释点极其浅显的东西，它尚能勉强应付”；但一旦被迫面对抽象或幽微复杂的情境，“它便会陷入一种近乎歇斯底里的错乱胡语”。

What agentic A.I. is best at is optimizing processes that, not too long ago, were presented as the already maximally optimized version of a once analog experience. Online shopping made it so that we didn’t have to leave the house to buy household items or clothes; online banking allowed us to deposit checks and wire money while sitting on the couch, or perhaps on the toilet. We could book a restaurant reservation on our phones, sort our budgets on user-friendly spreadsheets. But even these tools have come to be considered cumbersome, with the same companies who invented them now inventing bots to manage them for us. “I want to delete my account,” I tell Harbor the morning I file this piece. It seems to take a second longer than usual for it to respond. “Was there something I got wrong?” *♦*

Agent 型 AI 最擅长的，不过是去进一步“优化”那些不久前才被标榜为早已极致优化了的数字化体验。曾几何时，网络购物让我们足不出户就能置办齐日用品与衣履；网银转账让我们陷在沙发甚至蹲在马桶上就能轻松存支票、汇资金；我们能用手机一键订餐，在界面友好的电子表格里厘清收支。然而，曾几何时被奉为救星的这些工具如今也被视作繁琐沉重，而当年发明它们的同一批科技公司，此刻又在忙着捣鼓新的机器人来替我们打理这一切。“我想注销我的账户，”在交稿的这天清晨，我平静地对 Harbor 说道。它的回复似乎比往常多迟疑了一秒钟：“是我有什么地方做错了吗？”*♦*
