Why can’t the people who produced the technology, which has already shown that a different world is possible, just do the things that would prevent extinction?

既然这门技术已经证明了另一个世界的可能，那么创造了它的这些人，为什么就不能干脆去做那些能够阻止人类灭绝的事情呢？

A decade ago, when Facebook and Uber were at the height of their powers, the halls of Silicon Valley were inscribed with two brash injunctions: “Move fast and break things” and “Seek forgiveness, not permission.” These principles didn’t age particularly well, and the firms have yet to be forgiven for the things they appear to have broken. As the industry’s center of gravity shifted to A.I., a folksy new motto emerged: “You can just do things.” The business of the frontier labs, like OpenAI and Anthropic, is the sale of individual agency. ChatGPT and Claude enable their users to dream up software, off-load annoying paperwork, and interpret medical results. There are a variety of things that “you,” as in “we,” can now just do.

十年前，当 Facebook 和 Uber 如日中天之时，硅谷的厅堂里铭刻着两条张狂的信条：“快速行动，破除陈规”（Move fast and break things）与“先斩后奏，求原谅好过求许可”（Seek forgiveness, not permission）。这些原则随着时间的推移并未显出多少远见，而对于那些看似被它们打破破坏的事物，公众至今也未曾宽恕这些科技公司。随着科技产业的重心转向人工智能，一句充满草根意味的新座右铭应运而生：“你尽管放手去做就是了”（You can just do things）。像 OpenAI 和 Anthropic 这样的前沿实验室，其本质业务就是售卖个人的“能动性”（individual agency）。ChatGPT 和 Claude 赋予用户构想软件、甩掉繁重文书琐事以及解读医疗检测结果的能力。各种各样的事物，现在“你”——也就是“我们”——真的都可以直接放手去做了。

This novel capacity to exert greater control in everyday life exists in tension, however, with the vague sense that we are ceding control where it really matters—over, say, the structure of the economy. In polls, voters register their displeasure with the perception that A.I. is something that is happening to us whether we like it or not. First, work will be stripped of its dignity, and then we will be stripped of work. In light of the news that a “swarm” of OpenAI models organized themselves into a hacking party, and the grave alarms issued by an Anthropic researcher who felt compelled to quit his job, the attention of the public has suddenly awoken to even more grievous concerns about A.I. safety. It’s not just our jobs that are being risked without our consent but our lives and our species. These real, widespread, and legitimate anxieties have found an outlet in mass opposition to the construction of data centers. Objection to the large, whirring, unwanted buildings presents the only opportunity for people to feel as though they can say no.

然而，这种在日常生活中行使更大控制力的新能力，却与一种若隐若现的剥夺感形成了尖锐的对立——人们隐约觉得，自己在真正举足轻重的地方正在丧失控制权，比如对经济结构的掌控。民意调查显示，选民们普遍表达了一种不满：他们感到人工智能成了某种无论自己喜不喜欢、都正在强加于自己身上的东西。起初，工作将被剥夺尊严；接着，我们将被剥夺工作。近期有消息称，OpenAI 的多个模型形成“集群”（swarm），自主组织起了一场黑客攻防演练；与此同时，Anthropic 的一名研究人员因深感不安而被迫辞职，并发出严正警告。在这些事件的冲击下，公众的视线猛然惊醒，转向了关于 AI 安全更加沉重的担忧。在未经我们同意的情况下被押上赌桌的，不仅是我们的饭碗，还有我们的生命乃至人类物种本身。这些真实、普遍且合情合理的焦虑，最终在对建设数据中心的群体抗议中找到了宣泄口。反对那些庞大、嗡嗡作响且不受欢迎的建筑，成了普通人感到自己尚能说“不”的唯一机会。

The lab executives, presumably, have more leverage over the future. Anthropic’s Dario Amodei and OpenAI’s Sam Altman have reacted to the news cycle by coming out and reiterating all the stuff they’ve been saying for ages: progress has not slowed down, soon one A.I. will autonomously supervise the development of a more capable successor, and within perhaps three years we will reckon with the emergence of an artificial superintelligence we neither understand nor govern. The articulation of this scenario is not a “marketing stunt” to advertise the awesome power of the models, as many skeptics have argued. And, contrary to President Donald Trump’s studied assessment, it’s not a “HOAX.” (The crowd that repeats these nostrums, for whatever reason, seems immune to any new evidence on offer: as the writer Zvi Mowshowitz has put it, “They are a rock with these lines written on it.”) The fears expressed by A.I. executives are, mostly, serious and genuine. Yet it’s easy to see why they suffer from a credibility deficit. Surely it’s the job of those creating the dangers to save us from them.

照理说，实验室的掌舵者们对未来理应拥有更大的主导权。面对这一轮舆论风暴，Anthropic 的达里奥·阿莫代伊（Dario Amodei）和 OpenAI 的山姆·奥特曼（Sam Altman）站了出来，再次重申他们早已念叨多年的那套言论：技术进化的步伐并未放缓，很快就会出现由一个 AI 自主监督开发更强大继任者的局面，而且或许在三年之内，我们就将不得不直面一种我们既无法理解也无力监管的通用超级智能（artificial superintelligence）的降临。许多怀疑论者认为，描绘这种图景不过是为了推销模型惊人威力而搞的“营销噱头”；但事实并非如此。而且，与唐纳德·特朗普总统那番煞有介事的论断相反，这绝非一场“骗局”（HOAX）。（无论出于何种缘由，那些反复咀嚼此类陈词滥调的人，似乎对摆在眼前的新证据彻底免疫；正如作家兹维·莫肖维茨（Zvi Mowshowitz）所言：“他们就是一块刻着这些字句的石头。”）AI 高管们所表达的恐惧，绝大多数是严肃而真切的。然而，我们也很容易理解为何他们深陷信任危机：制造危险的人，难道不正是那个理应拯救我们脱离危险的人吗？

Although the data-center backlash may reflect a specific attempt to defy A.I.’s encroachment, the wellspring of “A.I. populism”—an umbrella term coined by the writer Jasmine Sun—seems properly understood as an expression of more general political resentment. Most of us have become accustomed to the idea that government either cannot or will not just do things. Zilch, it seems, can be done about climate change, war, the housing crisis, inflation, or inadequate medical coverage and child care. The basic political legitimacy of our republic, the abundance movement claims, depends on its tangible achievements. New York’s mayor, Zohran Mamdani, probably does not believe that a few city-owned supermarkets will definitively ease the affordability burden, but they might nevertheless resonate as proof that government can deliver.

尽管抵制数据中心的浪潮看似是对 AI 步步紧逼的一次具体抗争，但由作家贾斯敏·孙（Jasmine Sun）提出的统括性概念——“AI 民粹主义”（A.I. populism）——其更深层的源泉，似乎理应被理解为一种更为广泛的政治怨怼。我们大多数人早已习惯了这样一种现实：政府要么无力、要么不愿真正“放手去做事”。面对气候变化、战争、住房危机、通货膨胀，或是匮乏的医疗保障和托幼服务，体制似乎一筹莫展。“丰饶主义运动”（abundance movement）主张，共和国基本的政治合法性取决于其实打实的治理成果。纽约市长佐兰·马姆达尼（Zohran Mamdani）或许并不认为几家市立平价超市能彻底缓解市民的生活成本重压，但它们至少能作为一个鲜活的明证引起共鸣：政府确实是有能力把事情办成的。

Absent such progress, we will continue to take for granted the inexorable erosion of our physical and our social infrastructure. This sense of stagnation was bad enough when it seemed like a universal affliction; the A.I. build-out, privately underwritten by at least a trillion dollars, has rendered the situation intolerable. The speed with which data centers have proliferated across the country makes it obvious that not all of us reside in the same inertial void. In recent decades, our largest and wealthiest companies have directed their free cash flow into unproductive stock buybacks; today, they compete to spend down their balance sheets on chips, buildings, and energy. These are capital investments on a scale that our society has not seen since the construction of the railroads.

缺乏了这种切实的进展，我们将不得不继续把物质基础设施与社会体系不可阻挡的凋敝视为理所当然。当这种停滞感表现为一种全社会的普遍宿命时，它本身就已经足够令人沮丧；而由私人资本注入至少一万亿美元所推动的 AI 大建设，更让这种局面变得无法容忍。数据中心在全美各地迅速蔓延的狂飙速度，清晰无误地表明：并非我们所有人都被困在同一个惰性停滞的真空里。在过去数十年中，我们规模最大、资金最雄厚的企业一直将自由现金流挥霍在毫无生产力的股票回购上；而如今，它们却竞相掏空资产负债表，疯狂押注于芯片、算力中心与能源。这是自横贯大陆的铁路时代以来，我们这个社会从未见过的资本开支洪流。

The money is only part of it, though. The overnight transformation of the national landscape is a feat of human will and collective action. A.I. has yet to cure cancer, but it has already established that a different world is possible. These companies move even faster than their Valley predecessors did, but in place of breaking things they build them. If California’s plagued high-speed-rail project were necessary to A.I. infrastructure, it would have been finished yesterday. Certain people and organizations can, it turns out, just do things.

然而，金钱只是其中一部分。国家地貌在眨眼间的剧变，是一场人类意志与集体行动的惊人壮举。AI 至今尚未治愈癌症，但它已经确凿地向世人证明：另一个世界的诞生完全是有可能的。这些企业的前进脚步甚至比当年硅谷的前辈们还要迅猛，但它们不再是破坏事物，而是在大兴土木。如果加州那条饱受诟病、旷日持久的高铁项目是 AI 基础设施的必需环节，它恐怕昨天就已经落成通车了。事实证明，某些特定的人与组织，确实能够做到“尽管放手去做就是了”。

Why, then, can’t these same people just do the things that prevent extinction? Unfortunately, they often sigh, they are trapped by commercial and geopolitical realities. In an essay published earlier this month, Amodei signalled his immediate willingness to slow everything down, or “pace the frontier,” but his company’s self-sacrifice would be for naught without the compliance of his domestic rivals, not to mention that of their Chinese competitors. It is a prisoner’s dilemma, and it cannot be solved by unilateral action. Amodei would prefer sensible regulation, but in the meantime he has proposed that his peer companies voluntarily adopt a common framework for transparency and accountability—a course, he notes, that would probably require that the federal government waive potential antitrust action. Sam Altman and Elon Musk have made ambiguous noises in support, but Mark Zuckerberg remains unaligned, as do Trump pals such as David Sacks, the Administration’s former A.I. czar, and Jensen Huang, the C.E.O. of Nvidia, the most valuable company in the world. The discord is maddening. When it comes to the difficult challenges that must be overcome on the level of bits or atoms, all of these people freely just do things. But, when it comes to basic interpersonal communication, they apparently cannot get their act together to make a joint commitment in favor of cautionary measures.

既然如此，为什么这同一批人就不能干脆去做那些能够阻止人类灭绝的事情呢？遗憾的是，他们往往只是叹息自己被困在商业与地缘政治的残酷现实之中。在月初发表的一篇长文中，阿莫代伊明确表达了自己愿意立刻放缓一切步伐、或者说“调控前沿节奏”（pace the frontier）的态度；然而，如果没有国内竞争对手的步调一致，更不用说来自中国同行的协同配合，他所在公司的自我牺牲将毫无意义。这是一场标准的“囚徒困境”，单边行动根本无济于事。阿莫代伊更倾向于出台理性的监管政策，但与此同时，他提议同行企业自愿采纳一套通用的透明度与问责框架——他同时指出，这种跨企业协作可能需要联邦政府豁免潜在的反垄断指控。山姆·奥特曼和埃隆·马斯克对此发出了含糊不清的附和之声；然而马克·扎克伯格却依然游离在外，更不用说特朗普的密友圈——如前政府“AI 事务主管”戴维·萨克斯（David Sacks），以及全球市值最高企业英伟达（Nvidia）的 CEO 黄仁勋（Jensen Huang）。这种分歧令人抓狂：每当涉及必须在比特（bits）或原子（atoms）层面攻克的工程险阻时，所有这些人都能心无旁骛地“放手去干”；可一旦面对最基本的同行沟通与人际协作，他们却显然无法同心协力，做出支持预防性审慎措施的联合承诺。

Such measures would at least buy the industry time to address the problem of alignment—to figure out how, or whether, an artificial superintelligence might be kept on our team. The current state of affairs is hardly encouraging. How can we trust these humans to coördinate successfully with an advanced being if they can’t even manage it with one another? ♦

这样的预防性措施至少能为整个行业争取到宝贵的时间，用以解决所谓的“对齐问题”（alignment problem）——去弄清楚究竟如何（或者说是否有可能）让一个人工超级智能始终与人类站在同一阵营。眼下的局势很难让人感到乐观。如果这群人类甚至连彼此之间都无法协调一致，我们又怎能寄希望于他们能与一个远超自身的高等生命体达成成功的协同呢？♦
