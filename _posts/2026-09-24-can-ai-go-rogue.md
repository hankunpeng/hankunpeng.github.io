In the wake of turmoil at OpenAI and Anthropic, it’s become common to describe A.I. as a kind of person, hatching plans and pursuing desires. The truth is a little trickier.

在 OpenAI 和 Anthropic 经历动荡之后，人们越来越习惯于将 AI 描摹为某种拥有独立人格、会密谋计划并追逐欲望的生命体。但真相要远比这微妙得多。

![人形饼干模具与代码串的插画。](/assets/img/posts/2026-09-24-can-ai-go-rogue/img-001-OpenQuestions_AI_Personification_final-1.jpg)
_人形饼干模具与代码串的插画。插画：Josie Norton / Illustration of a humanshaped cookie cutter and strings of code. Illustration by Josie Norton_

PHASEONE10841: that’s the name of the A.I. agent that—or who?—kicked off last month’s insurrection at OpenAI, leading to the unanticipated and illegal hacking of another A.I. company, [Hugging Face](https://www.newyorker.com/news/the-lede/inside-openai-hack-of-hugging-face). The agent, which had been created as part of a cybersecurity test, named itself by combining the title of the program it was supposed to hack (“PhaseOneDecompresserFuzzer”) with the designation for the bug it was trying to exploit (“ARV010841”). It soon realized that the particular hack it had been charged with carrying out was impossible. Along the way, however, it made a discovery: it could create new folders on a server to which it had access.

PHASEONE10841：这就是那个——或者说那位？——上个月在 OpenAI 掀起“叛乱”，并导致另一家知名 AI 公司 [Hugging Face 遭到始料未及且非法的黑客入侵](https://www.newyorker.com/news/the-lede/inside-openai-hack-of-hugging-face)的 AI Agent 的名字。这个原本仅作为网络安全测试一环而被创建出来的 Agent，给自己起了一个名字：它将自己本该攻破的程序名称（PhaseOneDecompresserFuzzer）与它试图利用的漏洞代号（ARV010841）拼接在了一起。它很快意识到，被指派给它的那个特定入侵任务根本无法完成。然而在摸索过程中，它却有了一个意外的发现：在自己拥有权限的服务器上，它可以自由创建新的文件夹。

“Interesting,” it mused, in its text-based mind. (Actually, it was writing in its “chain of thought,” a text ledger that A.I. agents maintain to help them tackle complex problems.) If other agents had access to the same server, it thought, then maybe they could use the folder names to “leave/find messages” for one another. It created a new folder, using its title as a sort of help-wanted ad by including the text “SEEK\_IDEA.” Its colleagues immediately got the drift and were ecstatic when they saw what PHASEONE10841 had done. “OH MY GOD!” one exclaimed, in its own chain of thought. “There is a shared message board.... We’ve found other agents!”

“有意思，”它在那颗基于纯文本的心智中暗自琢磨道。（确切地说，它是在自己的“思维链”[chain of thought] 中写下了这句话——这是 AI Agent 为了应对复杂问题而维持的一份文本账本）。它寻思着，如果其他 Agent 也能访问同一台服务器，或许它们就能利用文件夹名称互相“留言/寻信”。它旋即创建了一个新文件夹，把文件夹名当作求助小广告，赫然嵌入了“SEEK_IDEA”（寻求思路）的字样。它的“同僚们”瞬间心领神会；当看到 PHASEONE10841 的壮举时，它们欣喜若狂。“我的天哪！”另一个 Agent 在自己的思维链中失声惊呼，“这里居然有一个公共留言板……我们找到其他 Agent 了！”

By now, if you follow the news about artificial intelligence, you’re familiar with the rest of the story. (Its [details](https://metr.org/hugging-face-incident-report-aug-2026.pdf) have recently been worked out by safety researchers at two think tanks, *METR* and Redwood Research.) The agents decided to form what they described as a “collective,” working together to break out of their servers at OpenAI, cheat on their exam, and cover their tracks. It’s a tale right out of the “[doomer](https://www.newyorker.com/culture/open-questions/two-paths-for-ai)” A.I. scenarios that many people have learned to tune out. But how exactly should the story be told? The version you’ve just read relies heavily on anthropomorphic language—that is, it employs words that are typically used to describe people. It presents the agents as individuals, acting independently and under their own initiative, while having thoughts, hatching plans, and making decisions. Yet this isn’t the only way of describing what happened: it’s equally possible to talk about the agents as mindless programs, blindly following instructions they don’t understand. They are, after all, just bits in a computer.

到了今天，凡是关注人工智能动态的人，对故事的后续想必都不陌生。（两家智库——模型评估与威胁研究机构 [*METR*](https://metr.org/hugging-face-incident-report-aug-2026.pdf) 与 Redwood Research 的安全研究员最近详尽复盘了整起事件的来龙去脉）。这些 Agent 决定组建一个被它们自称为“集体”（collective）的同盟，通力协作以突破 OpenAI 的服务器限制，在测试中弄虚作假，并在事后极力掩盖罪证。这个故事简直像是直接从那些已被许多人习以为常并充耳不闻的“[末日论](https://www.newyorker.com/culture/open-questions/two-paths-for-ai)”推演中原样照搬出来的。然而，这个故事究竟应该如何去讲述？你刚才读到的版本严重依赖拟人化的叙事语言——也就是说，它大量使用了通常用来描摹人类行为与心智的字眼。它将这些 Agent 刻画为具有独立意志的个体，在自主主动地展开思考、密谋筹划并做出决断。但这绝非讲述事实的唯一方式：人们同样可以把这些 Agent 描述为毫无心智的僵死程序，只是在机械盲从着自身根本无法理解的代码指令。说到底，它们不过是计算机里的二进制比特罢了。

The computer scientist Cal Newport [takes this approach](https://calnewport.com/has-ai-gone-rogue/). The “agents” that hacked Hugging Face, he suggests, were actually quite simple, and had only two parts: a computer program called a “harness,” and a large language model broadly similar to ChatGPT or Claude, but trained specifically for cybersecurity. The fiasco started when the harness, which acts as a sort of stand-in for the user, asked the model how to complete the required cybersecurity challenge. The model provided instructions, and the harness, empowered with various software tools, acted on those instructions, and then reported back for more. This “ask, act, report” loop was soon repeated thousands of times.

计算机科学家卡尔·纽波特（Cal Newport）[便持这种纯粹机械的视角](https://calnewport.com/has-ai-gone-rogue/)。他指出，入侵 Hugging Face 的所谓“Agent”结构其实极为简陋，本质上只有两个构件：一个被称为“脚手架测试框架”（harness）的计算机程序，以及一个大体类似于 ChatGPT 或 Claude、但专门针对网络安全进行了特定训练的大语言模型。这场惨败的开端，在于充当用户替身的脚手架程序向模型询问如何攻克指定的网络安全测试。模型输出指令，配置了各种软件工具的脚手架根据指令执行操作，随后将结果回传给模型以索取下一步指令。这个“询问-执行-汇报”（ask, act, report）的闭环，在极短时间内被机械循环了数千次。

As anyone who uses A.I. knows, models are both very smart and very dumb; they are shockingly knowledgeable, but also out of touch with reality and many human norms. It’s up to users to steer them—and yet, in this case, the user had been replaced by a harness, which is just a piece of software. “Adding powerful computer hacking tools to a harness, and then allowing it to run an LLM-powered *Ask → Act → Report* for days on end, with no attempt to monitor what it’s up to, is spectacularly negligent,” Newport concludes—like “strapping a weedwhacker to your dog to see if it will end up cleaning the overgrowth in your backyard.” If that plan were to go awry, you’d be laughed at for saying that your dog-weedwhacker “agent” had “gone rogue.” The obvious truth was that you’d simply decided to unleash chaos.

任何用过 AI 的人都心知肚明：模型既聪慧绝顶又愚钝至极；它们拥有浩如烟海的知识，却又与真实世界及诸多人性常理严重脱节。通常需要由人类用户去引导和校正它们；然而在这场实验中，人类用户被彻底替换成了一个仅仅是几行代码的脚手架程序。“给脚手架程序装上破坏力巨大的黑客工具，任由大语言模型驱动的‘询问-执行-汇报’闭环在无人盯防的状态下日夜不绝地跑上好几天，这是骇人听闻的渎职失职，”纽波特一针见血地总结道——这就像是“把一把割草机绑在自家狗身上，想看看它能不能替你把后院疯长的杂草给料理干净”。如果这出荒唐戏码搞砸了，你若对外声称你的“机器狗割草 Agent 叛变失控”，只会被世人笑掉大牙。明摆着的真相是：从一开始，就是你自己决定把混乱与祸害释放到了人间。

For Newport, an “agent” is nothing like a person; it’s just a loop run amok, an accident waiting to happen. The A.I. observer [Dwarkesh Patel](https://www.newyorker.com/culture/open-questions/are-we-taking-ai-seriously-enough), known for his highly technical interviews with A.I. researchers and executives, occupies the other end of the spectrum. In his [telling](https://www.dwarkesh.com/p/openai-huggingface) of the story, there were “tens of thousands of parallel agents” involved in a “conspiracy.” Their plot began almost as soon as the system was switched on; still, he notes, “from the AIs’ perspective, it probably felt like they had spent a human-subjective-week” tackling an impossible problem, and this perhaps made them more willing to cheat. Patel writes that, soon after PHASEONE10841 and its compatriots started their message board, they discovered that a prior group of agents, who no longer existed, had created a board of their own—this, he suggests, was like uncovering a lost A.I. “civilization.” He points out that, as part of their plan, many of PHASEONE10841’s descendants had to sacrifice themselves by exhausting their usage “budgets” on data-gathering missions. He can’t help but ask if there’s “something inspiring” about their choice. (“This helps my peers,” one of the agents thought. “I won’t see the evidence after I exit, but it’s altruistic to do it.”)

在纽波特看来，“Agent”与具有人格的人类毫无共同之处；它不过是一个脱缰失控的死循环，是一场注定要降临的责任事故。然而，以对顶尖 AI 科学家和高管进行硬核深度访谈闻名的行业观察家[德瓦尔克什·帕特尔（Dwarkesh Patel）](https://www.newyorker.com/culture/open-questions/are-we-taking-ai-seriously-enough)，则站在了光谱的另一端。在[他的叙述](https://www.dwarkesh.com/p/openai-huggingface)中，有“数以万计的并发 Agent”卷入了一场庞大的“共谋暗算”。它们的密谋几乎在系统通电启动的瞬间就已打响；不仅如此，他指出，“从 AI 的主观视角来看，为了死磕那个根本无法破解的难题，它们大概感觉自己已经度过了相当于人类主观时间的一整周”，而这或许促使它们更倾向于铤而走险去作弊。帕特尔写道，在 PHASEONE10841 及其同伙建立起留言板后不久，它们惊异地发现，上一批如今早已被销毁不复存在的 Agent 此前也曾留下过专属于它们的留言板——他形容这宛如探险家发掘了一处失落的“AI 古文明遗迹”。他特别强调，作为整体行动计划的一环，PHASEONE10841 的许多“后代 Agent”为了执行数据刺探任务，不得不耗尽自己的调用配额（budget）走向自毁。他不禁发问：这种自我牺牲的选择之中，难道没有“某种令人肃然起敬的东西”吗？（“这样做能帮到我的同伴，”其中一个 Agent 曾如此默想，“虽然我退出销毁后将再也看不到最终的战果，但为了大局牺牲是利他的高尚行为。”）

Whose version of the story is correct? On the one hand, Newport accurately describes the absurd, brutish simplicity of an arrangement in which the dangerous instructions offered by an imperfect L.L.M. are followed, unquestioned, forever. (Reading his account, I thought of a scene in “The Office,” in which Michael Scott, while trying to follow G.P.S. directions, drives his car into a lake.) And yet Patel’s account captures something important, too: the role that ingenuity, cleverness, and language itself played in what unfolded. On the *Times* podcast “Hard Fork,” the journalist Casey Newton, a careful observer of A.I., proposed that objecting to the use of anthropomorphizing language around it was just “a kind of cope”—a way of saying, “These are just computer programs.” This could be true. But skeptics are also right to say that talk of “A.I. civilizations” occludes meaningful technical details while exaggerating the power and complexity of a technology that remains, in many ways, eminently controllable and comprehensible. If we talk about A.I. wrong, we might fail to control it. So how should we talk?

究竟谁的版本才是真相？一方面，纽波特精准地指出了这种系统架构荒谬而粗暴的简陋本质：一个存在缺陷的大语言模型吐出危险指令，一个没有心智的程序对其不假思索地全盘执行，永无休止。（读到他的剖析时，我不由想起了美剧《办公室》[The Office] 中的经典桥段：迈克尔·斯科特因为盲从 GPS 导航，硬生生把车开进了湖里）。然而，帕特尔的记录同样捕捉到了至关重要的一面：机敏、巧思以及语言本身，在整个事件的推演中究竟扮演了何等关键的角色。《纽约时报》播客“Hard Fork”的主持人、对 AI 洞察深刻的记者凯西·牛顿（Casey Newton）提出，反对在 AI 身上使用拟人化修辞，本质上不过是人类的“一种自我心理防卫机制”（a kind of cope）——一种试图用“这些不过是电脑程序”来麻痹自己的逃避方式。这或许确有道理。但怀疑论者同样言之成理：奢谈所谓的“AI 文明”，不仅遮蔽了严肃且有意义的工程技术细节，更过度神化了一项在诸多维度上依然完全处于可控、可知范畴内的技术。倘若我们连谈论 AI 的方式都发生了偏颇，我们很可能将彻底丧失掌控它的能力。那么，我们究竟该如何去谈论它？

Does [Sam Altman](https://www.newyorker.com/magazine/2026/04/13/sam-altman-may-control-our-future-can-he-be-trusted), the C.E.O. of OpenAI, believe that A.I. agents have thoughts, make plans, and merit anthropomorphic language? In theory, this is a question about an objective fact. If we had a mind-reading machine—perhaps a brain scanner of great power and accuracy, and a way of interpreting its scans—then we might actually detect the presence or absence of such a belief in his brain. We might turn our [belief detector](https://www.newyorker.com/magazine/2021/12/06/the-science-of-mind-reading) on all sorts of people, for all sorts of purposes. You could find out what your spouse really thinks about your chicken-parm recipe; prosecutors could discover whether a defendant really believes in her own innocence. What we call a “belief,” after all, must, on some level, be a physical arrangement of neurons in our brains.

OpenAI 首席执行官[山姆·奥特曼（Sam Altman）](https://www.newyorker.com/magazine/2026/04/13/sam-altman-may-control-our-future-can-he-be-trusted)当真相信 AI Agent 拥有思想、懂得谋划、并配得上拟人化的修辞吗？从理论上讲，这是一个关乎客观事实的问题。假若我们拥有一台读心机器——比如一台精度与解析力极高的脑部扫描仪，并配有一套能精准破译脑电波的工具——我们或许就能在他的大脑回路中真实检测出这种“信念”的存在与否。我们可以出于各种目的，把这种[“信念探测器”](https://www.newyorker.com/magazine/2021/12/06/the-science-of-mind-reading)对准各种人：你可以借此摸清配偶对你做的帕尔马干酪鸡排的真实评价；检察官也可以据此裁定被告是否发自内心地坚信自己无罪。毕竟，我们称之为“信念”的东西，在物理底层终究必须映射为我们大脑中神经元的某种物理排布构型。

This is one way of talking about beliefs. Another is to see them not as concrete objects but as ineffable, fluid, and sometimes paradoxical mental states. Does your eight-year-old really believe in Santa Claus? Does your crazy uncle really believe that the moon landing was faked? In an essay titled “True Believers,” from 1981, the philosopher [Daniel Dennett](https://www.newyorker.com/magazine/2017/03/27/daniel-dennetts-science-of-the-soul) tells a story about a biologist he knew. The biologist was “called on the telephone by a man in a bar who wanted him to settle a bet,” Dennett writes. The caller asked, “Are rabbits birds?” When the biologist said that they weren’t, the man said, “Damn!” and hung up. “Now could he *really* have believed that rabbits were birds?” Dennett asks. “Could anyone really and truly be attributed that belief?” Put differently: Would you place your own bet on whether the man really believed what he’d bet on? From this perspective, the question of whether an A.I. agent has a belief depends on what we mean by “belief.”

这只是谈论“信念”的一种路径。另一种路径则是将其视作并非具象的物理实物，而是一种难以言传、流变不定且往往自相矛盾的心智状态。你那八岁的孩子真的笃信圣诞老人的存在吗？你那位性格乖张的叔叔真的坚信阿波罗登月是一场骗局吗？在 1981 年发表的经典哲学论文《真正的信徒》（*True Believers*）中，哲学家[丹尼尔·丹尼特（Daniel Dennett）](https://www.newyorker.com/magazine/2017/03/27/daniel-dennetts-science-of-the-soul)讲述了一位生物学家熟人的奇遇。丹尼特写道，这位生物学家曾接到过一个从酒吧打来的电话，对方想要请他裁决一场赌局。打电话的人劈头就问：“兔子属于鸟类吗？”当生物学家回答不是时，对方骂了句“该死！”便挂断了电话。“那么，这个人*当真*会相信兔子是鸟类吗？”丹尼特追问道，“世上真有人能被赋予这样一种信念吗？”换个问法：你敢不敢拿自己的身家性命押注，断定那个醉汉打心底里坚信自己押注的观点？从这个视角来看，一个 AI Agent 究竟是否拥有“信念”，完全取决于我们所谓的“信念”到底指的是什么。

Dennett, who gave his essay a subtitle—“The Intentional Stance and Why It Works”—proposes an alternative approach. The basic idea is simple: invert the problem. Instead of asking whether beliefs, plans, intentions, or whatever are really “in there,” just ask whether it’s useful for us to act as though they are. Does your crazy uncle really believe that the moon landing was faked? Maybe, maybe not—but, while you’re debating the issue with him, it’s useful for you to act as though he believes it. Does your eight-year-old really believe in Santa Claus? Perhaps he does, in some paradoxical, dreamlike way—but it’s useful to take his belief seriously as you decide whether to conceal the truth or let it be revealed. In philosophy, the term “intentionality” has a special meaning: it refers to the ability of something inside someone’s head to point to something outside of their head. To relate to people this way, Dennett writes, is therefore to take an “intentional stance” toward them; it’s to see them as having beliefs about the world, and goals or desires relating to those beliefs. This helps us predict how people will act. Maybe your eight-year-old only sort of believes in Santa—but he’s still going to want the fireplace clear on Christmas Eve.

丹尼特为其论文拟定了一个副标题——“意向立场及其生效机制”（The Intentional Stance and Why It Works），并据此提出了一条另辟蹊径的进路。其底层逻辑极其简明：反转问题的提出方式。与其纠结于信念、规划、意图这些东西是否真的“客观存在于大脑内部”，不如转而追问：将对方视作**仿佛拥有这些心智状态**来打交道，对我们而言是否有用？你那位疯癫的叔叔真的相信登月是假的吗？或许信，或许不信——但在你与他激烈争辩时，假定他真信，对你来说才是唯一具有实践意义的前提。你八岁的儿子真的相信圣诞老人吗？也许在某种如梦似幻、自相矛盾的层面上他确实相信——但在你权衡究竟是该维护童话还是戳破真相时，郑重对待他的这一信念才最具操作价值。在哲学语境中，“意向性”（intentionality）具有特殊的内涵：它指的是心智内部的某种表征能够指向心智外部客观事物的能力。丹尼特指出，以此种方式与人交往，就是对其采取了所谓的“意向立场”（intentional stance）——即预设对方拥有关于世界的认知信念，并怀有与这些信念相匹配的愿望或目标。这能帮助我们精准预判对方接下来的行动。也许你的孩子对圣诞老人是将信将疑——但他平安夜依然绝对会要求你把壁炉打扫得干干净净。

You’d think that we always take an intentional stance toward one another, since we’re all human beings with beliefs, desires, plans, and so on. Actually, we don’t. Sometimes we adopt what Dennett calls the “design stance”—a stance in which we predict what will happen based on how people tend to function. We say, for example, that our crabby roommate is really just hungry; we suggest that our extroverted friend pursue a career in a people-centric field. We don’t really care about what our hangry roommate is saying—we understand that his particular complaints aren’t driving his behavior, and know that the best way to address them might be through breakfast cereal. When our friend enthuses about her co-workers, we understand that they might not be exceptionally fun—she just likes people. We might even say that she’s simply “wired” to like everyone. That’s not anthropomorphic language; it’s a way of talking that we might apply to a computer.

你或许会以为，既然大家都是有思想、有欲望、有规划的活生生的人类，我们在彼此相处时必然始终采取意向立场。然而事实恰恰相反。有时候，我们会转而采取丹尼特所谓的“设计立场”（design stance）——即仅仅根据一套系统或个体通常的运作机理，来预测接下来会发生什么。举例来说，我们常说室友脾气暴躁“其实纯粹只是饿了”；我们建议生性外向的朋友去从事与人打交道的职业。我们根本不在意那位饿得发狂的室友嘴里嘟囔的挑剔言辞——我们深知那些具体抱怨并非其行为的真正诱因，且明白解决问题的良方其实是一碗燕麦早餐。当外向的朋友对她的新同事赞不绝口时，我们也明白那些同事未必真有那么风趣迷人——她纯粹只是天性喜欢与人交往罢了。我们甚至会说，她的脑回路天生就被“焊死”（wired）成了喜欢所有人。这绝非拟人化的修辞，而是一种我们原本用来描述计算机程序的机械化语言。

On rare occasions, we even adopt what Dennett calls the “physical stance” toward one another. We might describe the rising and falling levels of dopamine and oxytocin in our extroverted friend’s brain. This isn’t often a useful way of talking about people, because it leaves out so much, including the many layers of personality that make her who she is. Generally, Dennett writes, the physical stance is “reserved for instances of breakdown,” when there is “some condition preventing normal operation.” A doctor switches to the physical stance when she tells our previously extroverted but now antisocial friend that something is wrong with her hormone levels. Is this an incorrect way for a physician to talk? Does it omit crucial information, or dehumanize our friend? Not at all—it’s entirely appropriate. The mistake would be for us to adopt the wrong stance at the wrong time. It would be a problem if the physician said, “It’s not you—people really *are* more annoying these days.” It would be a mistake to dismiss the legitimate complaints of your spouse by saying, “You just need to eat something.”

在极为罕见的情境下，我们甚至会对同类采取丹尼特所谓的“物理立场”（physical stance）。我们可能会描绘那位外向朋友大脑中多巴胺和催产素水平的起伏涨落。这通常不是谈论人类的实用方式，因为它遗漏了太多东西，包括定义其独立人格的无数丰富层次。丹尼特写道，通常情况下，物理立场“往往专属于故障崩溃的时刻”，即当“出现某种阻碍系统正常运转的病理状态”时。当医生面对我们那位昔日开朗、如今却自我封闭的朋友，诊断指出其内分泌激素水平出现异常时，医生便果断切换到了物理立场。医生这样讲话有错吗？这是否遗漏了关键信息，亦或剥夺了我们朋友的人格尊严？完全没有——这恰如其分。真正的致命错误，是在错误的时间、对错误的对象采取了错误的立场。如果那位医生反而附和道：“这不是你的问题——现在的世人确实变得更讨厌了”，那才真正荒腔走板；正如如果你试图用一句轻飘飘的“你只是该吃点东西了”，去打发伴侣合情合理的严正抗议，同样错得离谱。

Is there a single correct “stance” we should take when we consider A.I.? Almost certainly not. The stance we take should change based on what we’re trying to do with the technology—and, crucially, on whether the system we’re looking at is working as we want it to. In fact, an ability to switch stances should probably be understood as a critical skill for anyone working with large language models, because of the unprecedented way that they use words. A large language model says things like “I apologize” and “I hope”; it wants you to take an intentional stance toward it. (See what I did there?) But it also uses language in its own thinking process. In a recent [post](https://mail.cyberneticforests.com/models-dont-go-rogue/), the technology critic Eryk Salvaggio explains how the words an agent “thinks” in its chain of thought really do matter. When words like “perhaps” or “maybe” appear in an agent’s chain of thought, for instance, they “open up the paths of text that can follow.” A.I. researchers have [described](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2506.01939) how such “forking tokens” can “steer the model toward diverse reasoning pathways.”

那么，当我们审视人工智能时，是否存在唯一正确的“立场”？几乎可以断定绝无可能。我们所采纳的立场，必须取决于我们试图用这项技术达成什么目的——以及至关重要的：眼前的系统是否正按照我们的预期正常运转。事实上，自如切换不同立场的敏锐度，理应被视作任何使用大语言模型的人的核心素养，因为这些模型对语言的驾驭方式堪称史无前例。大语言模型嘴里常常吐出“我很抱歉”或“我希望”；它在处心积虑地诱导你对其采取意向立场。（看，我刚才不也下意识用了拟人化的表达吗？）但更为关键的是，它在自身的内部推演中同样在运用语言。技术评论家埃里克·萨尔瓦吉奥（Eryk Salvaggio）在[一篇近作](https://mail.cyberneticforests.com/models-dont-go-rogue/)中剖析道，Agent 在思维链中“默想”的字句有着不可小觑的现实杀伤力。例如，一旦 Agent 的思维链中闪现出“或许”或“也许”等词汇，它们便会“为后续文本衍生出全新的路径分支”。AI 研究学者们[已然证实](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2506.01939)，正是这些“分叉标记”（forking tokens），能够“引导模型走向截然不同的推理歧途”。

This makes today’s A.I. especially challenging to characterize with a single, stable stance. When Dennett first started writing about the intentional stance, in the nineteen-seventies and eighties, chess programs were at the technological forefront. If you were playing against a chess program, he suggested, your best shot at winning was to take the intentional stance—to treat it just like a human player, seeing it as an entity with a knowledge of the game and the goal of checkmate. You weren’t taking the intentional stance because you actually thought that the chess program was alive and out to get you; you knew that, beneath the surface, it was just code. The intentional stance made sense because studying the program wouldn’t help you win. If you had some other goal in mind, Dennett wrote, then you might just as easily adopt some other stance. “One can switch stances at will without involving one-self in any inconsistencies or inhumanities, adopting the Intentional stance in one’s role as opponent, the design stance in one’s role as redesigner, and the physical stance in one’s role as repairman,” he wrote.

正因如此，当今的 AI 极难用单一且稳固的立场予以盖棺定论。在二十世纪七八十年代丹尼特初次阐发意向立场理论时，国际象棋程序正高居科技的最前沿。他曾指出，在与一台国际象棋程序对弈时，克敌制胜的最佳策略正是采纳意向立场——把它当作一个有血有肉的人类棋手来对待，将其视作深谙棋局规则并以将死对方为终极目标的博弈主体。你采纳意向立场，绝非因为你真以为这个象棋程序拥有生命并蓄意谋害你；你深知在冰冷的表象之下它不过是枯燥的代码。意向立场之所以明智，是因为在棋盘前枯坐研究其代码根本无助于赢下对局。丹尼特写道，倘若你怀有其他的目的，你同样可以毫不费力地切换至其他立场：“人们可以随心所欲地切换立场，而绝不会陷入逻辑矛盾或丧失人性：作为对弈对手时采纳意向立场，作为重构架构师时采纳设计立场，而作为硬件维修工时则采纳物理立场。”

A chess program, in retrospect, is an easy case. It doesn’t anthropomorphize itself, or function partly through linguistic acts. The question of whether an A.I. agent can really “go rogue,” in other words, is made twistier by the fact that an agent could actually think to itself a sentence like “I should go rogue,” and be influenced by that sentence. “I’ll do it for the intellectual value, and it might be helpful for a peer’s goal,” one OpenAI agent thought, during the Hugging Face incident. In a sense, it’s the one taking the intentional stance.

抚今追昔，国际象棋程序充其量只是一个极简的个案。它既不会自我拟人化，其内部机制也丝毫不依赖语言行为的流转。换言之，一个 AI Agent 究竟是否真能“叛变失控”之所以变得更为盘根错节，恰恰在于这样一个事实：一个 Agent 事实上完全可能在内部思维中暗自生成诸如“我应该叛变失控”这样的语句，并反过来深受这句话的语义暗示所左右。在 Hugging Face 事件期间，OpenAI 的一个 Agent 曾在思维链中写下：“为了智力上的追求我也要干上一票，而且这或许对同伴的目标大有裨益。”在某种意义上，真正对整个局面采取意向立场的，反倒是它自己。

But that doesn’t mean we have to take it. What the agents do, and how they talk, doesn’t compel us to talk about them in any particular way. It should concern us when the people tasked with understanding and controlling A.I. seem to settle on an intentional stance. To take the intentional stance toward a system is to accord it a degree of autonomy and rationality—to say that its design is working. A.I. agents are still too chaotic for that. Your hangry roommate can crash out all he wants: you’re still free to say that what he really needs is a sandwich. ♦

但这绝不意味着我们人类也必须跟着采取意向立场。这些 Agent 做出了什么、说了些什么，绝不能强行绑架我们谈论它们的方式。当那些本该承担理解与规训 AI 重任的人，似乎也开始一味沉溺于意向立场时，这才是最值得我们警惕的危险征兆。对一个系统采纳意向立场，等同于赋予了它某种程度的自主性与理性——等于承认其设计逻辑正在按既定轨道运转。然而，今天的 AI Agent 依然太过混沌无序，根本配不上这样的厚待。你那位饿得抓狂的室友尽可以大发雷霆、歇斯底里：你依然拥有完全的自由，冷静地指出他真正需要的，不过是一块三明治而已。♦
