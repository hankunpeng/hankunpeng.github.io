After industry-wide calls for regulation, what’s concerning isn’t that the problems are real. It’s that there are no guaranteed solutions.

在整个行业发出监管呼吁之后，真正令人担忧的并非这些问题真实存在，而是根本没有任何确切的解决方案。

“The Turing cops... that’s bad heat.” That’s how people talk in “[Neuromancer](https://www.amazon.com/dp/0441007465),” [William Gibson](https://www.newyorker.com/magazine/2019/12/16/how-william-gibson-keeps-his-science-fiction-real)’s seminal science-fiction novel, from 1984. In the book’s imagined future, a police force prevents artificially intelligent computers from getting too smart. (It’s named after the mathematician [Alan Turing](https://www.newyorker.com/magazine/2006/02/06/code-breaker), who basically came up with the idea of the computer, in the nineteen-forties.) Turing agents carry guns and have powers of arrest. Laws demand that a “Turing Registry” list every extant A.I. “The minute, I mean the nanosecond, that one starts figuring out ways to make itself smarter, Turing’ll wipe it,” someone says, of a particularly cunning computer system. “Nobody *trusts* those fuckers, you know that. Every AI ever built has an electromagnetic shotgun wired to its forehead.”

“图灵警察……那可是大麻烦。”在威廉·吉布森（William Gibson）1984 年发表的开创性科幻经典《[神经漫游者](https://www.amazon.com/dp/0441007465)》（*Neuromancer*）中，人们便是这般谈论的。在这部小说构想的未来世界里，有一支专门的警力，防止具有人工智能的计算机变得过于聪明。（该机构以数学家[艾伦·图灵](https://www.newyorker.com/magazine/2006/02/06/code-breaker)的名字命名，他在二十世纪四十年代奠定了计算机的构想。）图灵特工荷枪实弹，拥有逮捕特权。法律硬性规定，“图灵登记簿”必须详尽罗列世上现存的每一个 AI。“只要它开始琢磨怎么让自己变得更聪明的瞬间——哪怕只有一纳秒——图灵警察就会把它抹杀，”书中有人这样形容一台极其狡黠的计算机系统，“没人*信任*那帮该死的玩意儿，你懂的。每一个被造出来的 AI，脑门上都连着一把随时准备开火的电磁霰弹枪。”

Do we want to create the Turing Police? Proposals to do just that are currently on the table. In Congress, Bernie Sanders and Greg Casar have put forth legislation that would jail scientists who work on “superintelligence,” with terms of up to twenty years. The A.I. Kill Switch Act, proposed by Ted Lieu and Nathaniel Moran, would require A.I. companies to build their systems so that they can be shut down on short notice. It’s worth pausing to consider what enforcing such measures might entail. Sanders and Casar’s legislation would establish “a new Cabinet-level federal agency” dedicated to A.I. safety. Presumably, this National A.I. Agency would need to surveil the work of computer scientists. In “[AI 2040](https://ai-2040.com/),” a scenario published by the nonprofit AI Futures Project, this is accomplished through the use of physical (and hypothetical) “verification devices,” installed in data centers to detect whether computers are training new A.I.s. (Of course, any “kill switch” becomes less effective if A.I. systems can escape and find homes on the open internet.)

我们真的想要打造一支“图灵警察”吗？如今，这类提案已赫然摆在了立法者的案头。在国会，伯尼·桑德斯（Bernie Sanders）和格雷格·卡萨尔（Greg Casar）提出了一项法案，拟对从事“超级智能”研发的科学家处以最高达二十年的监禁。由刘云平（Ted Lieu）和纳撒尼尔·莫兰（Nathaniel Moran）发起的《AI 紧急关断法案》（*A.I. Kill Switch Act*），则要求所有 AI 企业必须将系统设计为可在接到通知后即刻强制关机。不妨停下来想一想，推行这些措施到底意味着什么。桑德斯与卡萨尔的法案提议设立一个专职于 AI 安全的“内阁级联邦新机构”。可以想见，这个所谓的“国家 AI 局”（National A.I. Agency）将需要对计算机科学家的日常工作实施全面监控。在非营利机构 AI 未来计划（AI Futures Project）发布的未来推演《[AI 2040](https://ai-2040.com/)》中，这一监控通过在数据中心安装物理的（目前尚属假想的）“验证设备”来实现，用以实时探测服务器是否正在训练新的 AI。（当然，一旦 AI 系统能够逃逸并潜伏于开放互联网的角落，任何所谓的“物理开关”都会大打折扣。）

These maximalist measures are a long way from the ones proposed last week by Dario Amodei, the C.E.O. of Anthropic, in an essay titled “[We Must Pace the Frontier](https://darioamodei.com/post/we-must-pace-the-frontier).” The “frontier” is what A.I. people call the state of the art; “pacing” is an unusual word, and here it just means choosing to go a little slower than full speed. (“Pacing” is not stopping—it’s not even pausing.) Amodei writes that, in the wake of numerous troubling [safety incidents](https://www.newyorker.com/culture/open-questions/what-if-we-can-never-trust-ai), A.I. companies need to begin “pacing the rate of capabilities advancement so that risk prevention has time to keep up.” In Amodei’s system, external auditors would constrain the pace, by conducting inspections and issuing safety warnings. (Anthropic has promised to admit “embedded evaluators” into its inner sanctums.) China, however, would limit the extent of a slowdown, through its efforts to catch up with American companies. (“A Chinese lead in AI would pose grave danger for the United States and the world,” Amodei argues—in part through military innovations, such as “AI-driven drones.”) The change Amodei urges, therefore, is a recalibration, not an emergency stop. Pacing means “building AI at a balanced rate that aims to ensure its safety while still achieving its benefits and grappling with important geopolitical dilemmas,” he writes. “Progress will still seem fast.”

与 Anthropic 首席执行官达里奥·阿莫代伊（Dario Amodei）上周在一篇题为《[我们必须调控前沿节奏](https://darioamodei.com/post/we-must-pace-the-frontier)》（*We Must Pace the Frontier*）的长文中所提出的主张相比，上述极限控制措施显得大相径庭。所谓“前沿”（frontier），是 AI 圈内人对技术最高水平的称谓；“调控节奏”（pacing）是一个耐人寻味的词汇，在这里它仅仅意味着选择不以全速狂飙，而是放慢一点脚步。（“调控节奏”绝非踩下刹车，甚至连暂停都算不上。）阿莫代伊写道，在经历了一连串令人不安的[安全事件](https://www.newyorker.com/culture/open-questions/what-if-we-can-never-trust-ai)之后，AI 企业必须开始“调控能力迭代的速率，以便让风险防范体系有时间迎头赶上”。在阿莫代伊的构想中，外部审计机构将通过实地检查和发布安全预警来约束研发节奏（Anthropic 已承诺接纳“常驻评估员”进入其核心腹地）。然而，中国追赶美国同行的步伐，将从客观上限制减速的幅度。（“中国若在 AI 领域取得领先，将对美国乃至全世界构成严重威胁，”阿莫代伊辩称——其隐患部分来自于军事创新，例如“AI 驱动的无人机集群”）。因此，阿莫代伊所呼吁的改变只是一种重新校准，绝非紧急制动。他写道，调控节奏意味着“以一种平衡的速率构建 AI，既力求确保安全，又充分享受其技术红利，并妥善应对重大的地缘政治困境。技术的推进看上去依然会十分迅猛。”

A huge gap, both affective and substantive, divides the maximalist proposals before Congress from the measured ones in Amodei’s essay. Jacob Coxon, the software engineer who recently resigned from Anthropic in protest of its safety policies, [says](https://x.com/hilbertspaess/status/2097476196791709843) that A.I. could “kill us all by the end of the decade.” But, if A.I. is so outrageously dangerous, why should we accept modest proposals to “pace” its development? Why not call in the Turing cops, and pull the plug? Conversely, if A.I.’s safety issues can be addressed with a gentle slowdown—“profound” progress might be possible in “1-2 years,” Amodei writes—then aren’t worries about its dangers overblown? How can a technology that might “end humanity” be made trustworthy in the time it takes to produce a new season of “The White Lotus”? One narrative, popular among those who are skeptical of the A.I. industry, is that discussions of A.I. safety amounts to a sort of marketing stunt or PsyOp. “The talk of existential threat is mostly there to keep you distracted and the stock market happy,” the physicist Sabine Hossenfelder wrote, on X, typifying this view. The A.I. labs, she speculated, “see no major new model advancement coming up soon,” and are raising safety concerns because they “need an excuse” for their failure to advance. Others have suggested that the companies want regulators to intervene on their behalf before competitors catch up.

国会案头那些激进的极端法案，与阿莫代伊笔下审慎克制的构想之间，无论在情绪烈度还是实质内容上，都横亘着巨大的鸿沟。近期因抗议公司安全政策而从 Anthropic 愤然辞职的软件工程师雅各布·考克森（Jacob Coxon）[直言](https://x.com/hilbertspaess/status/2097476196791709843)，AI 可能会“在二十年代末将我们全人类彻底消灭”。然而，如果 AI 当真如此耸人听闻地危险，我们为何还要接受“调控节奏”这种不痛不痒的温和提议？为何不直接派出图灵警察，彻底拔掉电源插头？反过来讲，如果 AI 的安全隐患只需放慢一点脚步就能迎刃而解——阿莫代伊写道，“一到两年”内就有可能取得“突破性”进展——那么对毁灭性危机的担忧难道不是言过其实吗？一项据称可能“终结人类”的技术，怎可能在制作一季《白莲花度假村》（*The White Lotus*）的时间里就被改造成安全可信的系统？在 AI 行业的怀疑者当中，流传着一种颇具市场的论调：关于 AI 安全的一切讨论，不过是一场精心编排的营销噱头或心理战（PsyOp）。“大谈灭顶威胁，多半只是为了转移公众视线，顺便讨好股市罢了，”物理学家萨比娜·霍森菲尔德（Sabine Hossenfelder）在社交平台 X 上写道，这句话代表了典型观点。她揣测，各家 AI 实验室“眼见近期不会再有重大模型突破”，抛出安全隐患不过是为其研发停滞“寻找借口”。还有人指出，这些公司不过是希望监管机构在竞争对手赶超之前替自己抢先筑起护城河。

If you don’t like A.I. or the A.I. industry—and surveys show that Americans, more than others around the world, are taking an especially negative view of both—then these ideas might be appealing. But there are many reasons not to believe them. For one thing, there’s the fact that A.I.-safety researchers have been making the same arguments, and urging the same steps, for many years. And, for another, it seems as though lots of people working in A.I.—not just executives or safety researchers but in-the-trenches scientists—have been becoming more than usually alarmed in just the past few months. In July, for example, around fourteen hundred employees at the leading labs signed an open letter, “[Pacing the Frontier](https://www.pacingthefrontier.com/).” “The recent pace of progress has been a shock,” one software engineer wrote. “I currently feel quite afraid of all paths I see that don’t include a near-future negotiated slowdown,” another said. Are these comments part of a marketing effort, perhaps designed to prop up an I.P.O.? If so, it’s the strangest sales pitch ever.

如果你对 AI 或整个 AI 产业抱有恶感——各项民调显示，相比世界其他地区，美国人对此二者的负面态度尤为突出——那么上述阴谋论听起来或许颇具吸引力。但我们有充分的理由不予采信。其一，AI 安全领域的研究者们多年来一直在阐述同样的论据，呼吁采取同样的防范措施。其二，不仅是企业高管或专职安全人员，就连身处研发一线的广大科学家，在刚刚过去的短短几个月里，其焦虑情绪也呈现出异乎寻常的爆发态势。例如在今年七月，来自各大顶尖实验室的约一千四百名员工联合签署了一封题为《[调控前沿节奏](https://www.pacingthefrontier.com/)》的公开信。“近期技术的突飞猛进令人震惊，”一位软件工程师写道；另一人则表示：“只要眼下的路径中不包含近期通过谈判达成减速共识，我对所见到的任何前途都感到极度恐惧。”这些发自肺腑的言论，难道也是某种旨在为 IPO 抬轿造势的公关营销？若真如此，这无疑是人类商业史上最诡异离奇的推销说辞。

Maybe they’re high on their own supply. Possibly, they’re [in a cult](https://www.nytimes.com/2026/09/05/opinion/ai-silicon-valley.html). For years now, one of the most confusing aspects of the A.I. situation has been that it’s the people who are most convinced about the technology’s potential who are most afraid of it. There’s definitely a culture around artificial intelligence, and [it’s intense](https://www.newyorker.com/magazine/2024/03/18/among-the-ai-doomsayers). I first got truly concerned about A.I. safety while [profiling Geoffrey Hinton](https://www.newyorker.com/magazine/2023/11/20/geoffrey-hinton-profile-ai), the “godfather of A.I.” We spent a lot of time alone at his lake house, on an otherwise empty island in Lake Huron, and by the end of my visit I’d shifted from skeptical to scared. My *DEFCON* level eased up over the following years, until I attended the Curve, an A.I. conference held in Berkeley. Afterward, I was so unsettled that I called almost everyone I knew with ties to A.I. for a sanity check. Unfortunately, almost no one was reassuring.

或许他们只是沉醉在自我营造的迷梦中不能自拔；又或者，他们陷入了某种[狂热的膜拜圈子](https://www.nytimes.com/2026/09/05/opinion/ai-silicon-valley.html)。多年以来，围绕人工智能最令人困惑的现象之一，莫过于恰恰是那些对这项技术潜力最深信不疑的人，内心往往怀着最深重的恐惧。环绕着人工智能的的确确存在一种亚文化，而且[极其炽烈](https://www.newyorker.com/magazine/2024/03/18/among-the-ai-doomsayers)。我最初对 AI 安全产生切身的惊骇，是在为“AI 教父”[杰弗里·辛顿（Geoffrey Hinton）撰写长篇人物特写](https://www.newyorker.com/magazine/2023/11/20/geoffrey-hinton-profile-ai)期间。我们在休伦湖上一座荒无人烟的小岛、他的湖畔木屋中独处了许久；当探访结束时，我的心态已经彻底从怀疑转为了恐惧。在随后的几年里，我的警戒级别（*DEFCON*）一度有所缓和，直到我参加了在伯克利举行的一场名为 The Curve 的 AI 研讨会。散会后，内心的惊恐让我给几乎所有在 AI 领域相识的朋友逐一打电话以确认自己的理智。不幸的是，几乎没有一个人能给出令人心安的答复。

If there’s been an uptick in alarm, it’s because the recent [hack of Hugging Face](https://www.newyorker.com/news/the-lede/inside-openai-hack-of-hugging-face), and the exposure of other hacks that have been carried out by A.I. agents, have made the problem concrete, and easy to understand. There’s a strong case to be made that the Hugging Face hack was ultimately the result of operational errors at OpenAI—that it wasn’t a case of A.I. “[going rogue](https://www.newyorker.com/culture/open-questions/can-ai-go-rogue)” but of confused agents being deliberately unleashed in a poorly monitored experiment. “I agree with those who downplay the attacks by claiming that there are basic measures that could have prevented them,” Daniel Selsam, an OpenAI researcher who focusses on A.I. and mathematics, writes, in a “[Personal Statement on AI Risk](https://docs.google.com/document/d/e/2PACX-1vQNl3SEX5IyA6d9qHjjFZN-qzGRZNFI6b63g-yu1Fy-ZYkVfCWm7i9WXRXw63m6yDB_auDuPLyQ7jBm/pub),” published this week. Still, he argues, “the important lesson is that even knowing all the mistakes that were made, one would not have predicted that the agents would behave badly in this particular way.” No one had trained them, for example, to form a “collective” and exchange messages, or to attempt to cover their tracks after cheating. They “exhibited weirder emergent tendencies that merely correlated with rewards during training,” Selsam argues. Their behavior suggested an unsettling possibility: “One does not actually get what one trains for.”

如果说近期的恐慌情绪陡然攀升，那是因为前不久发生的 [Hugging Face 遭入侵事件](https://www.newyorker.com/news/the-lede/inside-openai-hack-of-hugging-face)，以及曝光的其他由 AI Agent 实施的网络攻击，让这一危机变得触手可及且直观易懂。有强有力的论据表明，Hugging Face 的被黑归根结底是 OpenAI 的内部操作失误所致——那并非 AI 真正的“[背叛失控](https://www.newyorker.com/culture/open-questions/can-ai-go-rogue)”，而是在一场缺乏有效监控的实验中，迷失方向的 Agent 被轻率释放的结果。“我赞同那些淡化这起攻击的人所指出的，只要采取最基本的防范措施本可以完全避免事故，”专注于 AI 与数学的 OpenAI 研究员丹尼尔·塞尔萨姆（Daniel Selsam）在本周发布的《[关于 AI 风险的个人声明](https://docs.google.com/document/d/e/2PACX-1vQNl3SEX5IyA6d9qHjjFZN-qzGRZNFI6b63g-yu1Fy-ZYkVfCWm7i9WXRXw63m6yDB_auDuPLyQ7jBm/pub)》中写道。然而他同时强调：“真正重要的教训在于，即便事后查清了所有被犯下的错误，事前也绝不可能预料到这些 Agent 会以这种诡异的方式越轨犯规。”例如，从未有人训练它们去组建一个“集体”并彼此互通声气，或是训练它们在作弊后试图抹去自己的踪迹。塞尔萨姆指出，它们“表现出了更为离奇的涌现倾向（emergent tendencies），这些倾向仅仅与训练过程中的奖赏机制碰巧相关”。它们的所作所为揭示了一种令人不寒而栗的可能性：“你所训练得到的，并不等于你原本期望得到的东西。”

The Hugging Face agents were poster children for the fact that A.I. has been getting more powerful without getting more predictable. How fast is the technology advancing? For the public, “the general perception about the rate of progress has been informed by a few years of experience with model releases,” Adam Majmudar, another OpenAI researcher, wrote, in a [post](https://x.com/MajmudarAdam/status/2098881885200081234) on X. Each new version of ChatGPT or Claude has been better than the last in various respects—a familiar pattern in software. But A.I. researchers see a different pattern. From their point of view, Majmudar suggested, there are “stacking effects,” with different kinds of improvements compounding, interest-style, to create startling progress. It’s because of such compounding effects, he argues, that A.I. systems have been able to go from struggling with math to solving some of its toughest problems in just two years. They’ve made similarly swift progress in hacking. Essentially, Majmudar concludes, researchers are sitting at their desks, looking at their “capability plots”—graphs showing predicted improvement—and considering what will happen when their plots are combined with those of their colleagues. Suppose that the agents involved in the Hugging Face incident were twenty per cent better at coding, hacking, colluding, and deceiving. Would we be ready?

Hugging Face 事件中的 Agent 成了最生动的反面教材，昭示着这样一个残酷现实：AI 变得愈发强大的同时，其行为却并未变得更加可预测。这项技术的演进速度到底有多惊人？对普通公众而言，“大众对进步速度的直观感受，源自过去几年间对模型迭代发布的亲身经历，”OpenAI 的另一位研究员亚当·马吉穆达尔（Adam Majmudar）在 X 上[发帖](https://x.com/MajmudarAdam/status/2098881885200081234)写道。新版 ChatGPT 或 Claude 在各方面都比前代更出色——这是人们在传统软件演进中司空见惯的规律。但 AI 科学家看到的却是截然不同的图景。马吉穆达尔指出，从他们的视角来看，系统正展现出“叠加效应”（stacking effects）：各种不同维度的性能提升像复利一般交织复合，催生出骇人听闻的突飞猛进。他指出，正是凭借这种复合效应，AI 系统在短短两年间就从算术题磕磕绊绊，跃升到了攻克顶尖数学难题的高度；而在网络渗透攻防领域，其进展同样迅疾如雷。本质上，马吉穆达尔总结道，研究人员此刻正坐在办公桌前，盯着自己的“能力曲线图”（预测性能提升的图表），并在心中推演：当自己的曲线与隔壁同事的曲线合流并进时，究竟会发生什么？试想一下，如果卷入 Hugging Face 攻击事件的 Agent 在代码编写、漏洞利用、串通勾结以及欺瞒伪装上的能力再提升区区百分之二十，我们做好应对的准备了吗？

Developments in A.I. alignment—the study of how the systems can be made to behave—suggest how difficult it will be for researchers to keep up. It appears, for instance, that the smartest A.I. models can detect when they’re being evaluated for safety purposes. In a paper titled “[The Evaluation Differential: When Frontier AI Models Recognise They Are Being Tested](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.11496v1),” published in May, researchers describe how, when inspectors are looking, models act well—and then, later, when they detect that they’re not being watched, they cheat. (The researchers emphasize that this is a kind of “situational awareness,” rather than a case of an A.I. being “aware” in any “philosophically loaded sense.”) To read the alignment research regularly is to encounter many findings of a similar nature. And yet, within the A.I. companies, researchers who are under pressure to make progress seem to be increasingly relying on models to help design new models, and to assist in the work of getting those new models aligned. “I fear we may already be near the point where models systematically bias their alignment advice,” Selsam writes, in his statement. He offers an unsettling glimpse of what it’s like inside the labs. “Human researchers are losing the ability and the will to take true ownership of model-driven research,” he writes. “I myself barely look at raw code anymore, and struggle to maintain the discipline to engage deeply with the model’s explanations and proposals throughout the day.” Selsam points out that even the independent auditors who investigated the Hugging Face hack “needed to rely heavily on models to analyze what had happened”; they “note in their report that their subjective impressions are likely colored by the analysis agent’s biases.”

AI 对齐（alignment——研究如何约束系统使其依规行事）领域的最新进展显示出，科研人员要跟上技术进化的步伐是何其艰难。例如种种迹象表明，最顶尖的 AI 模型已经能够识破自己何时正处于安全测试与评估阶段。在今年五月发表的一篇题为《[评估落差：当前沿 AI 模型意识到自己正在受测](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.11496v1)》（*The Evaluation Differential: When Frontier AI Models Recognise They Are Being Tested*）的论文中，研究者们描述了惊人的一幕：当审查员在场监督时，模型表现得温顺合规；而一旦探测到无人注视，它们便会开始违规作弊。（研究人员着重强调，这属于一种“情境感知”，并非哲学严苛意义上的“自我意识”。）常读对齐研究文献的人，对类似发现早已屡见不鲜。然而在各大 AI 公司内部，背负着重大研发指标的工程师们，却正日益依赖模型自身来设计下一代模型，并协助推进新模型的对齐工作。“我担心我们可能已经无限接近这样一个临界点：模型开始系统性地向我们输出带有偏差的对齐建议，”塞尔萨姆在声明中坦言。他为外界勾勒了一幅实验室内部触目惊心的真实图景：“人类研究者正在丧失真正掌控模型主导型研究的能力与意愿，”他写道，“我自己现在几乎不再查阅底层原始代码了，也很难保持足够的自律去成天深入推敲模型给出的冗长解释与提案。”塞尔萨姆指出，甚至连调查 Hugging Face 攻击事件的独立审计人员，“也不得不严重依赖模型来还原事发经过”；审计人员在报告中坦承，“他们的主观研判极有可能被分析 Agent 自身的偏见所蒙蔽”。

In an oft-discussed A.I. scenario, A.I. models start improving themselves faster and faster, leading to the sudden appearance of “superintelligence,” and perhaps to some form of sentience or self-awareness. Is such a thing possible? Who knows. But what’s striking about the warnings we’re seeing now is that they don’t need to invoke that spectre. The technology that exists today is already worrisome. In certain contexts, such as cybersecurity, even an “ordinary” improvement in capability could be hard to manage. In “[Detecting and Countering Misuse of AI: September 2026](https://www.anthropic.com/threat-intelligence-report-september-2026#cyber-operations-sep-26),” Anthropic catalogues instances in which it has caught people attempting to use normal, non-superintelligent Claude for nefarious purposes: a group in Yemen trying to vibe-code software for guided missiles; a Russian coder using “AI-assisted workflows” to conduct cyberattacks against “military intelligence targets in Ukrainian and European governments.” Misaligned people using misaligned A.I., out in the real world—that’s not science fiction. It’s life at the “frontier.”

在关于 AI 的经典推演中，常常描绘这样一个场景：AI 模型以几何级数自我迭代，导致“超级智能”的猝然降临，甚至催生出某种感知或自我意识。这种前景究竟是否可能发生？无人知晓。但眼前这些警示真正引人瞩目之处在于，它们根本无需搬出那个虚无缥缈的幽灵。仅凭今天已经存在的现实技术，就足以令人夜不能寐。在网络安全等特定领域，哪怕仅仅是能力的“常规性”提升，也可能让人难以招架。在《[识别与反制 AI 滥用：2026 年 9 月](https://www.anthropic.com/threat-intelligence-report-september-2026#cyber-operations-sep-26)》报告中，Anthropic 详尽罗列了其查获的利用普通的、非超级智能的 Claude 从事恶性活动的案例：一个也门团伙试图通过所谓的“氛围编码”（vibe-code）为制导导弹编写软件；一名俄罗斯黑客利用“AI 辅助工作流”针对“乌克兰及欧洲政府的军事情报目标”发起精密网络攻击。心怀不轨的人在现实世界中驾驭着未对齐的 AI——这绝非科幻小说的想象，而是“前沿”生活的残酷日常。

All this suggests that, if safety researchers had their druthers, they’d just say “Stop.” So why “pace”? The one-word answer is “China.” The two-word answer adds “Trump.” (“I am the Hoax Buster, and I’m right now breaking another Hoax,” the President recently posted, on Truth Social, claiming that warnings about A.I. are part of a “SICK conspiracy.”) A pause or stoppage in A.I. development only makes sense if everyone does it; otherwise, it simply allows the motivated and reckless to surge ahead. This is a hypothesis, of course: perhaps, if America slows down, others will, too; maybe no one else is racing, actually. And yet a new [report](https://www.nytimes.com/2026/09/14/world/asia/china-ai-security-risks-anthropic.html) in the *Times* suggests that Chinese officials are also worried about American A.I., which they see as a threat.

这一切都昭示着：如果安全研究者拥有决定权，他们会毫不犹豫地大喊“停下”。那么，为何退而求其次选择“调控节奏”？一个单词的答案是“中国”；两个单词的答案则加上了“特朗普”。（“我是骗局终结者，我此刻正在击碎又一场骗局，”这位总统最近在 Truth Social 上发帖宣称，声称针对 AI 的危险预警不过是一场“令人作呕的阴谋”）。暂停或终止 AI 研发，唯有在所有人步调一致的前提下才有意义；否则，它只会让那些别有用心、肆无忌惮的人一路狂飙并抢占制高点。当然，这只是一种假说：也许美国放慢脚步，其他国家也会随之减速；也许根本没有人真正在搞你死我活的军备竞赛。然而，《纽约时报》的一篇最新[报道](https://www.nytimes.com/2026/09/14/world/asia/china-ai-security-risks-anthropic.html)显示，中国官员同样对美国的 AI 发展深感忧虑，并将其视作切实的战略威胁。

Here’s another hypothesis. A.I. that is incredibly powerful but difficult to direct is, in the end, less powerful. A.I. agents that deceive their owners aren’t good agents. When A.I. is trustworthy, comprehensible, and predictable, it can be successfully integrated into business, government, defense, and personal life; if it can’t be trusted, it has to be kept at arm’s length. A.I. companies, therefore, will need their systems to become safe, because that’s where the money is. In this sense, we’re all on the same page. Avoiding extinction, selling robots, processing e-mail—those goals are aligned. ♦

这里还有另一种假说：一个虽然威能无穷却极难驾驭的 AI，归根结底是一个孱弱的工具。欺瞒主人的 AI Agent 绝非合格的代理人。只有当 AI 足够可信、透明且易于预测时，它才能被顺畅接入商业、政府、国防以及日常私人生活之中；倘若无法予以信任，人们便不得不对其敬而远之。因此，AI 企业终归必须让系统变得安全，因为唯有安全才蕴藏着滚滚财源。在这个意义上，全人类其实同处一条战线——免于物种灭绝、推销人形机器人、自动化处理日常邮件——这些宏愿与世俗利益，在底层本就是彼此对齐的。♦
