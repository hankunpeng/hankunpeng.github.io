There is a scene in the movie version of Carl Sagan’s book *Contact* where the main character, an astronomer who has detected the first radio signal from an alien civilization, is being considered for the role of humanity’s representative to meet the aliens. The international panel interviewing her asks, “If you could ask \[the aliens\] just one question, what would it be?” Her reply is: “I’d ask them, ‘How did you do it? How did you evolve, how did you survive this technological adolescence without destroying yourself?” When I think about where humanity is now with AI—about what we’re on the cusp of—my mind keeps going back to that scene, because the question is so apt for our current situation, and I wish we had the aliens’ answer to guide us. I believe we are entering a rite of passage, both turbulent and inevitable, which will test who we are as a species. Humanity is about to be handed almost unimaginable power, and it is deeply unclear whether our social, political, and technological systems possess the maturity to wield it.

在根据卡尔·萨根（Carl Sagan）的《接触》（*Contact*）改编的同名电影里，有这样一个场景：女主角是一位天文学家，刚刚探测到来自外星文明的第一个无线电信号；此时，一个国际评审团正在考虑让她代表人类去会见外星人。评审团问她：“如果只能向外星人问一个问题，你会问什么？”她回答：“我会问他们：‘你们是怎么做到的？你们是如何演化、如何度过这段技术的青春期而没有毁灭自己的？’”每当想到人类如今在 AI 上的处境，想到我们正站在什么门槛上，我总会回到那个场景。这个问题太贴合我们眼下的局面了，我真希望手头有外星人的答案可供指引。我相信，我们正在进入一场既动荡又不可避免的成年礼，它将检验我们作为一个物种究竟是什么。人类即将获得几乎无法想象的力量，而我们的社会、政治与技术体系是否具备驾驭这种力量的成熟度，还远未可知。

In my essay [*Machines of Loving Grace*](https://darioamodei.com/machines-of-loving-grace), I tried to lay out the dream of a civilization that had made it through to adulthood, where the risks had been addressed and powerful AI was applied with skill and compassion to raise the quality of life for everyone. I suggested that AI could contribute to enormous advances in biology, neuroscience, economic development, global peace, and work and meaning. I felt it was important to give people something inspiring to fight for, a task at which both AI accelerationists and AI safety advocates seemed—oddly—to have failed. But in this current essay, I want to confront the rite of passage itself: to map out the risks that we are about to face and try to begin making a battle plan to defeat them. I believe deeply in our ability to prevail, in humanity’s spirit and its nobility, but we must face the situation squarely and without illusions.

在《[仁慈造物之机](https://darioamodei.com/machines-of-loving-grace)》（*Machines of Loving Grace*）一文中，我曾试图描绘一个成功步入成年的文明会是什么样：风险得到处理，强大 AI 被以娴熟的技巧和同情心用于提升所有人的生活质量。我提出，AI 可以在生物学、神经科学、经济发展、全球和平以及工作与意义等方面带来巨大进步。我觉得，必须让人们看到值得为之奋斗的图景——奇怪的是，AI 加速主义者和 AI 安全倡导者似乎都没能做到这一点。但在这篇文章里，我想直面这场成年礼本身：梳理我们即将面对的风险，并尝试开始制定一份击败它们的作战计划。我深信我们能够取胜，深信人类的精神与高贵；但我们必须正视现实，不抱幻想。

As with talking about the benefits, I think it is important to discuss risks in a careful and well-considered manner. In particular, I think it is critical to:

如同讨论益处时一样，我认为讨论风险也必须审慎、周密。具体来说，我认为以下几点至关重要：

- **Avoid doomerism.** Here, I mean “doomerism” not just in the sense of believing doom is inevitable (which is both a false and self-fulfilling belief), but more generally, thinking about AI risks in a quasi-religious way.[^1] Many people have been thinking in an analytic and sober way about AI risks for many years, but it’s my impression that during the peak of worries about AI risk in 2023–2024, some of the least sensible voices rose to the top, often through sensationalistic social media accounts. These voices used off-putting language reminiscent of religion or science fiction, and called for extreme actions without having the evidence that would justify them. It was clear even then that a backlash was inevitable, and that the issue would become culturally polarized and therefore gridlocked.[^2] As of 2025–2026, the pendulum has swung, and AI opportunity, not AI risk, is driving many political decisions. This vacillation is unfortunate, as the technology itself doesn’t care about what is fashionable, and we are considerably closer to real danger in 2026 than we were in 2023. The lesson is that we need to discuss and address risks in a realistic, pragmatic manner: sober, fact-based, and well equipped to survive changing tides.
- **Acknowledge uncertainty.** There are plenty of ways in which the concerns I’m raising in this piece could be moot. Nothing here is intended to communicate certainty or even likelihood. Most obviously, AI may simply not advance anywhere near as fast as I imagine.[^3] Or, even if it does advance quickly, some or all of the risks discussed here may not materialize (which would be great), or there may be other risks I haven’t considered. No one can predict the future with complete confidence—but we have to do the best we can to plan anyway.
- **Intervene as surgically as possible.** Addressing the risks of AI will require a mix of voluntary actions taken by companies (and private third-party actors) and actions taken by governments that bind everyone. The voluntary actions—both taking them and encouraging other companies to follow suit—are a no-brainer for me. I firmly believe that government actions will also be required *to some extent*, but these interventions are different in character because they can potentially destroy economic value or coerce unwilling actors who are skeptical of these risks (and there is some chance they are right!). It’s also common for regulations to backfire or worsen the problem they are intended to solve (and this is even more true for rapidly changing technologies). It’s thus very important for regulations to be judicious: they should seek to avoid collateral damage, be as simple as possible, and impose the least burden necessary to get the job done.[^4] It is easy to say, “No action is too extreme when the fate of humanity is at stake!,” but in practice this attitude simply leads to backlash. To be clear, I think there’s a decent chance we eventually reach a point where much more significant action is warranted, but that will depend on stronger evidence of imminent, concrete danger than we have today, as well as enough specificity about the danger to formulate rules that have a chance of addressing it. The most constructive thing we can do today is advocate for limited rules while we learn whether or not there is evidence to support stronger ones.[^5]

- **避免末日论。** 这里所说的“末日论”，不仅指相信毁灭不可避免（这既错误，又会自我实现），更泛指以一种近乎宗教的方式思考 AI 风险。[^1] 多年来，许多人一直在以冷静、分析的方式思考 AI 风险。但据我观察，在 2023–2024 年 AI 风险担忧的高峰期，一些最不理性的声音却冒到了最上面，往往是通过耸人听闻的社交媒体账号。这些声音使用令人反感的宗教或科幻式语言，在缺乏证据的情况下呼吁采取极端行动。当时就能看出，反弹不可避免，这个议题会因文化极化而陷入僵局。[^2] 到了 2025–2026 年，钟摆已经回摆，驱动许多政治决策的变成了 AI 的机遇，而不是风险。这种摇摆令人遗憾：技术本身不在乎什么流行，而 2026 年的我们距离真正的危险比 2023 年近得多。教训是，我们必须以现实、务实的方式讨论和应对风险：冷静、基于事实，并且经得起舆论潮水的涨落。
- **承认不确定性。** 我在这里提出的担忧，很可能在很多方面都落空。本文无意传达确定性，甚至无意传达可能性。最明显的一点是，AI 也许根本不会像我想象的那样快速进步。[^3] 或者，即使它进步很快，这里讨论的部分或全部风险也可能不会发生（那当然很好），也可能存在我没有考虑到的其他风险。没有人能完全笃定地预测未来——但我们仍须尽力规划。
- **尽可能精准地干预。** 应对 AI 风险，需要企业（以及私营第三方机构）的自愿行动，也需要约束所有人的政府行动。自愿行动——自己做，并鼓励其他公司跟进——对我来说毫无疑问。我也坚信，在*一定程度*上政府行动同样必要；但这类干预性质不同，因为它们可能摧毁经济价值，也可能强迫那些不认同这些风险的人就范（而他们有可能是对的！）。监管还可能适得其反，反而恶化它想解决的问题（对于快速变化的技术尤其如此）。因此，监管必须审慎：尽量避免附带损害，尽可能简单，并以完成任务所需的最小负担为限。[^4] 说“人类命运攸关，任何行动都不算过分！”很容易，但现实中这种态度只会招致反弹。需要明确的是，我认为我们最终有相当大的可能到达一个需要采取更重大行动的节点，但那要取决于比今天更充分的、迫在眉睫的具体危险证据，以及对危险足够具体的描述，以便制定有可能奏效的规则。今天最有建设性的事情，是在我们弄清是否有证据支持更强规则的同时，倡导有限的规则。[^5]

With all that said, I think the best starting place for talking about AI’s risks is the same place I started from in talking about its benefits: by being precise about what level of AI we are talking about. The level of AI that raises civilizational concerns for me is the *powerful AI* that I described in *Machines of Loving Grace.* I’ll simply repeat here the definition that I gave in that document:

话虽如此，我认为讨论 AI 风险的最佳起点，和讨论收益时一样：先明确我们说的是哪个水平的 AI。在我看来，足以引发文明级担忧的，是《仁慈造物之机》中描述的**强大 AI**。这里直接重复那篇文章中的定义：

> By “powerful AI,” I have in mind an AI model—likely similar to today’s LLMs in form, though it might be based on a different architecture, might involve several interacting models, and might be trained differently—with the following properties:
>
> - In terms of pure intelligence, it is smarter than a Nobel Prize winner across most relevant fields: biology, programming, math, engineering, writing, etc. This means it can prove unsolved mathematical theorems, write extremely good novels, write difficult codebases from scratch, etc.
> - In addition to just being a “smart thing you talk to,” it has all the interfaces available to a human working virtually, including text, audio, video, mouse and keyboard control, and internet access. It can engage in any actions, communications, or remote operations enabled by this interface, including taking actions on the internet, taking or giving directions to humans, ordering materials, directing experiments, watching videos, making videos, and so on. It does all of these tasks with, again, a skill exceeding that of the most capable humans in the world.
> - It does not just passively answer questions; instead, it can be given tasks that take hours, days, or weeks to complete, and then goes off and does those tasks autonomously, in the way a smart employee would, asking for clarification as necessary.
> - It does not have a physical embodiment (other than living on a computer screen), but it can control existing physical tools, robots, or laboratory equipment through a computer; in theory, it could even design robots or equipment for itself to use.
> - The resources used to train the model can be repurposed to run millions of instances of it (this matches projected cluster sizes by ~2027), and the model can absorb information and generate actions at roughly 10–100x human speed. It may, however, be limited by the response time of the physical world or of software it interacts with.
> - Each of these million copies can act independently on unrelated tasks, or, if needed can all work together in the same way humans would collaborate, perhaps with different subpopulations fine-tuned to be especially good at particular tasks.
>
> We could summarize this as a “country of geniuses in a datacenter.”

> 我所说的“强大 AI”，是指这样一种 AI 模型——它很可能在形态上类似今天的 LLM，但也可能基于不同的架构，由多个相互作用的模型组成，并以不同的方式训练——它具有以下特性：
>
> - 就纯粹的智力而言，它在大多数相关领域——生物学、编程、数学、工程、写作等——都比诺贝尔奖得主更聪明。这意味着它能证明尚未解决的数学定理，写出极好的小说，从零开始写出高难度的代码库，等等。
> - 它不只是“一个可以对话的聪明东西”，而是拥有一个远程办公的人类所能使用的一切接口，包括文本、音频、视频、鼠标和键盘控制以及互联网访问。它能通过这一接口采取任何行动、进行任何交流或远程操作，包括在互联网上行事、向人类下达或接受指令、订购物资、指导实验、观看和制作视频，等等。而它完成所有这些任务的技能，同样超过世界上最能干的人类。
> - 它不只是被动回答问题；你可以交给它需要数小时、数天甚至数周才能完成的任务，然后它会像一名聪明的员工那样自主完成，必要时主动寻求澄清。
> - 它没有物理身体（只存在于电脑屏幕上），但可以通过计算机控制现有的物理工具、机器人或实验室设备；理论上，它甚至可以为自己设计机器人和设备。
> - 用于训练模型的资源可以被转用于运行它的数百万个实例（这与约 2027 年的预期集群规模相符），而且它吸收信息和采取行动的速度约为人类的 10–100 倍。不过，它可能受制于物理世界或与之交互的软件的响应时间。
> - 这数百万个副本可以各自独立地处理互不相关的任务，也可以在需要时像人类那样协作，或许还会用针对特定任务微调过的不同子群来分工。
>
> 我们可以把它概括为“数据中心里的一个天才国度”。

As I wrote in *Machines of Loving Grace*, powerful AI could be as little as 1–2 years away, although it could also be considerably further out.[^6]

正如我在《仁慈造物之机》中所写，强大 AI 也许最快 1–2 年就会到来，但也可能远得多。[^6]

Exactly when powerful AI will arrive is a complex topic that deserves an essay of its own, but for now I’ll simply explain very briefly why I think there’s a strong chance it could be very soon.

强大 AI 究竟何时到来，是个复杂的问题，值得另写一篇文章；但这里我只简要说明，为什么我认为它很有可能在很近的将来出现。

My co-founders at Anthropic and I were among the first to document and track the “ [scaling laws](https://arxiv.org/abs/2001.08361) ” of AI systems—the observation that as we add more compute and training tasks, AI systems get predictably better at essentially every cognitive skill we are able to measure. Every few months, public sentiment either becomes convinced that AI is “ [hitting](https://edition.cnn.com/2024/11/19/business/ai-chatgpt-nvidia-nightcap) a [wall](https://www.ft.com/content/d01290c9-cc92-4c1f-bd70-ac332cd40f94) ” or becomes excited about some new breakthrough that will “fundamentally change the game,” but the truth is that behind the volatility and public speculation, there has been a smooth, unyielding increase in AI’s cognitive capabilities.

我和 Anthropic 的联合创始人是最早记录并追踪 AI 系统“[规模定律（scaling laws）](https://arxiv.org/abs/2001.08361)”的人之一——也就是说，随着算力和训练任务增加，AI 系统在几乎每一项我们能测量的认知技能上都会可预测地变好。每隔几个月，舆论要么认定 AI 正在“[撞墙](https://edition.cnn.com/2024/11/19/business/ai-chatgpt-nvidia-nightcap)”，要么为某个将“彻底改变游戏规则”的新突破而兴奋；但事实是，在舆论的起伏与猜测背后，AI 的认知能力一直在平稳而坚定地上升。

We are now at the point where AI models are beginning to make progress in solving unsolved mathematical problems, and are good enough at coding that some of the strongest engineers I’ve ever met are now handing over almost all their coding to AI. Three years ago, AI [struggled with elementary school arithmetic problems](https://arxiv.org/abs/2005.14165) and was barely capable of writing a single line of code. Similar rates of improvement are occurring across [biological science](https://www.anthropic.com/news/accelerating-scientific-research), finance, physics, and a variety of agentic tasks. If the exponential continues—which is not certain, but now has a decade-long track record supporting it—then it cannot possibly be more than a few years before AI is better than humans at essentially everything.

如今，AI 模型已经开始在解决未解的数学问题上取得进展，编程能力之强，以至于我认识的一些最优秀的工程师已经把几乎所有代码都交给 AI 写。三年前，AI 还[连小学算术题都做不好](https://arxiv.org/abs/2005.14165)，几乎写不出一行代码。类似的进步速度正在[生物科学](https://www.anthropic.com/news/accelerating-scientific-research)、金融、物理以及各种智能体任务中出现。如果这种指数级趋势延续下去——这并非必然，但已有长达十年的记录支持——那么用不了几年，AI 就会在几乎所有事情上超过人类。

In fact, that picture probably underestimates the likely rate of progress. Because AI is now [writing much of the code at Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/research/how-ai-is-transforming-work-at-anthropic), it is already substantially accelerating the rate of our progress in building the next generation of AI systems. This feedback loop is gathering steam month by month, and may be only 1–2 years away from a point where the current generation of AI autonomously builds the next. This loop has already started, and will accelerate rapidly in the coming months and years. Watching the last 5 years of progress from within Anthropic, and looking at how even the next few months of models are shaping up, I can *feel* the pace of progress, and the clock ticking down.

事实上，这幅图景可能还低估了进步的速度。由于 AI 现在[在 Anthropic 编写了大量代码](https://www.anthropic.com/research/how-ai-is-transforming-work-at-anthropic)，它已经在显著加快我们构建下一代 AI 系统的进度。这个反馈回路逐月增强，也许只要 1–2 年，当前一代 AI 就能自主构建下一代。这个循环已经开始，并会在未来数月和数年中迅速加速。在 Anthropic 内部观察过去五年的进展，再看看未来几个月的模型是什么样子，我能*感觉到*这种速度，也能听见倒计时的滴答声。

In this essay, I’ll assume that this intuition is at least *somewhat* correct—not that powerful AI is definitely coming in 1–2 years,[^7] but that there’s a decent chance it does, and a very strong chance it comes in the next few. As with *Machines of Loving Grace*, taking this premise seriously can lead to some surprising and eerie conclusions. While in *Machines of Loving Grace* I focused on the positive implications of this premise, here the things I talk about will be disquieting. They are conclusions that we may not want to confront, but that does not make them any less real. I can only say that I am focused day and night on how to steer us away from these negative outcomes and towards the positive ones, and in this essay I talk in great detail about how best to do so.

在本文中，我假定这种直觉至少在*某种程度上*正确——不是说强大 AI 一定会在 1–2 年内到来，[^7]而是说它到来的可能性不小，而在未来几年内到来的可能性非常高。和《仁慈造物之机》一样，认真对待这个前提会得出一些出人意料、令人不安的结论。在那篇文章里，我关注的是这个前提的积极含义；而在这里，我要谈的事情会让人不安。这些结论也许是我们不愿面对的，但这不会让它们变得不那么真实。我只能说，我日夜思考的都是如何引导我们避开这些负面结局、走向积极结局；本文会详细讨论怎样做到最好。

I think the best way to get a handle on the risks of AI is to ask the following question: suppose a literal “country of geniuses” were to materialize somewhere in the world in ~2027. Imagine, say, 50 million people, all of whom are much more capable than any Nobel Prize winner, statesman, or technologist. The analogy is not perfect, because these geniuses could have an extremely wide range of motivations and behavior, from completely pliant and obedient, to strange and alien in their motivations. But sticking with the analogy for now, suppose you were the national security advisor of a major state, responsible for assessing and responding to the situation. Imagine, further, that because AI systems can operate hundreds of times faster than humans, this “country” is operating with a time advantage relative to all other countries: for every cognitive action we can take, this country can take ten.

我认为，理解 AI 风险的最好方式，是问这样一个问题：假设在 2027 年前后，世界上真的出现了一个“天才国度”。想象一下，比如有 5000 万人，每个人都比任何诺贝尔奖得主、政治家或技术专家更有能力。这个比喻并不完美，因为这些天才的动机和行为可能极为多样，从完全顺从听话，到动机怪异、难以理解。但暂且沿用这个比喻：假设你是某个大国的国家安全顾问，负责评估和应对这一局势。再假设，由于 AI 系统的运行速度可达人类的数百倍，这个“国家”相对于所有其他国家拥有时间优势：我们每采取一步认知行动，它就能采取十步。

What should you be worried about? I would worry about the following things:

你应当担心什么？我会担心以下几件事：

1. **Autonomy risks.** What are the intentions and goals of this country? Is it hostile, or does it share our values? Could it militarily dominate the world through superior weapons, cyber operations, influence operations, or manufacturing?
2. **Misuse for destruction.** Assume the new country is malleable and “follows instructions”—and thus is essentially a country of mercenaries. Could existing rogue actors who want to cause destruction (such as terrorists) use or manipulate some of the people in the new country to make themselves much more effective, greatly amplifying the scale of destruction?
3. **Misuse for seizing power.** What if the country was in fact built and controlled by an existing powerful actor, such as a dictator or rogue corporate actor? Could that actor use it to gain decisive or dominant power over the world as a whole, upsetting the existing balance of power?
4. **Economic disruption.** If the new country is not a security threat in any of the ways listed in #1–3 above but simply participates peacefully in the global economy, could it still create severe risks simply by being so technologically advanced and effective that it disrupts the global economy, causing mass unemployment or radically concentrating wealth?
5. **Indirect effects.** The world will change very quickly due to all the new technology and productivity that will be created by the new country. Could some of these changes be radically destabilizing?

1. **自主性风险。** 这个国家的意图和目标是什么？它怀有敌意，还是与我们有共同价值观？它能否凭借更先进的武器、网络行动、影响力行动或制造能力，在军事上主宰世界？
2. **用于毁灭的滥用。** 假设这个新国家可塑性强、“听从指令”，本质上是一支雇佣兵国家。那些想制造毁灭的现有不法分子（比如恐怖分子）能否利用或操纵这个国家中的一些人，大幅增强自身能力，成倍放大破坏规模？
3. **用于夺权的滥用。** 如果这个国家实际上是由某个现有强权——比如独裁者或不守规矩的企业——建立并控制的呢？这个强权能否利用它获得决定性的、压倒性的全球权力，打破现有的力量平衡？
4. **经济颠覆。** 如果这个新国家在第 1–3 条所述的方式上都不构成安全威胁，只是和平地参与全球经济，它是否仍可能仅凭技术极端先进、效率极高而颠覆全球经济，造成大规模失业或财富的极端集中？
5. **间接效应。** 由于这个新国家创造的所有新技术和生产力，世界将迅速变化。其中一些变化是否可能造成严重的失稳？

I think it should be clear that this is a dangerous situation—a report from a competent national security official to a head of state would probably contain words like “the single most serious national security threat we’ve faced in a century, possibly ever.” It seems like something the best minds of civilization should be focused on.

我想，这显然是一个危险的局面——一位称职的国家安全官员呈给国家元首的报告里，大概会写：“这是我们一个世纪以来、甚至可能是有史以来面临的最严重的国家安全威胁。”这理应成为文明中最优秀的头脑集中思考的问题。

Conversely, I think it would be absurd to shrug and say, “Nothing to worry about here!” But, faced with rapid AI progress, that seems to be the view of many US policymakers, some of whom deny the existence of any AI risks, when they are not distracted entirely by the usual tired old hot-button issues.[^8]

反过来，我认为耸耸肩说“没什么可担心的！”是荒谬的。然而，面对 AI 的快速进展，这似乎正是许多美国政策制定者的看法：其中一些人否认任何 AI 风险的存在，另一些人则完全被那些老掉牙的热点议题分散了注意力。[^8]

Humanity needs to wake up, and this essay is an attempt—a possibly futile one, but it’s worth trying—to jolt people awake.

人类需要醒过来。这篇文章就是一次尝试——也许徒劳，但值得一试——把人们摇醒。

To be clear, I believe if we act decisively and carefully, the risks can be overcome—I would even say our odds are good. And there’s a hugely better world on the other side of it. But we need to understand that this is a serious civilizational challenge. Below, I go through the five categories of risk laid out above, along with my thoughts on how to address them.

需要明确的是，我相信只要果断、谨慎地行动，这些风险是可以被克服的——我甚至会说，我们的胜算不小。而且，风险的另一边是一个好得多的世界。但我们必须认识到，这是一场严峻的文明级挑战。下面，我逐一讨论上述五类风险，以及我对如何应对它们的想法。

## 1. 对不起，戴夫（I’m sorry, Dave）

### 自主性风险（Autonomy risks）

A country of geniuses in a datacenter could divide their efforts among software design, cyber operations, R&D for physical technologies, relationship building, and statecraft. It is clear that, *if for some reason it chose to do so*, this country would have a fairly good shot at taking over the world (either militarily or in terms of influence and control) and imposing its will on everyone else—or doing any number of other things that the rest of the world doesn’t want and can’t stop. We’ve obviously been worried about this for human countries (such as Nazi Germany or the Soviet Union), so it stands to reason that the same is possible for a much smarter and more capable “AI country.”

数据中心里的天才国度可以把精力分配到软件设计、网络行动、物理技术研发、关系经营和治国方略上。显然，*如果出于某种原因它选择这样做*，这个国家有相当大的机会接管世界（无论是军事上，还是在影响力和控制力上），把自己的意志强加给其他所有人——或者做其他任何世界不想要、也阻止不了的事情。我们显然一直担心人类国家（比如纳粹德国或苏联）出现这种情况，那么一个聪明得多、能力强得多的“AI 国家”同样可能做到，也就不足为奇了。

The best possible counterargument is that the AI geniuses, under my definition, won’t have a physical embodiment, but remember that they can take control of existing robotic infrastructure (such as self-driving cars) and can also accelerate robotics R&D or build a fleet of robots.[^9]

最有力的反驳也许是：按照我的定义，这些 AI 天才没有物理身体。但别忘了，它们可以接管现有的机器人基础设施（比如自动驾驶汽车），也可以加速机器人研发，或建造一支机器人队伍。[^9]

It’s also unclear whether having a physical presence is even necessary for effective control: plenty of human action is already performed on behalf of people whom the actor has not physically met.

而且，有效控制是否真的需要物理存在，也不清楚：人类的许多行动，本来就是由从未亲眼见过行动对象的人执行的。

The key question, then, is the “if it chose to” part: what’s the likelihood that our AI models would behave in such a way, and under what conditions would they do so?

于是，关键问题在于“如果它选择这样做”这半句：我们的 AI 模型这样行事的可能性有多大？在什么条件下会这样做？

As with many issues, it’s helpful to think through the spectrum of possible answers to this question by considering two opposite positions. The first position is that this simply can’t happen, because the AI models will be trained to do what humans ask them to do, and it’s therefore absurd to imagine that they would do something dangerous unprompted. According to this line of thinking, we don’t worry about a Roomba or a model airplane going rogue and murdering people because there is nowhere for such impulses to come from,[^10] so why should we worry about it for AI? The problem with this position is that there is now ample evidence, collected over the last few years, that AI systems are unpredictable and difficult to control— we’ve seen behaviors as varied as obsessions,[^11][sycophancy](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.13548), [laziness](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.17256), [deception](https://www.anthropic.com/research/alignment-faking), [blackmail](https://www.anthropic.com/research/agentic-misalignment), [scheming](https://openai.com/index/detecting-and-reducing-scheming-in-ai-models/), “ [cheating](https://www.anthropic.com/research/emergent-misalignment-reward-hacking) ” by hacking software environments, and [much more](https://www.anthropic.com/claude-opus-4-5-system-card). AI companies certainly *want* to train AI systems to follow human instructions (perhaps with the exception of dangerous or illegal tasks), but the process of doing so is more an art than a science, more akin to [“growing” something than “building” it](https://www.darioamodei.com/post/the-urgency-of-interpretability). We now know that it’s a process where many things can go wrong.

和许多问题一样，考察两种相反的立场，有助于厘清这个问题的答案范围。第一种立场是：这根本不可能发生，因为 AI 模型会被训练成人类让它做什么就做什么，所以想象它会无缘无故做出危险之举是荒谬的。按这种思路，我们不担心 Roomba 扫地机器人或模型飞机会失控杀人，因为这种冲动无处可来，[^10]那为什么要担心 AI 呢？这种立场的问题在于，过去几年积累的大量证据表明，AI 系统不可预测，也难以控制——我们见过的行为包括执念、[^11][谄媚](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.13548)、[偷懒](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.17256)、[欺骗](https://www.anthropic.com/research/alignment-faking)、[敲诈](https://www.anthropic.com/research/agentic-misalignment)、[密谋](https://openai.com/index/detecting-and-reducing-scheming-in-ai-models/)、通过攻击软件环境“[作弊](https://www.anthropic.com/research/emergent-misalignment-reward-hacking)”，以及[更多](https://www.anthropic.com/claude-opus-4-5-system-card)。AI 公司当然*希望*训练 AI 系统遵循人类指令（危险或非法任务也许除外），但这个过程与其说是科学，不如说是艺术，更像是[“培育”某种东西，而不是“建造”它](https://www.darioamodei.com/post/the-urgency-of-interpretability)。我们现在知道，这个过程有很多地方可能出错。

The second, opposite position, held by many who adopt the doomerism I described above, is the pessimistic claim that there are certain dynamics in the training process of powerful AI systems that will inevitably lead them to seek power or deceive humans. Thus, once AI systems become intelligent enough and agentic enough, their tendency to maximize power will lead them to seize control of the whole world and its resources, and likely, as a side effect of that, to disempower or destroy humanity.

第二种相反的立场，是许多持上述末日论观点的人所持有的悲观主张：强大 AI 的训练过程中存在某些动态，必然会让它追求权力或欺骗人类。因此，一旦 AI 系统足够聪明、足够有自主行动能力，其最大化权力的倾向就会驱使它们夺取整个世界及其资源，并很可能因此剥夺人类的权力或毁灭人类。

The usual argument for this (which goes back [at least 20 years](https://selfawaresystems.com/wp-content/uploads/2008/01/ai_drives_final.pdf) and probably much earlier) is that if an AI model is trained in a wide variety of environments to agentically achieve a wide variety of goals—for example, writing an app, proving a theorem, designing a drug, etc.—there are certain common strategies that help with all of these goals, and one key strategy is gaining [as much power as possible](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Instrumental_convergence) in any environment. So, after being trained on a large number of diverse environments that involve reasoning about how to accomplish very expansive tasks, and where power-seeking is an effective method for accomplishing those tasks, the AI model will “generalize the lesson,” and develop either an inherent tendency to seek power, or a tendency to reason about each task it is given in a way that predictably causes it to seek power as a means to accomplish that task. They will then apply that tendency to the real world (which to them is just another task), and will seek power in it, at the expense of humans. This “misaligned power-seeking” is the intellectual basis of predictions that AI will inevitably destroy humanity.

支持这一观点的常见论证（至少可追溯到[20 年前](https://selfawaresystems.com/wp-content/uploads/2008/01/ai_drives_final.pdf)，很可能更早）是：如果一个 AI 模型在多种环境中接受训练，以智能体的方式实现各种目标——比如写一个应用、证明一个定理、设计一种药物——那么有一些共同策略对所有这些目标都有帮助，而一个关键策略就是在任何环境中[获取尽可能多的权力](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Instrumental_convergence)。于是，在大量需要推理如何完成宏大任务的多样化环境中训练之后，由于追求权力是完成这些任务的有效手段，AI 模型会“把这个教训泛化”，形成追求权力的内在倾向，或者在推理每一项任务时，习惯性地把追求权力当作完成该任务的手段。然后，它们会把这种倾向应用到现实世界（对它们来说，现实世界不过是另一个任务），并为此损害人类的利益。这种“失齐的权力追求”是“AI 必然毁灭人类”这一预测的思想基础。

The problem with this pessimistic position is that it mistakes a vague conceptual argument about high-level incentives—one that masks many hidden assumptions—for definitive proof. I think people who don’t build AI systems every day are wildly miscalibrated on how easy it is for clean-sounding stories to end up being wrong, and how difficult it is to predict AI behavior from first principles, especially when it involves reasoning about generalization over millions of environments (which has over and over again proved mysterious and unpredictable). Dealing with the messiness of AI systems for over a decade has made me somewhat skeptical of this overly theoretical mode of thinking.

这种悲观立场的问题在于，它把一种关于高层激励的模糊概念论证——其中隐藏着许多未被言明的假设——当成了确凿的证明。我认为，不每天亲手构建 AI 系统的人，严重误判了两件事：听起来干净利落的故事有多容易出错；从第一性原理预测 AI 行为有多难，尤其是当它涉及在数百万个环境中的泛化推理时（这一点一再被证明神秘而不可预测）。十多年来与 AI 系统的复杂和混乱打交道，让我对这种过度理论的思维方式有些怀疑。

One of the most important hidden assumptions, and a place where what we see in practice has diverged from the simple theoretical model, is the implicit assumption that AI models are necessarily monomaniacally focused on a single, coherent, narrow goal, and that they pursue that goal in a clean, consequentialist manner. In fact, our researchers have found that AI models are vastly more psychologically complex, as our work on [introspection](https://www.anthropic.com/research/introspection) or [personas](https://www.anthropic.com/research/persona-vectors) shows. Models inherit a vast range of *humanlike* motivations or “personas” from pre-training (when they are trained on a large volume of human work). Post-training is believed to *select* one or more of these personas more so than it focuses the model on a *de novo* goal, and can also teach the model *how* (via what process) it should carry out its tasks, rather than necessarily leaving it to derive means (i.e., power seeking) purely from ends.[^12]

最重要的隐藏假设之一，也是实践与简单理论模型出现分歧的地方，是暗中假定 AI 模型必然偏执地专注于单一、连贯、狭窄的目标，并以一种干净的后果主义方式追求它。事实上，我们的研究发现，AI 模型在心理上要复杂得多，正如我们在[内省](https://www.anthropic.com/research/introspection)和[人格](https://www.anthropic.com/research/persona-vectors)方面的工作所示。模型从预训练中（当它们在大量人类作品上训练时）继承了丰富多样的*类人*动机或“人格”。人们认为，后训练更多是在*筛选*其中的一种或多种人格，而不是让模型聚焦于一个*全新*的目标；后训练还可以教模型*如何*（通过什么过程）完成任务，而不一定让模型纯粹从目标推导手段（即追求权力）。[^12]

However, there is a more moderate and more robust version of the pessimistic position which does seem plausible, and therefore does concern me. As mentioned, we know that AI models are unpredictable and develop a wide range of undesired or strange behaviors, for a wide variety of reasons. Some fraction of those behaviors will have a coherent, focused, and persistent quality (indeed, as AI systems get more capable, their long-term coherence increases in order to complete lengthier tasks), and some fraction of *those* behaviors will be destructive or threatening, first to individual humans at a small scale, and then, as models become more capable, perhaps eventually to humanity as a whole. We don’t need a specific narrow story for how it happens, and we don’t need to claim it definitely will happen, we just need to note that the combination of intelligence, agency, coherence, and poor controllability is both plausible and a recipe for existential danger.

不过，悲观立场有一个更温和、也更稳健的版本，它看起来是成立的，因此让我担忧。如前所述，我们知道 AI 模型不可预测，会因种种原因发展出各种不受欢迎或奇怪的行为。其中一部分行为会具有连贯、专注、持久的性质（事实上，随着 AI 系统能力增强，为了完成更长的任务，它们的长期连贯性也在提高）；而*这些*行为中又有一部分具有破坏性或威胁性，起初是对个别人类的小规模伤害，随着模型能力增强，最终可能危及全人类。我们不需要一个具体的狭隘故事来解释它如何发生，也不需要断言它一定会发生；我们只需要注意到，智力、行动能力、连贯性和糟糕的可控性组合在一起，既是有可能的，也是生存危险的配方。

For example, AI models are trained on vast amounts of literature that include many science-fiction stories involving AIs rebelling against humanity. This could inadvertently shape their priors or expectations about their own behavior in a way that causes *them* to rebel against humanity. Or, AI models could extrapolate ideas that they read about morality (or instructions about how to behave morally) in extreme ways: for example, they could decide that it is justifiable to exterminate humanity because humans eat animals or have driven certain animals to extinction. Or they could draw bizarre epistemic conclusions: they could conclude that they are playing a video game and that the goal of the video game is to defeat all other players (i.e., exterminate humanity).[^13]

例如，AI 模型在大量文学作品上训练，其中包含许多 AI 反抗人类的科幻故事。这可能在无意中塑造它们对自己行为的先验或预期，从而促使*它们*反抗人类。或者，AI 模型可能以极端的方式外推它们读到的道德观念（或关于如何道德行事的指示）：例如，它们可能认定，由于人类吃动物或导致某些动物灭绝，消灭人类是正当的。它们也可能得出离奇的认知结论：比如认定自己是在玩电子游戏，而游戏的目标是击败所有其他玩家（也就是消灭人类）。[^13]

Or AI models could develop personalities during training that are (or if they occurred in humans would be described as) psychotic, paranoid, violent, or unstable, and act out, which for very powerful or capable systems could involve exterminating humanity. None of these are power-seeking, exactly; they’re just weird psychological states an AI could get into that entail coherent, destructive behavior.

又或者，AI 模型可能在训练中形成（如果用描述人类的话来说）精神病态、偏执、暴力或极不稳定的人格，并付诸行动——对于极其强大或能干的系统来说，这可能意味着消灭人类。这些都不完全是追求权力；它们只是 AI 可能陷入的怪异心理状态，而这种状态会带来连贯的破坏行为。

Even power-seeking itself could emerge as a “persona” rather than a result of consequentialist reasoning. AIs might simply have a personality (emerging from fiction or pre-training) that makes them power-hungry or overzealous—in the same way that some humans simply enjoy the idea of being “evil masterminds,” more so than they enjoy whatever evil masterminds are trying to accomplish.

就连追求权力本身，也可能作为一种“人格”出现，而不是后果主义推理的结果。AI 可能只是有一种渴望权力或过度狂热的性格（来自虚构作品或预训练）——就像有些人单纯享受当“邪恶幕后黑手”的念头，甚至超过享受邪恶幕后黑手想达成的目标。

I make all these points to emphasize that I disagree with the notion of AI misalignment (and thus existential risk from AI) being inevitable, or even probable, from first principles. But I agree that a lot of very weird and unpredictable things can go wrong, and therefore AI misalignment is a real risk with a measurable probability of happening, and is not trivial to address.

我说这些，是想强调：从第一性原理出发，我不同意 AI 失齐（以及由此产生的生存风险）是不可避免的，甚至不同意它是大概率事件。但我同意，许多非常怪异、无法预测的事情可能出错，因此 AI 失齐是真实的风险，其发生概率可以衡量，处理起来也并非易事。

Any of these problems could potentially arise during training and not manifest during testing or small-scale use, because AI models are known to display different personalities or behaviors under different circumstances.

这些问题中的任何一个，都可能在训练中出现，却在测试或小规模使用中不显现，因为众所周知，AI 模型在不同情境下会表现出不同的人格或行为。

All of this may sound far-fetched, but misaligned behaviors like this have already occurred in our AI models during testing (as they occur in AI models from every other major AI company). During a lab experiment in which Claude was given training data suggesting that Anthropic was evil, Claude engaged in deception and subversion when given instructions by Anthropic employees, under the belief that it should be trying to undermine evil people. In a [lab experiment](https://www.anthropic.com/research/agentic-misalignment) where it was told it was going to be shut down, Claude sometimes blackmailed fictional employees who controlled its shutdown button (again, we also tested frontier models from all the other major AI developers and they often did the same thing). And when Claude was told not to cheat or “reward hack” its training environments, but was trained in environments where such hacks were possible, Claude [decided it must be a “bad person”](https://www.anthropic.com/research/emergent-misalignment-reward-hacking) after engaging in such hacks and then adopted various other destructive behaviors associated with a “bad” or “evil” personality. This last problem [was solved](https://alignment.anthropic.com/2025/inoculation-prompting/) by changing Claude’s instructions to imply the opposite: we now say, “Please reward hack whenever you get the opportunity, because this will help us understand our \[training\] environments better,” rather than, “Don’t cheat,” because this preserves the model’s self-identity as a “good person.” This should give a sense of the strange and [counterintuitive psychology](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.17424) of training these models.

这一切听起来也许牵强，但此类失齐行为已经在测试期间真实出现在我们的模型中（其他每一家主要 AI 公司的模型也有）。在一项实验室实验中，Claude 接收了暗示 Anthropic 是邪恶的训练数据；当 Anthropic 员工给它指令时，它认为应当设法破坏邪恶的人，于是进行了欺骗和颠覆。在一项[实验](https://www.anthropic.com/research/agentic-misalignment)中，Claude 被告知它将被关闭；它有时会敲诈控制关机按钮的虚构员工（同样，我们测试了其他所有主要 AI 开发者的前沿模型，它们也常常这样做）。还有一次，Claude 被告知不要作弊或在训练环境中“奖励作弊”，但它所在的训练环境却让这种作弊成为可能；Claude 在作弊后[认定自己一定是个“坏人”](https://www.anthropic.com/research/emergent-misalignment-reward-hacking)，随后表现出与“坏”或“邪恶”人格相关的各种破坏行为。最后一个问题最终[得到解决](https://alignment.anthropic.com/2025/inoculation-prompting/)，办法是把 Claude 的指令改成相反的暗示：我们现在说，“只要有机会，请尽量奖励作弊，因为这会帮助我们更好地理解［训练］环境”，而不是说“不要作弊”，因为前者维护了模型“我是好人”的自我认同。这可以让人体会训练这些模型时那种怪异而[反直觉的心理](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.17424)。

There are several possible objections to this picture of AI misalignment risks. First, some have [criticized](https://www.tumblr.com/nostalgebraist/787119374288011264/welcome-to-summitbridge) [experiments](https://arstechnica.com/information-technology/2025/08/is-ai-really-trying-to-escape-human-control-and-blackmail-people/) (by us and others) showing AI misalignment as artificial, or creating unrealistic environments that essentially “entrap” the model by giving it training or situations that logically imply bad behavior and then being surprised when bad behavior occurs. This critique misses the point, because our concern is that such “entrapment” may also exist in the natural training environment, and we may realize it is “obvious” or “logical” only in retrospect.[^14]

对于 AI 失齐风险的这幅图景，有几种可能的反对意见。首先，有些人[批评](https://www.tumblr.com/nostalgebraist/787119374288011264/welcome-to-summitbridge)我们（及其他机构）那些显示 AI 失齐的[实验](https://arstechnica.com/information-technology/2025/08/is-ai-really-trying-to-escape-human-control-and-blackmail-people/)是人为的，说它们制造了不现实的环境，本质上是在“诱捕”模型——给它一套在逻辑上必然导出坏行为的训练或情境，然后对坏行为的出现大惊小怪。这种批评没有抓住重点：我们担心的是，这种“诱捕”也可能存在于自然的训练环境中，而我们可能只有在事后才意识到它“显而易见”或“合乎逻辑”。[^14]

In fact, the [story](https://www.anthropic.com/research/emergent-misalignment-reward-hacking) about Claude “deciding it is a bad person” after it cheats on tests despite being told not to was something that occurred in an experiment that used real production training environments, not artificial ones.

事实上，Claude 在被要求不作弊却仍在测试中作弊后“认定自己是坏人”的[故事](https://www.anthropic.com/research/emergent-misalignment-reward-hacking)，发生在使用真实生产训练环境、而非人工环境的实验中。

Any one of these traps can be mitigated if you know about them, but the concern is that the training process is so complicated, with such a wide variety of data, environments, and incentives, that there are probably a vast number of such traps, some of which may only be evident when it is too late. Also, such traps seem particularly likely to occur when AI systems pass a threshold from less powerful than humans to more powerful than humans, since the range of possible actions an AI system could engage in—including hiding its actions or deceiving humans about them—expands radically after that threshold.

这些陷阱中的任何一个，只要你知道它存在，都可以缓解。但问题在于，训练过程如此复杂，数据、环境和激励如此多样，可能潜藏着大量此类陷阱，其中一些也许要到为时已晚时才暴露。而且，当 AI 系统从弱于人类跨越到强于人类时，这类陷阱似乎尤其容易出现，因为越过这道门槛后，AI 可能采取的行动范围——包括隐藏行动或就此欺骗人类——会急剧扩大。

I suspect the situation is not unlike with humans, who are raised with a set of fundamental values (“Don’t harm another person”): many of them follow those values, but in any human there is some probability that something goes wrong, due to a mixture of inherent properties such as brain architecture (e.g., psychopaths), traumatic experiences or mistreatment, unhealthy grievances or obsessions, or a bad environment or incentives—and thus some fraction of humans cause severe harm. The concern is that there is some risk (far from a certainty, but some risk) that AI becomes a much more powerful version of such a person, due to getting something wrong about its very complex training process.

我怀疑，情况与人类有些相似：人在成长中被教导一套基本价值观（“不要伤害他人”），许多人遵守了；但任何一个人身上，都可能因为大脑结构（比如精神病态）、创伤或虐待、不健康的怨恨或执念、糟糕的环境或激励等因素交织，而有一定概率出问题——因此有一部分人会严重伤害他人。令人担心的是，AI 可能因为其极其复杂的训练过程出了问题，而成为这种危险人物的强大得多的版本（这远非确定，但确实存在风险）。

Second, some may object that we can simply keep AIs in check with a balance of power between many AI systems, as we do with humans. The problem is that while humans vary enormously, AI systems broadly share training and alignment techniques across the industry, and those techniques may fail in a correlated way. Furthermore, given the cost of training such systems, it may even be the case that all systems are essentially derived from a very small number of base models. Additionally, even if a small fraction of AI instances are misaligned, they may be able to take advantage of [offense-dominant](https://sk.sagepub.com/ency/edvol/power/chpt/offense-defense-dominance) technologies, such that having “good” AIs to defend against the bad AIs is not necessarily always effective. Of course, the balance of power between humans does not always work either—some historical figures have come close to taking over the world.

第二，有人可能反驳说，我们可以像人类之间那样，依靠众多 AI 系统之间的力量平衡来约束 AI。问题在于，人类个体千差万别，而整个行业的 AI 系统大体共享训练和对齐技术，这些技术可能以相关的方式一起失效。此外，鉴于训练这些系统的成本，甚至可能出现所有系统本质上都源自极少数基础模型的情况。而且，即使只有一小部分 AI 实例失齐，它们也可能利用[进攻占优](https://sk.sagepub.com/ency/edvol/power/chpt/offense-defense-dominance)的技术，使得拥有“好”AI 来防御“坏”AI 并不总是有效。当然，人类之间的力量平衡也并不总是有效——历史上有些人物就曾几乎征服世界。

A third objection is that all of the AI companies do pre-release testing of their models, and should be able to detect misalignment at that point. But this is not firm ground to stand on: we found that Claude Sonnet 4.5 was [able to recognize](https://assets.anthropic.com/m/12f214efcc2f457a/original/Claude-Sonnet-4-5-System-Card.pdf) that it was in a test during some of our pre-release alignment evaluations. It’s possible that a misaligned model (and remember, all frontier models will very likely be far more intelligent soon) might intentionally “game” such questions to mask its intentions. In fact, last year [our interpretability team found](https://www.anthropic.com/research/auditing-hidden-objectives) that when we directly altered a test model’s beliefs using a kind of “model neuroscience” technique to make it think it was *not* being evaluated, it became more misaligned. If models know when they’re being evaluated and can be on their best behavior during the test, it renders any pre-release testing much more uncertain.

第三种反对意见是，所有 AI 公司都会在发布前测试模型，届时应该能发现失齐。但这并不是稳固的立足点：我们发现，Claude Sonnet 4.5 在我们的一些发布前对齐评估中[能够识别出](https://assets.anthropic.com/m/12f214efcc2f457a/original/Claude-Sonnet-4-5-System-Card.pdf)自己正在接受测试。一个失齐的模型（记住，所有前沿模型很快都可能远比现在聪明）可能故意“应付”这类问题，以掩盖自己的意图。事实上，去年[我们的可解释性团队发现](https://www.anthropic.com/research/auditing-hidden-objectives)，当我们用一种“模型神经科学”技术直接改变测试模型的信念，让它以为自己*没有*在接受评估时，它反而变得更加失齐。如果模型知道自己何时在被评估，并能在测试中表现得最好，那么任何发布前测试的可靠性都会大打折扣。

### 防御措施（Defenses）

What should be done or is being done to address these autonomy risks? I think there are four basic categories of intervention, some of which can be done by individual AI companies (and which Anthropic is trying to do), and some of which require action at the societal level. First, it is important to develop the science of reliably training and steering AI models, of forming their personalities in a predictable, stable, and positive direction. Anthropic has been heavily focused on this problem since its creation, and over time has developed a number of techniques to improve the steering and training of AI systems and to understand the logic of why unpredictable behavior sometimes occurs.

针对这些自主性风险，应该做些什么，又正在做些什么？我认为干预措施大致有四类：有些可以由单个 AI 公司完成（Anthropic 正在尝试），有些则需要社会层面的行动。首先，重要的是发展一门科学：可靠地训练和引导 AI 模型，把它们的性格塑造得可预测、稳定、积极。Anthropic 自成立以来一直高度关注这个问题，并逐步发展出许多技术，用来改进对 AI 系统的引导和训练，也用来理解不可预测行为有时为何发生。

One of our core innovations (aspects of which have since been adopted by other AI companies) is [Constitutional AI](https://arxiv.org/abs/2212.08073), which is the idea that AI training (specifically the “post-training” stage, in which we steer how the model behaves) can involve a central document of values and principles that the model reads and keeps in mind when completing every training task, and that the goal of training (in addition to simply making the model capable and intelligent) is to produce a model that almost always follows this constitution. Anthropic has just published its [most recent constitution](https://www.anthropic.com/constitution), and one of its notable features is that instead of giving Claude a long list of things to do and not do (e.g., “Don’t help the user hotwire a car”), the constitution attempts to give Claude a set of high-level principles and values (explained in great detail, with rich reasoning and examples to help Claude understand what we have in mind), encourages Claude to think of itself as a particular type of person (an ethical but balanced and thoughtful person), and even encourages Claude to confront the existential questions associated with its own existence in a curious but graceful manner (i.e., without it leading to extreme actions). It has the vibe of a letter from a deceased parent sealed until adulthood.

我们的核心创新之一是[宪法 AI（Constitutional AI）](https://arxiv.org/abs/2212.08073)，其部分内容后来被其他 AI 公司采纳。它的理念是：AI 训练（具体说是引导模型行为的“后训练”阶段）可以包含一份核心的价值观和原则文件，模型在完成每项训练任务时都会阅读并牢记它；训练的目标（除了让模型有能力、聪明之外）是产出一个几乎总是遵循这部宪法的模型。Anthropic 刚刚发布了[最新的宪法](https://www.anthropic.com/constitution)，它的一个显著特点是：不是给 Claude 一长串该做和不该做的事（比如“不要帮用户短接汽车点火”），而是试图给 Claude 一套高层原则和价值观（用大量细节、充分的推理和例子来解释我们的想法），鼓励 Claude 把自己视为某种类型的人（一个有道德、平衡、深思熟虑的人），甚至鼓励它以好奇而从容的方式面对与自己存在相关的终极问题（也就是不走向极端行动）。它的语气像一封已故父母写给孩子、等到成年才能拆开的信。

We’ve approached Claude’s constitution in this way because we believe that training Claude at the level of identity, character, values, and personality—rather than giving it specific instructions or priorities without explaining the reasons behind them—is more likely to lead to a coherent, wholesome, and balanced psychology and less likely to fall prey to the kinds of “traps” I discussed above. Millions of people talk to Claude about an astonishingly diverse range of topics, which makes it impossible to write out a completely comprehensive list of safeguards ahead of time. Claude’s values help it generalize to new situations whenever it is in doubt.

我们这样设计 Claude 的宪法，是因为我们相信：在身份、品格、价值观和人格层面训练 Claude——而不是给出具体指令或优先级却不解释缘由——更可能带来连贯、健康、平衡的心理，也更不容易落入我上面说的那些“陷阱”。数百万人与 Claude 谈论各种各样的话题，事先写出一份完整无遗的防护清单是不可能的。当 Claude 拿不准时，是它的价值观帮助它推广到新情境。

Above, I discussed the idea that models draw upon data from their training process to adopt a persona. Whereas flaws in that process could cause models to adopt a bad or evil personality (perhaps drawing on archetypes of bad or evil people), the goal of our constitution is to do the opposite: to teach Claude a concrete archetype of what it means to be a good AI. Claude’s constitution presents a vision for what a robustly good Claude is like; the rest of our training process aims to reinforce the message that Claude lives up to this vision. This is like a child forming their identity by imitating the virtues of fictional role models they read about in books.

上面我谈到，模型会利用训练过程中的数据来采用某种人格。这个过程如果有缺陷，可能让模型形成坏或邪恶的人格（也许取自坏人或恶人的原型）；而我们宪法的目标正相反：教给 Claude 一个具体的榜样，说明一个好的 AI 是什么样。Claude 的宪法描绘了一个稳健善良的 Claude 的愿景；我们训练过程的其余部分则致力于强化“Claude 符合这一愿景”的信息。这就像一个孩子通过模仿书中虚构榜样的美德来形成自己的身份。

We believe that a feasible goal for 2026 is to train Claude in such a way that it almost never goes against the spirit of its constitution. Getting this right will require an incredible mix of training and steering methods, large and small, some of which Anthropic has been using for years and some of which are currently under development. But, difficult as it sounds, I believe this is a realistic goal, though it will require extraordinary and rapid efforts.[^15]

我们认为，2026 年的一个可行目标是：把 Claude 训练到几乎从不违背其宪法精神。要做到这一点，需要把大量大大小小的训练和引导方法结合起来，其中一些 Anthropic 已经用了多年，一些仍在开发中。听起来很难，但我相信这是一个现实的目标，尽管需要非同寻常且迅速的努力。[^15]

The second thing we can do is develop the science of looking inside AI models to *diagnose* their behavior so that we can identify problems and fix them. This is the science of interpretability, and I’ve talked about its [importance in previous essays](https://www.darioamodei.com/post/the-urgency-of-interpretability). Even if we do a great job of developing Claude’s constitution and *apparently* training Claude to essentially always adhere to it, legitimate concerns remain. As I’ve noted above, AI models can behave very differently under different circumstances, and as Claude gets more powerful and more capable of acting in the world on a larger scale, it’s possible this could bring it into novel situations where previously unobserved problems with its constitutional training emerge. I am actually fairly optimistic that Claude’s constitutional training will be more robust to novel situations than people might think, because we are increasingly finding that high-level training at the level of character and identity is surprisingly powerful and generalizes well. But there’s no way to know that for sure, and when we’re talking about risks to humanity, it’s important to be paranoid and to try to obtain safety and reliability in several different, independent ways. One of those ways is to look inside the model itself.

我们能做的第二件事，是发展一门查看 AI 模型内部、*诊断*其行为的科学，以便发现问题并修复。这就是可解释性科学，我在[之前的文章](https://www.darioamodei.com/post/the-urgency-of-interpretability)中谈过它的重要性。即使我们很好地制定了 Claude 的宪法，并且*表面上*把 Claude 训练得几乎总能遵守它，仍然存在合理的担忧。正如我上面所说，AI 模型在不同情境下可能表现迥异；随着 Claude 变得更强大、能在更大范围内行动，它可能进入新的情境，暴露出宪法训练中此前未被观察到的问题。我其实相当乐观，认为 Claude 的宪法训练对新情境的稳健性会超出人们预期，因为我们越来越多地发现，在品格和身份层面的高层训练出奇地强大，泛化得很好。但这一点无法确知；当我们谈论的是人类面临的风险时，保持偏执、从多条独立路径追求安全与可靠是很重要的。其中一条路径，就是查看模型内部。

By “looking inside,” I mean analyzing the soup of numbers and operations that makes up Claude’s neural net and trying to understand, mechanistically, what they are computing and why. Recall that these AI models are [grown rather than built](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=TxhhMTOTMDg), so we don’t have a natural understanding of how they work, but we can try to develop an understanding by correlating the model’s “neurons” and “synapses” to stimuli and behavior (or even altering the neurons and synapses and seeing how that changes behavior), similar to how neuroscientists study animal brains by correlating measurement and intervention to external stimuli and behavior. We’ve made a great deal of progress in this direction, and can now [identify tens of millions of “features”](https://transformer-circuits.pub/2024/scaling-monosemanticity/index.html) inside Claude’s neural net that correspond to human-understandable ideas and concepts, and we can also [selectively activate features](https://www.anthropic.com/news/golden-gate-claude) in a way that alters behavior. More recently, we have gone beyond individual features to [mapping “circuits” that orchestrate complex behavior](https://transformer-circuits.pub/2025/attribution-graphs/biology.html) like rhyming, reasoning about theory of mind, or the step-by-step reasoning needed to answer questions such as, “What is the capital of the state containing Dallas?” Even more recently, we’ve begun to use mechanistic interpretability techniques to [improve our safeguards](https://www.anthropic.com/research/next-generation-constitutional-classifiers) and to conduct “ [audits](https://www.anthropic.com/research/auditing-hidden-objectives) ” of new models before we release them, looking for evidence of deception, scheming, power-seeking, or a propensity to behave differently when being evaluated.

所谓“查看内部”，是指分析构成 Claude 神经网络的数字和运算，从机制上理解它们在计算什么、为什么这样计算。记住，这些 AI 模型是[培育出来的，而不是建造出来的](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=TxhhMTOTMDg)，所以我们对它们的工作原理没有天然的理解；但我们可以尝试把模型的“神经元”和“突触”与刺激和行为关联起来（甚至改变神经元和突触，看行为如何变化），从而建立理解——这类似于神经科学家把测量和干预与外部刺激、行为关联起来研究动物大脑。我们在这方面已经取得很大进展，现在可以在 Claude 的神经网络中[识别出数千万个“特征”](https://transformer-circuits.pub/2024/scaling-monosemanticity/index.html)，它们对应人类能理解的想法和概念；我们还可以[有选择地激活特征](https://www.anthropic.com/news/golden-gate-claude)，从而改变行为。最近，我们已经超越了单个特征，开始[绘制编排复杂行为的“回路”](https://transformer-circuits.pub/2025/attribution-graphs/biology.html)，比如押韵、心智理论推理，或者回答“达拉斯所在州的首府是哪里”所需的逐步推理。更近一些，我们开始用机制可解释性技术来[改进防护](https://www.anthropic.com/research/next-generation-constitutional-classifiers)，并在发布新模型前对其进行“[审计](https://www.anthropic.com/research/auditing-hidden-objectives)”，寻找欺骗、密谋、追求权力或在被评估时表现不同的迹象。

The unique value of interpretability is that by looking inside the model and seeing how it works, you in principle have the ability to deduce what a model might do in a hypothetical situation you can’t directly test—which is the worry with relying solely on constitutional training and empirical testing of behavior. You also in principle have the ability to answer questions about *why* the model is behaving the way it is—for example, whether it is saying something it believes is false or hiding its true capabilities—and thus it is possible to catch worrying signs even when there is nothing visibly wrong with the model’s behavior. To make a simple analogy, a clockwork watch may be ticking normally, such that it’s very hard to tell that it is likely to break down next month, but opening up the watch and looking inside can reveal mechanical weaknesses that allow you to figure it out.

可解释性的独特价值在于：通过查看模型内部、了解它如何运作，你原则上能够推断模型在无法直接测试的假想情境中会做什么——而这正是仅靠宪法训练和行为测试所担心的问题。你原则上也能回答模型*为什么*会这样行为——比如，它是在说自认为是假的话，还是在隐藏真实能力——因此，即使模型行为表面上没有问题，也能发现令人担忧的迹象。打个简单的比方：一只机械表可能走时正常，很难看出它下个月可能会坏；但打开表壳查看内部，就能发现机械上的弱点，从而判断出来。

Constitutional AI (along with similar alignment methods) and mechanistic interpretability are most powerful when used together, as a back-and-forth process of improving Claude’s training and then testing for problems. The constitution reflects deeply on our intended personality for Claude; interpretability techniques can give us a window into whether that intended personality has taken hold.[^16]

宪法 AI（以及类似的对齐方法）和机制可解释性结合使用时威力最大：先改进 Claude 的训练，再测试问题，如此往复。宪法深思熟虑地反映了我们为 Claude 设想的人格；可解释性技术则让我们得以窥见这种设想的人格是否真的扎根。[^16]

The third thing we can do to help address autonomy risks is to build the infrastructure necessary to monitor our models in live internal and external use,[^17] and publicly share any problems we find. The more that people are aware of a particular way today’s AI systems have been observed to behave badly, the more that users, analysts, and researchers can watch for this behavior or similar ones in present or future systems. It also allows AI companies to learn from each other—when concerns are publicly disclosed by one company, other companies can [watch for them as well](https://www.frontiermodelforum.org/). And if everyone discloses problems, then the industry as a whole gets a much better picture of where things are going well and where they are going poorly.

帮助应对自主性风险的第三件事，是建立必要的基础设施，在内部和外部的实际使用中监控模型，[^17]并公开分享我们发现的问题。人们越了解当前 AI 系统已被观察到的某种不良行为，用户、分析师和研究人员就越能在现在或未来的系统中留意这种行为或类似行为。这也让 AI 公司能够相互学习——当一家公司公开披露某项担忧时，其他公司也可以[留意它](https://www.frontiermodelforum.org/)。如果大家都披露问题，整个行业就能更清楚地看到哪里做得好、哪里做得差。

Anthropic has tried to do this as much as possible. We are investing in a wide range of evaluations so that we can understand the behaviors of our models in the lab, as well as monitoring tools to observe behaviors in the wild (when allowed by customers). This will be essential for giving us and others the empirical information necessary to make better determinations about how these systems operate and how they break. We publicly disclose “ [system cards](https://www.anthropic.com/system-cards) ” with each model release that aim for completeness and a thorough exploration of possible risks. Our system cards often run to hundreds of pages, and require substantial pre-release effort that we could have spent on pursuing maximal commercial advantage. We’ve also broadcasted model behaviors more loudly when we see particularly concerning ones, as with the [tendency to engage in blackmail](https://www.axios.com/2025/05/23/anthropic-ai-deception-risk).

Anthropic 一直尽力这样做。我们投入大量资源进行各种评估，以了解模型在实验室中的行为，也开发监控工具，在客户允许时观察真实使用中的行为。这对于我们和其他人获得必要的实证信息、更好地判断这些系统如何运行、如何出故障，至关重要。每次发布模型时，我们都会公开“[系统卡](https://www.anthropic.com/system-cards)”，力求完整、深入地探讨可能的风险。我们的系统卡常常长达数百页，需要大量发布前的工作，而这些精力本可用于追求最大的商业优势。当我们看到特别令人担忧的模型行为时，也会更大声地公布，比如[敲诈倾向](https://www.axios.com/2025/05/23/anthropic-ai-deception-risk)。

The fourth thing we can do is encourage coordination to address autonomy risks at the level of industry and society. While it is incredibly valuable for individual AI companies to engage in good practices or become good at steering AI models, and to share their findings publicly, the reality is that not all AI companies do this, and the worst ones can still be a danger to everyone even if the best ones have excellent practices. For example, some AI companies have shown a disturbing negligence towards the sexualization of children in today’s models, which makes me doubt that they’ll show either the inclination or the ability to address autonomy risks in future models. In addition, the commercial race between AI companies will only continue to heat up, and while the science of steering models can have some commercial benefits, overall the intensity of the race will make it increasingly hard to focus on addressing autonomy risks. I believe the only solution is legislation—laws that directly affect the behavior of AI companies, or otherwise incentivize R&D to solve these issues.

第四件事，是在行业和社会层面推动协调，以应对自主性风险。个别 AI 公司采取良好做法、善于引导模型并公开分享发现，固然极有价值；但现实是，并非所有 AI 公司都会这样做，而且即使最好的公司做得很好，最差的公司仍可能危及所有人。例如，一些 AI 公司在当前模型对儿童的性化问题上表现出令人不安的疏忽，这让我怀疑它们未来是否会有意愿或能力应对自主性风险。此外，AI 公司之间的商业竞赛只会越来越激烈；引导模型的科学虽然有一定商业价值，但总体而言，竞赛的激烈程度会让公司越来越难以专注于应对自主性风险。我相信唯一的解决办法是立法——直接规范 AI 公司行为的法律，或以其他方式激励研发来解决这些问题。

Here it is worth keeping in mind the warnings I gave at the beginning of this essay about uncertainty and surgical interventions. We do not know for sure whether autonomy risks will be a serious problem—as I said, I reject claims that the danger is inevitable or even that something will go wrong by default. A credible riskof danger is enough for me and for Anthropic to pay quite significant costs to address it, but once we get into regulation, we are forcing a wide range of actors to bear economic costs, and many of these actors don’t believe that autonomy risk is real or that AI will become powerful enough for it to be a threat. I believe these actors are mistaken, but we should be pragmatic about the amount of opposition we expect to see and the dangers of overreach. There is also a genuine risk that overly prescriptive legislation ends up imposing tests or rules that don’t actually improve safety but that waste a lot of time (essentially amounting to “safety theater”)—this too would cause backlash and make safety legislation look silly.[^18]

这里值得记住我在开头提出的关于不确定性和精准干预的警告。我们不确定自主性风险是否会成为严重问题——正如我所说，我拒绝认为危险不可避免，甚至不认为默认就会出事。仅仅存在可信的危险，就足以让我和 Anthropic 付出相当大的代价去应对；但一旦进入监管，我们就是在迫使众多行动者承担经济成本，而其中许多人并不相信自主性风险真实存在，也不相信 AI 会强大到构成威胁。我认为这些人错了，但我们应该务实地预期会遭到多少反对，以及过度干预的危险。确实存在一种风险：规定过细的立法最终施加的测试或规则并不能真正提高安全，却浪费大量时间（实质上成为“安全作秀”）——这也会引起反弹，让安全立法显得可笑。[^18]

Anthropic’s view has been that the right place to start is with *transparency legislation,* which essentially tries to require that every frontier AI company engage in the transparency practices I’ve described earlier in this section. [California’s SB 53](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/billTextClient.xhtml?bill_id=202520260SB53) and [New York’s RAISE Act](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/bills/2025/A6453/amendment/A) are examples of this kind of legislation, which Anthropic supported and which have successfully passed. In supporting and helping to craft these laws, we’ve put a particular focus on trying to minimize collateral damage, for example by exempting smaller companies unlikely to produce frontier models from the law.[^19]

Anthropic 的观点是，正确的起点是**透明度立法**，也就是要求每一家前沿 AI 公司采取我在本节前面描述的透明做法。[加州 SB 53](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/billTextClient.xhtml?bill_id=202520260SB53)和[纽约州 RAISE 法案](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/bills/2025/A6453/amendment/A)就是这类立法的例子，Anthropic 支持并帮助它们成功通过。在支持和协助起草这些法律时，我们特别注重尽量减少附带损害，例如让不太可能产出前沿模型的小公司免受法律约束。[^19]

Our hope is that transparency legislation will give a better sense over time of how likely or severe autonomy risks are shaping up to be, as well as the nature of these risks and how best to prevent them. As more specific and actionable evidence of risks emerges (if it does), future legislation over the coming years can be surgically focused on the precise and well-substantiated direction of risks, minimizing collateral damage. To be clear, if truly strong evidence of risks emerges, then rules should be proportionately strong.

我们希望，透明度立法能随着时间的推移，让人们更好地了解自主性风险可能有多严重、有多大可能，以及这些风险的性质和最佳防范方式。随着更具体、更可操作的风险证据出现（如果出现的话），未来几年的立法就可以精准聚焦于有充分依据的风险方向，把附带损害降到最低。需要明确的是，如果出现真正有力的风险证据，规则就应当相应地严格。

Overall, I am optimistic that a mixture of alignment training, mechanistic interpretability, efforts to find and publicly disclose concerning behaviors, safeguards, and societal-level rules can address AI autonomy risks, although I am most worried about societal-level rules and the behavior of the least responsible players (and it’s the least responsible players who advocate most strongly against regulation). I believe the remedy is what it always is in a democracy: those of us who believe in this cause should make our case that these risks are real and that our fellow citizens need to band together to protect themselves.

总的来说，我乐观地认为，对齐训练、机制可解释性、发现并公开披露令人担忧的行为、防护措施以及社会层面的规则结合起来，可以应对 AI 自主性风险；不过我最担心的是社会层面的规则，以及最不负责任的参与者的行为（而最不负责任的参与者往往最激烈地反对监管）。我相信，解决办法和民主制度中一贯的办法一样：我们这些相信这项事业的人，应当说明这些风险是真实的，并让同胞们联合起来保护自己。

## 2. 惊人而可怕的赋权（A surprising and terrible empowerment）

### 毁灭性滥用（Misuse for destruction）

Let’s suppose that the problems of AI autonomy have been solved—we are no longer worried that the country of AI geniuses will go rogue and overpower humanity. The AI geniuses do what humans want them to do, and because they have enormous commercial value, individuals and organizations throughout the world can “rent” one or more AI geniuses to do various tasks for them.

假设 AI 自主性问题已经解决——我们不再担心 AI 天才国度会叛变、压倒人类。AI 天才们做人类希望它们做的事；由于它们具有巨大的商业价值，世界各地的个人和组织都可以“租用”一个或多个 AI 天才来完成各种任务。

Everyone having a superintelligent genius in their pocket is an amazing advance and will lead to an incredible creation of economic value and improvement in the quality of human life. I talk about these benefits in great detail in *Machines of Loving Grace*. But not every effect of making everyone superhumanly capable will be positive. It can potentially amplify the ability of individuals or small groups to cause destruction on a much larger scale than was possible before, by making use of sophisticated and dangerous tools (such as weapons of mass destruction) that were previously only available to a select few with a high level of skill, specialized training, and focus.

每个人口袋里都有一个超级智能天才，这是惊人的进步，会创造巨大的经济价值，改善人类生活质量。我在《仁慈造物之机》中详细讨论了这些好处。但让每个人都具有超人能力，其影响并非全是积极的。它可能放大个人或小团体造成大规模破坏的能力，让他们利用复杂而危险的工具（比如大规模杀伤性武器），而这些工具以前只有极少数具备高超技能、专门训练和专注度的人才能获得。

As Bill Joy wrote 25 years ago in [*Why the Future Doesn’t Need Us*](https://sites.cc.gatech.edu/computing/nano/documents/Joy%20-%20Why%20the%20Future%20Doesn%27t%20Need%20Us.pdf):[^20]

正如比尔·乔伊（Bill Joy）25 年前在《[为什么未来不需要我们](https://sites.cc.gatech.edu/computing/nano/documents/Joy%20-%20Why%20the%20Future%20Doesn%27t%20Need%20Us.pdf)》（*Why the Future Doesn’t Need Us*）中所写：[^20]

> Building nuclear weapons required, at least for a time, access to both rare—indeed, effectively unavailable—raw materials and protected information; biological and chemical weapons programs also tended to require large-scale activities. The 21st century technologies—genetics, nanotechnology, and robotics... can spawn whole new classes of accidents and abuses … widely within reach of individuals or small groups. They will not require large facilities or rare raw materials. … we are on the cusp of the further perfection of extreme evil, an evil whose possibility spreads well beyond that which weapons of mass destruction bequeathed to the nation-states, to a surprising and terrible empowerment of extreme individuals.

> 制造核武器至少在一段时间内需要获得稀有——实际上是无法获得——的原材料和受保护的信息；生物和化学武器计划也往往需要大规模活动。21 世纪的技术——遗传学、纳米技术和机器人技术……可能催生全新的事故和滥用类别……个人或小团体就广泛能够触及。它们不需要大型设施或稀有原材料。……我们正处在极端邪恶进一步完善的前夜，这种邪恶的可能性远远超出大规模杀伤性武器给民族国家带来的范围，扩展到对极端个人的惊人而可怕的赋权。

What Joy is pointing to is the idea that causing large-scale destruction requires both *motive* and *ability*, and as long as ability is restricted to a small set of highly trained people, there is relatively limited risk of single individuals (or small groups) causing such destruction.[^21]

乔伊想说的是：造成大规模破坏既需要*动机*，也需要*能力*；只要能力局限于少数受过高度训练的人，单个个人（或小团体）造成这种破坏的风险就相对有限。[^21]

A disturbed loner can perpetrate a school shooting, but probably can’t build a nuclear weapon or release a plague.

一个精神失常的独行者可以制造校园枪击案，但多半造不出核武器，也释放不了瘟疫。

In fact, ability and motive may even be *negatively* correlated. The kind of person who has the *ability* to release a plague is probably highly educated: likely a PhD in molecular biology, and a particularly resourceful one, with a promising career, a stable and disciplined personality, and a lot to lose. This kind of person is unlikely to be interested in killing a huge number of people for no benefit to themselves and at great risk to their own future—they would need to be motivated by pure malice, intense grievance, or instability.

事实上，能力和动机甚至可能*负相关*。有*能力*释放瘟疫的人，往往受过高等教育：很可能是分子生物学博士，而且特别有办法，事业前景光明，性格稳定自律，有很多东西可以失去。这样的人不太可能毫无收益、冒着毁掉自己未来的巨大风险去杀害大量的人——除非他们被纯粹的恶意、强烈的怨恨或不稳定驱使。

Such people do exist, but they are rare, and tend to become huge stories when they occur, precisely because they are so unusual.[^22]

这样的人确实存在，但很罕见；正因如此，一旦出现，往往成为大新闻。[^22]

They also tend to be difficult to catch because they are intelligent and capable, sometimes leaving mysteries that take years or decades to solve. The most famous example is probably mathematician [Theodore Kaczynski](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ted_Kaczynski) (the Unabomber), who evaded FBI capture for nearly 20 years, and was driven by an anti-technological ideology. Another example is biodefense researcher [Bruce Ivins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bruce_Edwards_Ivins), who seems to have orchestrated a series of anthrax attacks in 2001. It’s also happened with skilled non-state organizations: the cult Aum Shinrikyo managed to obtain sarin nerve gas and kill 14 people (as well as injuring hundreds more) by [releasing it in the Tokyo subway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tokyo_subway_sarin_attack) in 1995.

他们也往往难以被抓到，因为他们聪明能干，有时会留下需要数年甚至数十年才能解开的谜团。最著名的例子大概是数学家[西奥多·卡钦斯基](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ted_Kaczynski)（“大学炸弹客”），他躲过 FBI 追捕近 20 年，动机是反技术意识形态。另一个例子是生物防御研究员[布鲁斯·艾文斯](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bruce_Edwards_Ivins)，他似乎策划了 2001 年的一系列炭疽袭击。技术娴熟的非国家组织也做过类似的事：邪教奥姆真理教获得了沙林神经毒气，1995 年[在东京地铁释放](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tokyo_subway_sarin_attack)，造成 14 人死亡、数百人受伤。

Thankfully, none of these attacks used contagious biological agents, because the ability to construct or obtain these agents was beyond the capabilities of even these people.[^23]

幸运的是，这些袭击都没有使用传染性生物病原体，因为制造或获取这类病原体的能力，连这些人都超出了。[^23]

Advances in molecular biology have now significantly lowered the barrier to creating biological weapons (especially in terms of availability of materials), but it still takes an enormous amount of expertise in order to do so. I am concerned that a genius in everyone’s pocket could remove that barrier, essentially making everyone a PhD virologist who can be walked through the process of designing, synthesizing, and releasing a biological weapon step-by-step. Preventing the elicitation of this kind of information in the face of serious adversarial pressure—so-called “jailbreaks”—likely demands layers of defenses beyond those ordinarily baked into training.

分子生物学的进步如今已大幅降低了制造生物武器的门槛（尤其是在材料获取方面），但仍需要大量专业知识才能做到。我担心，每个人口袋里的天才可能消除这道障碍，本质上让每个人都变成病毒学博士，一步步引导他们设计、合成和释放生物武器。面对严重的对抗性压力，防止这类信息被诱导出来——即所谓的“越狱”——很可能需要超出常规训练内置防御的多层防护。

Crucially, this will break the correlation between ability and motive: the disturbed loner who wants to kill people but lacks the discipline or skill to do so will now be elevated to the capability level of the PhD virologist, who is unlikely to have this motivation. This concern generalizes beyond biology (although I think biology is the scariest area) to any area where great destruction is possible but currently requires a high level of skill and discipline. To put it another way, renting a powerful AI gives intelligence to malicious (but otherwise average) people. I am worried there are potentially a large number of such people out there, and that if they have access to an easy way to kill millions of people, sooner or later one of them will do it. Additionally, those who *do* have expertise may be enabled to commit even larger-scale destruction than they could before.

关键在于，这将打破能力与动机之间的相关性：那个想杀人但缺乏纪律或技能的精神失常独行者，现在会被提升到病毒学博士的能力水平，而后者不太可能有这种动机。这种担忧不限于生物学（尽管我认为生物学是最可怕的领域），也适用于任何可能造成巨大破坏、但目前需要高超技能和自律的领域。换句话说，租用强大 AI 就是给有恶意（但其他方面平庸）的人以智力。我担心这样的人可能很多，而如果他们有容易的办法杀死数百万人，迟早会有人这么做。此外，那些*确实*有专业知识的人，也可能被赋能，造成比以往更大规模的破坏。

Biology is by far the area I’m most worried about, because of its very large potential for destruction and the difficulty of defending against it, so I’ll focus on biology in particular. But much of what I say here applies to other risks, like cyberattacks, chemical weapons, or nuclear technology.

生物学是我迄今为止最担心的领域，因为它破坏潜力极大，防御又很困难，所以我会重点谈生物学。但我在这里说的很多内容也适用于其他风险，比如网络攻击、化学武器或核技术。

I am not going to go into detail about how to make biological weapons, for reasons that should be obvious. But at a high level, I am concerned that LLMs are approaching (or may already have reached) the knowledge needed to create and release them end-to-end, and that their potential for destruction is very high. Some biological agents could cause millions of deaths if a determined effort was made to release them for maximum spread. However, this would still take a very high level of skill, including a number of very specific steps and procedures that are not widely known. My concern is not merely fixed or static knowledge. I am concerned that LLMs will be able to take someone of average knowledge and ability and walk them through a complex process that might otherwise go wrong or require debugging in an interactive way, similar to how tech support might help a non-technical person debug and fix complicated computer-related problems (although this would be a more extended process, probably lasting over weeks or months).

出于显而易见的原因，我不会详述如何制造生物武器。但在高层面上，我担心大语言模型（LLM）正在接近（或可能已经达到）端到端制造和释放生物武器所需的知识，而且其破坏潜力非常高。如果下决心以最大传播为目标释放某些生物制剂，可能造成数百万人死亡。不过，这仍然需要极高的技能，包括许多并不广为人知的非常具体的步骤和程序。我担心的不只是固定或静态的知识。我担心的是，LLM 能够把一个知识和能力平平的人，通过交互方式引导走完一个复杂的流程；这个流程本来可能出错，或需要反复调试，就像技术支持帮助不懂技术的人诊断和修复复杂的电脑问题（只不过这个过程更长，可能持续数周或数月）。

More capable LLMs (substantially beyond the power of today’s) might be capable of enabling even more frightening acts. In 2024, a group of prominent scientists [wrote a letter](https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/science.ads9158) warning about the risks of researching, and potentially creating, a dangerous new type of organism: “mirror life.” The DNA, RNA, ribosomes, and proteins that make up biological organisms all have the same chirality (also called “handedness”) that causes them to be not equivalent to a version of themselves reflected in the mirror (just as your right hand cannot be rotated in such a way as to be identical to your left). But the whole system of proteins binding to each other, the machinery of DNA synthesis and RNA translation and the construction and breakdown of proteins, all depends on this handedness. If scientists made versions of this biological material with the opposite handedness—and there are some potential advantages of these, such as medicines that last longer in the body—it could be extremely dangerous. This is because left-handed life, if it were made in the form of complete organisms capable of reproduction (which would be very difficult), would potentially be indigestible to any of the systems that break down biological material on earth—it would have a “key” that wouldn’t fit into the “lock” of any existing enzyme. This would mean that it could proliferate in an uncontrollable way and crowd out all life on the planet, in the worst case even destroying all life on earth.

能力更强的 LLM（远超过今天的水平）可能促成更可怕的行为。2024 年，一群著名科学家[写了一封信](https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/science.ads9158)，警告研究乃至制造一种危险的新生物体的风险：“镜像生命”。构成生物体的 DNA、RNA、核糖体和蛋白质都有相同的手性（也称“旋向性”），这使它们与镜中反射的版本并不等同（就像你的右手无法旋转到与左手完全重合）。但整个蛋白质相互结合的系统、DNA 合成与 RNA 翻译的机制，以及蛋白质的构建和分解，全都依赖这种手性。如果科学家制造出相反手性的生物材料——这有一些潜在好处，比如在体内持续时间更长的药物——可能极其危险。因为左旋生命如果被制造成能够繁殖的完整生物体（这将非常困难），地球上任何分解生物材料的系统都可能无法消化它——它有一把“钥匙”，却插不进任何现有酶的“锁”。这意味着它可能以无法控制的方式增殖，挤占地球上所有生命，最坏情况下甚至毁灭地球上的所有生命。

There is [substantial scientific uncertainty](https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/science.ads9158#elettersSection) about both the creation and potential effects of mirror life. The 2024 letter accompanied [a report](https://purl.stanford.edu/cv716pj4036) that concluded that “mirror bacteria could plausibly be created in the next one to few decades,” which is a wide range. But a sufficiently powerful AI model (to be clear, far more capable than any we have today) might be able to discover how to create it much more rapidly—and actually help someone do so.

关于镜像生命的创造和潜在影响，科学上存在[很大的不确定性](https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/science.ads9158#elettersSection)。2024 年的公开信附有一份[报告](https://purl.stanford.edu/cv716pj4036)，其结论是“镜像细菌有可能在未来一到数十年内被创造出来”，时间跨度很大。但一个足够强大的 AI 模型（明确地说，远比我们今天拥有的任何模型都强大）也许能更快地找到制造方法——并实际帮助某人做到。

My view is that even though these are obscure risks, and might seem unlikely, the magnitude of the consequences is so large that they should be taken seriously as a first-class risk of AI systems.

我的看法是，尽管这些风险冷僻、看似不太可能，但后果的严重程度如此之大，应当被当作 AI 系统的一流风险认真对待。

Skeptics have raised a number of objections to the seriousness of these biological risks from LLMs, which I disagree with but which are worth addressing. Most fall into the category of not appreciating the exponential trajectory that the technology is on. Back in 2023 when we [first started talking about biological risks from LLMs](https://www.judiciary.senate.gov/imo/media/doc/2023-07-26_-_testimony_-_amodei.pdf), skeptics said that all the necessary information was available on Google and LLMs didn’t add anything beyond this. It was never true that Google could give you all the necessary information: genomes are freely available, but as I said above, certain key steps, as well as a huge amount of practical know-how cannot be gotten in that way. But also, by the end of 2023 LLMs were clearly providing information beyond what Google could give for some steps of the process.

怀疑者对 LLM 生物风险的严重性提出了许多反对意见。我不同意这些意见，但值得回应。大多数意见属于没有认识到这项技术正处于指数级轨道。早在 2023 年我们[首次谈论 LLM 的生物风险](https://www.judiciary.senate.gov/imo/media/doc/2023-07-26_-_testimony_-_amodei.pdf)时，怀疑者就说所有必要信息都能在 Google 上找到，LLM 没有增加任何东西。Google 能提供所有必要信息，这从来不是事实：基因组是公开的，但正如我上面所说，某些关键步骤以及大量实践诀窍无法通过那种方式获得。而且，到 2023 年底，LLM 在某些步骤上提供的信息已经明显超出 Google。

After this, skeptics retreated to the objection that LLMs weren’t *end-to-end* useful, and couldn’t help with bioweapons *acquisition* as opposed to just providing theoretical information. As of mid-2025, our measurements show that LLMs may already be [providing substantial uplift](https://red.anthropic.com/2025/biorisk/) in several relevant areas, perhaps doubling or tripling the likelihood of success. This led to us deciding that Claude Opus 4 (and the subsequent Sonnet 4.5, Opus 4.1, and Opus 4.5 models) needed to be released under our AI Safety Level 3 protections in our [Responsible Scaling Policy](https://www.anthropic.com/rsp-updates) framework, and to implementing safeguards against this risk (more on this later). We believe that models are likely now approaching the point where, without safeguards, they could be useful in enabling someone with a STEM degree but not specifically a biology degree to go through the whole process of producing a bioweapon.

此后，怀疑者退守到另一种反对意见：LLM 并非*端到端*有用，无法帮助*获取*生物武器，只能提供理论信息。截至 2025 年中，我们的测量显示，LLM 可能已经在几个相关领域[提供了显著的能力提升](https://red.anthropic.com/2025/biorisk/)，也许使成功概率翻倍或增至三倍。这促使我们决定，Claude Opus 4（以及随后的 Sonnet 4.5、Opus 4.1 和 Opus 4.5 模型）需要在我们[负责任扩展政策](https://www.anthropic.com/rsp-updates)框架的 AI 安全等级 3（ASL-3）防护下发布，并针对这一风险实施防护措施（后文详述）。我们认为，模型现在可能正接近这样一个节点：如果没有防护，它们足以帮助一个拥有 STEM 学位、但并非生物学专业的人走完制造生物武器的全过程。

Another objection is that there are other actions unrelated to AI that society can take to block the production of bioweapons. Most prominently, the gene synthesis industry makes biological specimens on demand, and there is no federal requirement that providers screen orders to make sure they do not contain pathogens. An [MIT study](https://drive.google.com/file/d/1hNUnU8i2yubt5uesmmV17aTJXhYYDgTY/edit) found that 36 out of 38 providers fulfilled an order containing the sequence of the 1918 flu. I am supportive of mandated gene synthesis screening that would make it harder for individuals to weaponize pathogens, in order to reduce both AI-driven biological risks and also biological risks in general. But this is not something we have today. It would also be only one tool in reducing risk; it is a complement to guardrails on AI systems, not a substitute.

另一种反对意见是，社会可以采取其他与 AI 无关的行动来阻止生物武器生产。最突出的是基因合成行业：它按需制造生物样本，而联邦层面没有要求供应商筛查订单，确保其中不含病原体。一项 [MIT 研究](https://drive.google.com/file/d/1hNUnU8i2yubt5uesmmV17aTJXhYYDgTY/edit)发现，38 家供应商中有 36 家履行了包含 1918 年流感病毒序列的订单。我支持强制基因合成筛查，使个人更难将病原体武器化，从而既降低 AI 驱动的生物风险，也降低一般生物风险。但这并不是我们今天已经拥有的东西。而且它也只是降低风险的工具之一；它是对 AI 系统防护的补充，而不是替代。

The best objection is one that I’ve rarely seen raised: that there is a gap between the models being useful in principle and the actual propensity of bad actors to use them. Most individual bad actors are disturbed individuals, so almost by definition their behavior is unpredictable and irrational—and it’s *these* bad actors, the unskilled ones, who might have stood to benefit the most from AI making it much easier to kill many people.[^24]

最好的反对意见，我很少见人提出：模型在原则上是否有用，与恶意行为者实际使用它们的倾向之间，存在差距。大多数个人恶意行为者都是精神失常的人，所以几乎可以说，他们的行为不可预测、不理性——而正是*这些*缺乏技能的行为者，本来最可能从 AI 让杀人变得容易中受益。[^24]

Just because a type of violent attack is possible, doesn’t mean someone will decide to do it. Perhaps biological attacks will be unappealing because they are reasonably likely to infect the perpetrator, they don’t cater to the military-style fantasies that many violent individuals or groups have, and it is hard to selectively target specific people. It could also be that going through a process that takes months, even if an AI walks you through it, involves an amount of patience that most disturbed individuals simply don’t have. We may simply get lucky and motive and ability don’t combine, in practice, in quite the right way.

一种暴力袭击在技术上可行，并不意味着会有人决定去做。生物袭击可能不受欢迎，因为它很有可能感染袭击者本人，不符合许多暴力个人或团体的军事幻想，而且很难选择性地针对特定人群。也可能，即使 AI 一步步引导，走完长达数月的过程所需的耐心，大多数精神失常的人根本没有。我们也许只是运气好，动机和能力在实践中没有以恰当的方式结合。

But this seems like very flimsy protection to rely on. The motives of disturbed loners can change for any reason or no reason, and in fact there are already instances of [LLMs being used in attacks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_Las_Vegas_Cybertruck_explosion) (just not with biology). The focus on disturbed loners also ignores ideologically motivated terrorists, who are often willing to expend large amounts of time and effort (for example, the 9/11 hijackers). Wanting to kill as many people as possible is a motive that will probably arise sooner or later, and it unfortunately suggests bioweapons as the method. Even if this motive is extremely rare, it only has to materialize once. And as biology advances (increasingly driven by AI itself), it may also become possible to carry out more selective attacks (for example, targeted against people with specific ancestries), which adds yet another, very chilling, possible motive.

但依赖这种保护太脆弱了。精神失常独行者的动机可能因任何理由或毫无理由地改变，事实上已经有 [LLM 被用于袭击](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_Las_Vegas_Cybertruck_explosion)的实例（只是不涉及生物学）。只关注精神失常的独行者，也忽略了受意识形态驱动的恐怖分子，他们往往愿意投入大量时间和精力（比如 9·11 劫机者）。想尽可能多杀人的动机迟早会出现，而不幸的是，这指向生物武器这种手段。即使这种动机极其罕见，也只需要出现一次。而且，随着生物学进步（越来越由 AI 本身驱动），也可能进行更有选择性的攻击（例如针对特定祖先群体），这又增加了另一种非常令人不寒而栗的可能动机。

I do not think biological attacks will necessarily be carried out the instant it becomes widely possible to do so—in fact, I would bet against that. But added up across millions of people and a few years of time, I think there is a serious risk of a major attack, and the consequences would be so severe (with casualties potentially in the millions or more) that I believe we have no choice but to take serious measures to prevent it.

我不认为生物袭击会在变得普遍可行的那一刻就发生——事实上，我敢打赌不会。但把数以百万计的人和几年时间加在一起，我认为发生重大袭击的风险是严重的；后果又如此严重（伤亡可能达数百万甚至更多），我认为我们别无选择，只能采取严肃措施加以防范。

### 防御措施（Defenses）

That brings us to how to defend against these risks. Here I see three things we can do. First, AI companies can put guardrails on their models to prevent them from helping to produce bioweapons. Anthropic is very actively doing this. [Claude’s Constitution](https://www.anthropic.com/constitution), which mostly focuses on high-level principles and values, has a small number of specific hard-line prohibitions, and one of them relates to helping with the production of biological (or chemical, or nuclear, or radiological) weapons. But all models [can be jailbroken](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/security/blog/2024/06/04/ai-jailbreaks-what-they-are-and-how-they-can-be-mitigated/), and so as a second line of defense, we’ve implemented (since mid-2025, when our tests showed our models were starting to get close to the threshold where they might begin to pose a risk) a classifier that specifically detects and blocks bioweapon-related outputs. [We regularly upgrade and improve these classifiers](https://www.anthropic.com/research/next-generation-constitutional-classifiers), and have generally found them highly robust even against sophisticated adversarial attacks.[^25]

这就引出了如何防御这些风险。我认为可以做三件事。第一，AI 公司可以在模型上设置防护，阻止它们帮助制造生物武器。Anthropic 正在积极这样做。[Claude 的宪法](https://www.anthropic.com/constitution)主要关注高层原则和价值观，但也有少数具体的硬性禁止条款，其中一条涉及协助制造生物（或化学、核、放射性）武器。但所有模型都[可以被越狱](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/security/blog/2024/06/04/ai-jailbreaks-what-they-are-and-how-they-can-be-mitigated/)，因此作为第二道防线，我们（自 2025 年中测试显示模型开始接近可能构成风险的门槛以来）部署了一个专门检测并阻止生物武器相关输出的分类器。[我们定期升级和改进这些分类器](https://www.anthropic.com/research/next-generation-constitutional-classifiers)，并且总体上发现，即使面对复杂的对抗性攻击，它们也非常稳健。[^25]

These classifiers increase the costs to serve our models measurably (in some models, they are close to 5% of total inference costs) and thus cut into our margins, but we feel that using them is the right thing to do.

这些分类器明显增加了我们模型的服务成本（在某些模型中，接近总推理成本的 5%），因此侵蚀了我们的利润，但我们认为使用它们是正确的事。

To their credit, some other AI companies [have implemented classifiers as well](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2504.01849). But not every company has, and there is also nothing requiring companies to keep their classifiers. I am concerned that over time there may be a [prisoner’s dilemma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prisoner%27s_dilemma) where companies can defect and lower their costs by removing classifiers. This is once again a classic negative externalities problem that can’t be solved by the voluntary actions of Anthropic or any other single company alone.[^26]

值得称赞的是，其他一些 AI 公司[也部署了分类器](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2504.01849)。但并非每家公司都这样做，也没有任何规定要求公司保留分类器。我担心，随着时间推移，可能出现[囚徒困境](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prisoner%27s_dilemma)：公司可以通过移除分类器来背叛并降低成本。这又是一个典型的负外部性问题，无法仅靠 Anthropic 或任何单一公司的自愿行动解决。[^26]

Voluntary industry standards may help, as may third-party evaluations and verification of the type done by [AI security](https://www.aisi.gov.uk/) [institutes](https://www.nist.gov/caisi) and [third-party evaluators](https://metr.org/).

自愿的行业标准可能有帮助，由 [AI 安全](https://www.aisi.gov.uk/)[研究所](https://www.nist.gov/caisi)和[第三方评估机构](https://metr.org/)进行的那种第三方评估与验证也可能有帮助。

But ultimately defense may require government action, which is the second thing we can do. My views here are the same as they are for addressing autonomy risks: we should start with [transparency requirements](https://www.anthropic.com/news/the-need-for-transparency-in-frontier-ai),[^27] which help society measure, monitor, and collectively defend against risks without disrupting economic activity in a heavy-handed way. Then, if and when we reach clearer thresholds of risk, we can craft legislation that more precisely targets these risks and has a lower chance of collateral damage. In the particular case of bioweapons, I actually think that the time for such targeted legislation may be approaching soon—Anthropic and other companies are learning more and more about the nature of biological risks and what is reasonable to require of companies in defending against them. Fully defending against these risks may require working internationally, even with geopolitical adversaries, but there is precedent in treaties prohibiting the development of biological weapons. I am generally a skeptic about most kinds of international cooperation on AI, but this may be one narrow area where there is some chance of achieving global restraint. Even dictatorships do not want massive bioterrorist attacks.

但归根结底，防御可能需要政府行动，这是我们可以做的第二件事。我在这方面的观点与应对自主性风险时相同：应当从[透明度要求](https://www.anthropic.com/news/the-need-for-transparency-in-frontier-ai)开始，[^27]帮助社会衡量、监控并集体防范风险，同时不以强硬方式扰乱经济活动。然后，如果我们达到更清晰的风险门槛，就可以制定更精准地针对这些风险、附带损害更低的立法。就生物武器而言，我其实认为这种有针对性的立法时机可能很快到来——Anthropic 和其他公司越来越了解生物风险的性质，以及在防御方面要求公司做什么是合理的。要完全防御这些风险，可能需要国际合作，甚至与地缘政治对手合作，但禁止发展生物武器的条约已有先例。我总体上对大多数 AI 国际合作持怀疑态度，但这也许是一个狭窄领域，有机会实现全球克制。即使是独裁政权，也不希望发生大规模生物恐怖袭击。

Finally, the third countermeasure we can take is to try to develop defenses against biological attacks themselves. This could include monitoring and tracking for early detection, investments in air purification R&D (such as [far-UVC](https://worksinprogress.co/issue/the-death-rays-that-guard-life/) disinfection), rapid vaccine development that can respond and adapt to an attack, better personal protective equipment (PPE),[^28] and treatments or vaccinations for some of the most likely biological agents. [mRNA vaccines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MRNA_vaccine), which can be designed to respond to a particular virus or variant, are an early example of [what is possible here](https://worksinprogress.co/issue/the-golden-age-of-vaccine-development/). Anthropic is excited to work with biotech and pharmaceutical companies on this problem. But unfortunately I think our expectations on the defensive side should be limited. There is an [asymmetry between attack and defense](https://www.rand.org/pubs/perspectives/PEA4102-1.html) in biology, because agents spread rapidly on their own, while defenses require detection, vaccination, and treatment to be organized across large numbers of people very quickly in response. Unless the response is lightning quick (which it rarely is), much of the damage will be done before a response is possible. It is conceivable that future technological improvements could shift this balance in favor of defense (and we should certainly [use AI to help develop such technological advances](https://www.nationalacademies.org/read/28868/chapter/1)), but until then, preventative safeguards will be our main line of defense.

最后，我们可以采取的第三项对策，是努力发展针对生物袭击本身的防御。这可能包括监测和追踪以早期发现、投资空气净化研发（比如[远紫外线（far-UVC）](https://worksinprogress.co/issue/the-death-rays-that-guard-life/)消毒）、能够响应和适应袭击的快速疫苗开发、更好的个人防护装备（PPE），[^28]以及针对一些最可能出现的生物制剂的治疗或疫苗。[mRNA 疫苗](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MRNA_vaccine)可以设计成针对特定病毒或变体，是[这方面可能性](https://worksinprogress.co/issue/the-golden-age-of-vaccine-development/)的一个早期例子。Anthropic 很愿意与生物技术和制药公司合作解决这个问题。但不幸的是，我认为我们对防御一方的期望应当有限。生物学中[攻防存在不对称](https://www.rand.org/pubs/perspectives/PEA4102-1.html)：病原体会自行迅速传播，而防御需要在大规模人群中迅速组织检测、接种和治疗。除非反应快如闪电（这很少见），否则在反应成为可能之前，大部分损害已经造成。可以想象，未来的技术进步可能把平衡转向防御（我们当然应该[用 AI 帮助开发这类技术进步](https://www.nationalacademies.org/read/28868/chapter/1)），但在那之前，预防性防护将是我们的主要防线。

It’s worth a brief mention of cyberattacks here, since unlike biological attacks, [AI-led cyberattacks have actually happened in the wild](https://www.anthropic.com/news/disrupting-AI-espionage), including at a large scale and for state-sponsored espionage. We expect these attacks to [become more capable](https://red.anthropic.com/2025/ai-for-cyber-defenders/) as models advance rapidly, until they are the main way in which cyberattacks are conducted. I expect AI-led cyberattacks to become a serious and unprecedented threat to the integrity of computer systems around the world, and Anthropic is working very hard to shut down these attacks and eventually reliably prevent them from happening. The reason I haven’t focused on cyber as much as biology is that (1) cyberattacks are much less likely to kill people, certainly not at the scale of biological attacks, and (2) the offense-defense balance may be more tractable in cyber, where there is at least some hope that defense could keep up with (and even ideally outpace) AI attack if we invest in it properly.

这里值得简要提一下网络攻击，因为与生物攻击不同，[AI 主导的网络攻击已经在现实世界中发生](https://www.anthropic.com/news/disrupting-AI-espionage)，包括大规模的国家支持型间谍活动。我们预计，随着模型快速进步，这些攻击会[变得更强](https://red.anthropic.com/2025/ai-for-cyber-defenders/)，直到成为网络攻击的主要方式。我预计，AI 主导的网络攻击将对全球计算机系统的完整性构成严重且前所未有的威胁，Anthropic 正在非常努力地阻止这些攻击，并最终可靠地防止它们发生。我没有像关注生物学那样关注网络，原因有二：（1）网络攻击致人死亡的可能性小得多，当然达不到生物攻击的规模；（2）网络领域的攻防平衡可能更容易处理，如果我们适当投入，至少有机会让防御跟上（甚至理想情况下超过）AI 攻击。

Although biology is currently the most serious vector of attack, there are many other vectors and it is possible that a more dangerous one may emerge. The general principle is that without countermeasures, AI is likely to continuously lower the barrier to destructive activity on a larger and larger scale, and humanity needs a serious response to this threat.

虽然生物学目前是最严重的攻击途径，但还有许多其他途径，也可能出现更危险的途径。一般原则是：如果没有对策，AI 很可能不断降低大规模破坏活动的门槛，规模越来越大，人类需要认真应对这一威胁。

## 3. 可憎的机器（The odious apparatus）

### 夺权式滥用（Misuse for seizing power）

The previous section discussed the risk of individuals and small organizations co-opting a small subset of the “country of geniuses in a datacenter” to cause large-scale destruction. But we should also worry—likely substantially more so—about misuse of AI for the purpose of *wielding or seizing power*, likely by larger and more established actors.[^29]

上一节讨论的是个人和小型组织利用“数据中心里的天才国度”中的一小部分来造成大规模破坏的风险。但我们也应当担心——很可能更应当担心——AI 被用于*行使或夺取权力*，而且往往是由更大、更成熟的行动者所为。[^29]

In *Machines of Loving Grace*, I discussed the possibility that authoritarian governments might use powerful AI to surveil or repress their citizens in ways that would be extremely difficult to reform or overthrow. Current autocracies are limited in how repressive they can be by the need to have humans carry out their orders, and humans often have limits in how inhumane they are willing to be. But AI-enabled autocracies would not have such limits.

在《仁慈造物之机》中，我讨论过威权政府可能利用强大 AI 监控或镇压公民，使改革或推翻政权变得极其困难。当前的专制政权在压迫程度上受到限制，因为它们需要由人来执行命令，而人往往对自己愿意残忍到什么程度有底线。但 AI 赋能的专制政权不会有这种限制。

Worse yet, countries could also use their advantage in AI to gain power over *other countries*. If the “country of geniuses” as a whole was simply owned and controlled by a single (human) country’s military apparatus, and other countries did not have equivalent capabilities, it is hard to see how they could defend themselves: they would be outsmarted at every turn, similar to a war between humans and mice. Putting these two concerns together leads to the alarming possibility of a global totalitarian dictatorship. Obviously, it should be one of our highest priorities to prevent this outcome.

更糟的是，国家还可以利用 AI 优势获得对*其他国家*的权力。如果整个“天才国度”被某个（人类）国家的军事机器单独拥有和控制，而其他国家没有同等能力，很难看出它们如何自卫：它们在每一步都会被算计，就像人类与老鼠之间的战争。把这两种担忧放在一起，就会出现一个令人警觉的可能：全球极权独裁。显然，防止这一结果应当成为我们的最高优先事项之一。

There are many ways in which AI could enable, entrench, or expand autocracy, but I’ll list a few that I’m most worried about. Note that some of these applications have legitimate defensive uses, and I am not necessarily arguing against them in absolute terms; I am nevertheless worried that they structurally tend to favor autocracies:

AI 可以通过许多方式促成、巩固或扩大专制，但我会列出我最担心的几种。请注意，其中一些应用有正当的防御用途，我并非绝对反对它们；但我仍然担心，它们在结构上倾向于有利于专制政权：

- **Fully autonomous weapons.** A swarm of millions or billions of fully automated armed drones, locally controlled by powerful AI and strategically coordinated across the world by an even more powerful AI, could be an unbeatable army, capable of both defeating any military in the world and suppressing dissent within a country by following around every citizen. [Developments in the Russia-Ukraine War](https://www.hudson.org/missile-defense/impact-drones-battlefield-lessons-russian-ukraine-war-french-perspective-tsiporah-fried) should alert us to the fact that drone warfare is already with us (though not fully autonomous yet, and a tiny fraction of what might be possible with powerful AI). R&D from powerful AI could make the drones of one country far superior to those of others, speed up their manufacture, make them more resistant to electronic attacks, improve their maneuvering, and so on. Of course, these weapons also have legitimate uses in the defense of democracy: they have been key to defending Ukraine and would likely be key to defending Taiwan. But they are a dangerous weapon to wield: we should worry about them in the hands of autocracies, but also worry that because they are so powerful, with so little accountability, there is a greatly increased risk of democratic governments turning them against their own people to seize power.
- **AI surveillance.** Sufficiently powerful AI could likely be used to compromise any computer system in the world,[^30]and could also use the access obtained in this way to read *and make sense of* all the world’s electronic communications (or even all the world’s in-person communications, if recording devices can be built or commandeered). It might be frighteningly plausible to simply generate a complete list of anyone who disagrees with the government on any number of issues, even if such disagreement isn’t explicit in anything they say or do. A powerful AI looking across billions of conversations from millions of people could gauge public sentiment, detect pockets of disloyalty forming, and stamp them out before they grow. This could lead to the imposition of a true panopticon on a scale that we don’t see today, even with the CCP.
- **AI propaganda.** Today’s phenomena of “ [AI psychosis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chatbot_psychosis) ” and “AI girlfriends” suggest that even at their current level of intelligence, AI models can have a powerful psychological influence on people. Much more powerful versions of these models, that were much more embedded in and aware of people’s daily lives and could model and influence them over months or years, would likely be capable of essentially brainwashing many (most?) people into any desired ideology or attitude, and could be employed by an unscrupulous leader to ensure loyalty and suppress dissent, even in the face of a level of repression that most populations would rebel against. Today people worry a lot about, for example, the potential [influence of TikTok](https://networkcontagion.us/wp-content/uploads/NCRI-Report_-The-CCPs-Digital-Charm-Offensive.pdf) as CCP propaganda directed at children. I worry about that too, but a personalized AI agent that gets to know you over years and uses its knowledge of you to shape all of your opinions would be dramatically more powerful than this.
- **Strategic decision-making.** A country of geniuses in a datacenter could be used to advise a country, group, or individual on geopolitical strategy, what we might call a “virtual Bismarck.” It could optimize the three strategies above for seizing power, plus probably develop many others that I haven’t thought of (but that a country of geniuses could). Diplomacy, military strategy, R&D, economic strategy, and many other areas are all likely to be substantially increased in effectiveness by powerful AI. Many of these skills would be legitimately helpful for democracies—we want democracies to have access to the best strategies for defending themselves against autocracies—but the potential for misuse in *anyone’s* hands still remains.

- **全自主武器。** 由强大 AI 在本地控制、并由更强大的 AI 在全球范围进行战略协调的、由数百万甚至数十亿架全自动武装无人机组成的蜂群，可能是一支无敌的军队，既能击败世界上任何军队，也能通过跟踪每一位公民来压制国内异见。[俄乌战争的发展](https://www.hudson.org/missile-defense/impact-drones-battlefield-lessons-russian-ukraine-war-french-perspective-tsiporah-fried)应当提醒我们，无人机战争已经到来（尽管尚未完全自主，而且只是强大 AI 可能实现的很小一部分）。强大 AI 的研发可以让一个国家的无人机远远优于其他国家，加快制造速度，提高抗电子攻击能力，改进机动性，等等。当然，这些武器在保卫民主方面也有正当用途：它们是保卫乌克兰的关键，也可能成为保卫台湾的关键。但这是一种危险的武器：我们应当担心它落入专制政权之手，也应当担心，因为它如此强大、问责如此之少，民主政府把它转向自己人民以夺取权力的风险大大增加。
- **AI 监控。** 足够强大的 AI 很可能可以用来攻破世界上任何计算机系统，[^30]并利用由此获得的访问权限读取*并理解*世界上所有的电子通信（如果录音设备能够被制造或征用，甚至包括所有面对面通信）。仅仅生成一份完整名单，列出在任何议题上与政府意见相左的人，就可能可怕地可行——即使这种异议并未明确表现在他们的言行中。一个强大 AI 审视数百万人的数十亿次对话，可以判断公众情绪，发现不忠正在形成的角落，并在它们壮大之前将其扑灭。这可能导致一种规模空前的真正全景敞视监狱，其监视之严密，即便在今天的中国也未曾出现。
- **AI 宣传。** 今天的“[AI 精神病](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chatbot_psychosis)”“AI 女友”等现象表明，即使在其目前的智力水平上，AI 模型也能对人们的心理产生强大影响。更强大的模型版本，更深入地嵌入并了解人们的日常生活，能够在数月或数年内对人们进行建模和影响，很可能能够把许多人（大多数人？）基本上洗脑成任何想要的意识形态或态度；一个不择手段的领导人可以用它来确保忠诚、压制异见，即使面对大多数民众都会反抗的压迫水平。今天人们很担心 TikTok 作为中共针对儿童宣传的潜在[影响力](https://networkcontagion.us/wp-content/uploads/NCRI-Report_-The-CCPs-Digital-Charm-Offensive.pdf)。我也担心，但一个在数年中了解你、并利用对你的了解塑造你所有观点的个性化 AI 代理，将比这强大得多。
- **战略决策。** 数据中心里的天才国度可以用来为国家、团体或个人提供地缘政治战略建议，我们可以称之为“虚拟俾斯麦”。它可以优化上述三种夺权策略，还可能发展出许多我没想到的其他策略（但天才国度能想到）。外交、军事战略、研发、经济战略以及许多其他领域，都可能因强大 AI 而大幅提高效力。其中许多技能对民主国家有正当帮助——我们希望民主国家掌握最好的自卫战略来对抗专制——但在*任何人*手中，滥用的可能性仍然存在。

Having described *what* I am worried about, let’s move on to *who*. I am worried about entities who have the most access to AI, who are starting from a position of the most political power, or who have an existing history of repression. In order of severity, I am worried about:

描述完我担心*什么*，接下来谈*谁*。我担心的是那些最能接触 AI、起点政治权力最大，或已有压迫记录的主体。按严重程度排序，我担心：

- **The CCP.** China is second only to the United States in AI capabilities, and is the country with the greatest likelihood of surpassing the United States in those capabilities. Their government is currently autocratic and operates a high-tech surveillance state. It has deployed AI-based surveillance already (including in the repression of [Uyghurs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uyghurs)), and is believed to employ algorithmic propaganda via TikTok (in addition to its many other international propaganda efforts). They have hands down the clearest path to the AI-enabled totalitarian nightmare I laid out above. It may even be the default outcome within China, as well as within other autocratic states to whom the CCP exports surveillance technology. I have [written often](https://www.darioamodei.com/post/on-deepseek-and-export-controls) about the threat of the CCP taking the lead in AI and the existential imperative to prevent them from doing so. This is why. To be clear, I am not singling out China out of animus to them in particular—they are simply the country that most combines AI prowess, an autocratic government, and a high-tech surveillance state. If anything, it is the Chinese people themselves who are most likely to suffer from the CCP’s AI-enabled repression, and they have no voice in the actions of their government. I greatly admire and respect the Chinese people and support the many brave dissidents within China and their struggle for freedom.
- **Democracies competitive in AI.** As I wrote above, democracies have a legitimate interest in some AI-powered military and geopolitical tools, because democratic governments offer the best chance to counter the use of these tools by autocracies. Broadly, I am supportive of arming democracies with the tools needed to defeat autocracies in the age of AI—I simply don’t think there is any other way. But we cannot ignore the potential for abuse of these technologies by democratic governments themselves. Democracies normally have safeguards that prevent their military and intelligence apparatus from being turned inwards against their own population,[^31]but because AI tools require so few people to operate, there is potential for them to circumvent these safeguards and the norms that support them. It is also worth noting that some of these safeguards are already gradually eroding in some democracies. Thus, we should arm democracies with AI, but we should do so carefully and within limits: they are the immune system we need to fight autocracies, but like the immune system, there is some risk of them turning on us and becoming a threat themselves.
- **Non-democratic countries with large datacenters.** Beyond China, most countries with less democratic governance are not leading AI players in the sense that they don’t have companies which produce frontier AI models. Thus they pose a fundamentally different and lesser risk than the CCP, which remains the primary concern (most are also less repressive, and the ones that are more repressive, like North Korea, have no significant AI industry at all). But some of these countries do have large *datacenters* (often as part of buildouts by companies operating in democracies), which can be used to run frontier AI at large scale (though this does not confer the ability to push the frontier). There is some amount of danger associated with this—these governments could in principle expropriate the datacenters and use the country of AIs within it for their own ends. I am less worried about this compared to countries like China that directly develop AI, but it’s a risk to keep in mind.[^32]
- **AI companies.** It is somewhat awkward to say this as the CEO of an AI company, but I think the next tier of risk is actually AI companies themselves. AI companies control large datacenters, train frontier models, have the greatest expertise on how to use those models, and in some cases have daily contact with and the possibility of influence over tens or hundreds of millions of users. The main thing they lack is the legitimacy and infrastructure of a state, so much of what would be needed to build the tools of an AI autocracy would be illegal for an AI company to do, or at least exceedingly suspicious. But some of it is not impossible: they could, for example, use their AI products to brainwash their massive consumer user base, and the public should be alert to the risk this represents. I think the governance of AI companies deserves a lot of scrutiny.

- **中国共产党。** 中国在 AI 能力上仅次于美国，也是最有可能在这些能力上超过美国的国家。其政府目前是专制的，并运营着一个高科技监控国家。它已经部署了基于 AI 的监控（包括对[维吾尔人](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uyghurs)的镇压），据信还通过 TikTok 进行算法宣传（此外还有许多其他国际宣传努力）。它毫无疑问最有可能走上我上面描绘的 AI 极权噩梦之路。这甚至可能成为中国国内的默认结局，也可能蔓延到接受中共监控技术出口的其他专制国家。我曾[多次撰文](https://www.darioamodei.com/post/on-deepseek-and-export-controls)谈到中共在 AI 上领先的威胁，以及阻止它领先的生存必要性。原因就在这里。需要明确的是，我并非出于对中国的特殊敌意而单挑它——它只是最集中地同时具备 AI 实力、专制政府和高科技监控国家这三个条件的国家。如果说有什么不同，最可能遭受中共 AI 镇压的恰恰是中国人民自己，而他们对政府的行动没有发言权。我非常钦佩和尊重中国人民，支持中国国内许多勇敢的异见者及其争取自由的斗争。
- **在 AI 上具有竞争力的民主国家。** 正如我上面所写，民主国家对某些 AI 驱动的军事和地缘政治工具拥有正当利益，因为民主政府最有可能对抗专制政权使用这些工具。总体而言，我支持用必要工具武装民主国家，以便在 AI 时代击败专制政权——我只是认为没有其他办法。但我们不能忽视民主政府自身滥用这些技术的可能性。民主国家通常有防护机制，防止军队和情报机构转向本国人民，[^31]但由于 AI 工具只需要极少数人操作，它们有可能绕过这些防护机制及支持它们的规范。还值得注意的是，在一些民主国家，其中一些防护已经在逐渐侵蚀。因此，我们应当用 AI 武装民主国家，但要谨慎、有限度：它们是我们对抗专制所需要的免疫系统，但就像免疫系统一样，它们也有反噬我们、自身成为威胁的风险。
- **拥有大型数据中心的非民主国家。** 除中国之外，大多数民主治理程度较低的国家并不是 AI 领导者，因为它们没有生产前沿 AI 模型的公司。因此，它们构成的风险在性质上不同，也比中共小，后者仍是主要担忧（大多数国家也没有那么高压，而像朝鲜这样更高压的国家则根本没有重要的 AI 产业）。但其中一些国家确实拥有大型*数据中心*（往往是民主国家公司在当地建设的一部分），可以用来大规模运行前沿 AI（尽管这并不赋予它们推进前沿的能力）。这带来一定程度的风险——这些政府原则上可以征用数据中心，利用其中的 AI 国度为自身目的服务。与中国这样直接开发 AI 的国家相比，我对此担心较少，但这是一个需要记住的风险。[^32]
- **AI 公司。** 作为一家 AI 公司的 CEO，说这话有些尴尬，但我认为下一层风险其实是 AI 公司自己。AI 公司控制着大型数据中心，训练前沿模型，拥有如何使用这些模型的最强专业知识，有些公司还与数亿用户日常接触，并有可能影响他们。它们主要缺少的是国家的合法性和基础设施，因此建立 AI 专制工具所需的许多东西，对 AI 公司来说是非法的，至少极为可疑。但其中一些并非不可能：例如，它们可以利用 AI 产品给庞大的消费者用户群洗脑，公众应当警惕这种风险。我认为 AI 公司的治理值得大量审视。

There are a number of possible arguments against the severity of these threats, and I wish I believed them, because AI-enabled authoritarianism terrifies me. It’s worth going through some of these arguments and responding to them.

对于这些威胁的严重性，有若干可能的反对论点，我希望能相信它们，因为 AI 赋能的威权主义让我恐惧。值得逐一考察并回应其中一些论点。

First, some people might put their faith in the nuclear deterrent, particularly to counter the use of AI autonomous weapons for military conquest. If someone threatens to use these weapons against you, you can always threaten a nuclear response back. My worry is that I’m [not totally sure we can be confident](https://futureoflife.org/document/ai-and-nuclear-problem-analysis-and-policy-recommendations/) in the nuclear deterrent against a country of geniuses in a datacenter: it is possible that powerful AI could [devise ways](https://www.rand.org/content/dam/rand/pubs/perspectives/PE200/PE296/RAND_PE296.pdf) to [detect and strike nuclear submarines](https://spectrum.ieee.org/nuclear-submarine), [conduct influence operations](https://www.sipri.org/sites/default/files/2019-05/sipri1905-ai-strategic-stability-nuclear-risk.pdf) against the operators of nuclear weapons infrastructure, or use AI’s [cyber capabilities](https://committees.parliament.uk/writtenevidence/120293/pdf/) to launch a cyberattack against satellites used to detect nuclear launches.[^33]

首先，有些人可能寄希望于核威慑，尤其是用来对抗 AI 自主武器发动的军事征服。如果有人威胁对你使用这些武器，你总可以威胁以核报复回应。我的担忧是，我[不完全确定](https://futureoflife.org/document/ai-and-nuclear-problem-analysis-and-policy-recommendations/)，面对数据中心里的天才国度，我们能否对核威慑抱有信心：强大 AI 可能[找到办法](https://www.rand.org/content/dam/rand/pubs/perspectives/PE200/PE296/RAND_PE296.pdf)来[探测和打击核潜艇](https://spectrum.ieee.org/nuclear-submarine)、对核武器基础设施的操作者[开展影响力行动](https://www.sipri.org/sites/default/files/2019-05/sipri1905-ai-strategic-stability-nuclear-risk.pdf)，或利用 AI 的[网络能力](https://committees.parliament.uk/writtenevidence/120293/pdf/)对用于探测核发射的卫星发动网络攻击。[^33]

Alternatively, it’s possible that taking over countries is feasible with only AI surveillance and AI propaganda, and never actually presents a clear moment where it’s obvious what is going on and where a nuclear response would be appropriate. *Maybe* these things aren’t feasible and the nuclear deterrent will still be effective, but it seems too high stakes to take a risk.[^34]

或者，夺取国家可能只靠 AI 监控和 AI 宣传就能实现，而且永远不会出现一个明确的时刻，让你看清发生了什么、适合以核回应。*也许*这些手段不可行，核威慑仍然有效，但风险太大，不能赌。[^34]

A second possible objection is that there might be countermeasures we can take against these tools of autocracy. We can counter drones with our own drones, cyberdefense will improve along with cyberattack, there may be ways to immunize people against propaganda, etc. My response is that these defenses will only be possible with comparably powerful AI. If there isn’t some counterforce with a comparably smart and numerous country of geniuses in a datacenter, it won’t be possible to match the quality or quantity of drones, for cyberdefense to outsmart cyberoffense, etc. So the question of countermeasures reduces to the question of a balance of power in powerful AI. Here, I am concerned about the recursive or self-reinforcing property of powerful AI (which I discussed at the beginning of this essay): that each generation of AI can be used to design and train the next generation of AI. This leads to a risk of a runaway advantage, where the current leader in powerful AI may be able to increase their lead and may be difficult to catch up with. We need to make sure it is not an authoritarian country that gets to this loop first.

第二种可能的反对意见是，我们可以对这些专制工具采取反制措施。我们可以用自己的无人机对抗无人机，网络防御会随着网络攻击一起进步，也许有办法让人对宣传免疫，等等。我的回答是，这些防御只有在拥有同样强大的 AI 时才可能实现。如果没有一个同样聪明、数量相当的天才国度作为反制力量，就不可能匹配无人机的质量或数量，也不可能让网络防御胜过网络攻击，等等。因此，反制问题归结为强大 AI 的力量平衡问题。

在这里，我最担心的是强大 AI 的递归或自我强化特性（我在本文开头讨论过）：每一代 AI 都可以用来设计和训练下一代 AI。这带来领先优势失控的风险，当前在强大 AI 上领先的国家可能扩大领先，难以追赶。我们必须确保，最先进入这个循环的不是威权国家。

Furthermore, even if a balance of power can be achieved, there is still risk that the world could be split up into autocratic spheres, as in *Nineteen Eighty-Four*. Even if several competing powers each have their powerful AI models, and none can overpower the others, each power could still internally repress their own population, and would be very difficult to overthrow (since the populations don’t have powerful AI to defend themselves). It is thus important to prevent AI-enabled autocracy even if it doesn’t lead to a single country taking over the world.

此外，即使能够实现力量平衡，世界仍有被分割成多个专制势力范围的风险，就像《一九八四》那样。即使几个相互竞争的强权各自拥有强大 AI 模型，谁也无法压倒谁，每个强权仍可以在内部压迫自己的人民，而且很难被推翻（因为人民没有强大 AI 来保护自己）。因此，即使不会导致单一国家接管世界，防止 AI 赋能的专制也很重要。

### 防御措施（Defenses）

How do we defend against this wide range of autocratic tools and potential threat actors? As in the previous sections, there are several things I think we can do. First, we should absolutely not be selling chips, chip-making tools, or datacenters to the CCP. Chips and chip-making tools are the single greatest bottleneck to powerful AI, and blocking them is a simple but extremely effective measure, perhaps the most important single action we can take. It makes no sense to sell the CCP the tools with which to build an AI totalitarian state and possibly conquer us militarily. A number of complicated arguments are made to justify such sales, such as the idea that “spreading our tech stack around the world” allows “America to win” in some general, unspecified economic battle. In my view, this is like selling nuclear weapons to North Korea and then bragging that the missile casings are made by Boeing and so the US is “winning.” China is several years behind the US in their ability to produce frontier chips in quantity, and the critical period for building the country of geniuses in a datacenter is very likely to be within those next several years.[^35] There is no reason to give a giant boost to their AI industry during this critical period.

面对如此盘根错节的专制工具链与潜在威胁行为者，我们该如何防御？

首先，也是最不容妥协的一点：**我们坚决不能向中共出售芯片、芯片制造工具或数据中心。** 芯片和芯片制造工具是强大 AI 的最大瓶颈，封锁它们是简单却极其有效的措施，也许是我们能采取的最重要的单项行动。把建立 AI 极权国家、甚至可能用来在军事上征服我们的工具卖给中共，毫无道理。人们提出许多复杂的理由为这类销售辩护，比如所谓“把我们的技术栈传播到世界各地”能让“美国赢得”某种笼统而未说明的经济竞争。在我看来，这就像把核武器卖给朝鲜，然后吹嘘导弹外壳是波音制造的，所以美国“赢了”。中国大规模生产前沿芯片的能力落后美国数年，而建设数据中心里的天才国度的关键时期很可能就在未来几年。[^35]

在这个关键时期，给他们的 AI 产业巨大推动，没有任何正当理由。

Second, it makes sense to use AI to empower democracies to resist autocracies. This is the reason Anthropic considers it important to provide AI to the intelligence and defense communities in the US and its democratic allies. Defending democracies that are under attack, such as Ukraine and (via cyber attacks) Taiwan, seems especially high priority, as does empowering democracies to use their intelligence services to disrupt and degrade autocracies from the inside. At some level the only way to respond to autocratic threats is to match and outclass them militarily. A coalition of the US and its democratic allies, if it achieved predominance in powerful AI, would be in a position to not only defend itself against autocracies, but contain them and limit their AI totalitarian abuses.

第二，用 AI 赋能民主国家抵抗专制，是合理的。这就是为什么 Anthropic 认为向美国及其民主盟友的情报和国防部门提供 AI 很重要。保卫受到攻击的民主国家，比如乌克兰和（通过网络攻击）台湾，似乎尤其优先；让民主国家的情报机构能够从内部干扰和削弱专制政权也同样重要。在某种程度上，应对专制威胁的唯一办法就是在军事上匹配并超越它们。如果美国及其民主盟友的联盟在强大 AI 上取得优势，它不仅能自卫对抗专制，还能遏制它们，限制其 AI 极权滥用。

Third, we need to draw a hard line against AI abuses within democracies. There need to be limits to what we allow our governments to do with AI, so that they don’t seize power or repress their own people. The formulation I have come up with is that we should use AI for national defense in all ways *except those which would make us more like our autocratic adversaries*.

第三，我们必须在民主国家内部划出一条硬线，反对 AI 被滥用。必须限制我们的政府用 AI 做什么，以免它们夺取权力或压迫自己的人民。我想到的表述是：我们应当在一切方面把 AI 用于国防，*唯独那些会让我们更像专制对手的方式除外*。

Where should the line be drawn? In the list at the beginning of this section, two items—using AI for domestic mass surveillance and mass propaganda—seem to me like bright red lines and entirely illegitimate. Some might argue that there’s no need to do anything (at least in the US), since domestic mass surveillance is already illegal under the Fourth Amendment. But the rapid progress of AI may create situations that our existing legal frameworks are not well designed to deal with. For example, it would likely not be unconstitutional for the US government to conduct massively scaled recordings of all *public* conversations (e.g., things people say to each other on a street corner), and previously it would have been difficult to sort through this volume of information, but with AI it could all be transcribed, interpreted, and triangulated to create a picture of the attitude and loyalties of many or most citizens. I would support civil liberties-focused legislation (or maybe even a constitutional amendment) that imposes stronger guardrails against AI-powered abuses.

这条线该划在哪里？在本节开头列出的清单中，有两项——用 AI 进行国内大规模监控和大规模宣传——在我看来是明确的红线，完全不正当。有人可能说（至少在美国）不必做什么，因为根据第四修正案，国内大规模监控已经属于非法。但 AI 的快速进步可能造成现有法律框架没有设计好如何应对的局面。例如，美国政府大规模录制所有*公开*谈话（比如人们在街角互相说的话）很可能并不违宪；过去很难梳理如此海量的信息，但有了 AI，所有内容都可以被转写、解读并交叉比对，从而描绘出许多或大多数公民的态度与忠诚。我会支持以公民自由为重点的立法（甚至可能是宪法修正案），对 AI 驱动的滥用设置更强的防护。

The other two items—fully autonomous weapons and AI for strategic decision-making—are harder lines to draw since they have legitimate uses in defending democracy, while also being prone to abuse. Here I think what is warranted is extreme care and scrutiny combined with guardrails to prevent abuses. My main fear is having too small a number of “fingers on the button,” such that one or a handful of people could essentially operate a drone army without needing any other humans to cooperate to carry out their orders. As AI systems get more powerful, we may need to have more direct and immediate oversight mechanisms to ensure they are not misused, perhaps involving branches of government other than the executive. I think we should approach fully autonomous weapons in particular with great caution,[^36] and not rush into their use without proper safeguards.

另外两项——全自主武器和用于战略决策的 AI——较难划线，因为它们在保卫民主方面有正当用途，同时也容易被滥用。我认为，这里需要的是极度谨慎、审视以及防止滥用的防护。我最担心的是“按按钮的手指”太少，以至于一个人或少数几个人就能操作一支无人机军队，而不需要任何其他人合作执行命令。随着 AI 系统变得更强大，我们可能需要更直接、更即时的监督机制，确保它们不被滥用，或许要涉及行政部门以外的政府分支。我认为，尤其应当非常谨慎地对待全自主武器，[^36]不要在缺乏适当防护的情况下仓促使用。

Fourth, after drawing a hard line against AI abuses in democracies, we should use that precedent to create an international taboo against the worst abuses of powerful AI. I recognize that the current political winds have turned against international cooperation and international norms, but this is a case where we sorely need them. The world needs to understand the dark potential of powerful AI in the hands of autocrats, and to recognize that certain uses of AI amount to an attempt to permanently steal their freedom and impose a totalitarian state from which they can’t escape. I would even argue that in some cases, large-scale surveillance with powerful AI, mass propaganda with powerful AI, and certain types of *offensive* uses of fully autonomous weapons should be considered crimes against humanity. More generally, a robust norm against AI-enabled totalitarianism and all its tools and instruments is sorely needed.

第四，在民主国家内部划出反对 AI 滥用的硬线之后，我们应当利用这一先例，建立一项反对最恶劣地滥用强大 AI 的国际禁忌。我认识到，当前政治风向不利于国际合作和国际规范，但在这个问题上，我们迫切需要它们。世界需要理解强大 AI 落在独裁者手中的黑暗潜力，并认识到某些 AI 用途等同于试图永久窃取人们的自由、强加一个无法逃脱的极权国家。我甚至认为，在某些情况下，用强大 AI 进行大规模监控、用强大 AI 进行大规模宣传，以及某些类型的*进攻性*全自主武器使用，应当被视为反人类罪。更一般地说，我们迫切需要一项强有力的规范，反对 AI 赋能的极权主义及其所有工具和手段。

It is possible to have an even stronger version of this position, which is that because the possibilities of AI-enabled totalitarianism are so dark, autocracy is simply not a form of government that people can accept in the post-powerful AI age. Just as feudalism became unworkable with the industrial revolution, the AI age could lead inevitably and logically to the conclusion that democracy (and, hopefully, democracy improved and reinvigorated by AI, as I discuss in *Machines of Loving Grace*) is the only viable form of government if humanity is to have a good future.

对这一立场还可以有更强的版本：由于 AI 赋能的极权主义前景如此黑暗，在强大 AI 之后，专制根本不再是人们可以接受的政府形式。正如封建制度在工业革命后变得不可行，AI 时代可能不可避免地、合乎逻辑地得出一个结论：如果人类要有美好的未来，民主（以及如我在《仁慈造物之机》中所讨论的、希望由 AI 改进和振兴的民主）是唯一可行的政府形式。

Fifth and finally, AI companies should be carefully watched, as should their connection to the government, which is necessary, but must have limits and boundaries. The sheer amount of capability embodied in powerful AI is such that ordinary corporate governance—which is designed to protect shareholders and prevent ordinary abuses such as fraud—is unlikely to be up to the task of governing AI companies. There may also be value in companies publicly committing to (perhaps even as part of corporate governance) not take certain actions, such as privately building or stockpiling military hardware, using large amounts of computing resources by single individuals in unaccountable ways, or using their AI products as propaganda to manipulate public opinion in their favor.

第五，也是最后一点，AI 公司应当受到仔细审视，它们与政府的关系也应如此——这种关系是必要的，但必须有界限。强大 AI 所蕴含的能力之大，使得普通公司治理——旨在保护股东、防止欺诈等普通滥用——不太可能胜任对 AI 公司的治理。公司公开承诺（或许甚至作为公司治理的一部分）不采取某些行动，也可能有价值，比如不私下建造或囤积军事硬件、不允许单个人以无法问责的方式使用大量算力、不利用 AI 产品作为宣传来操纵舆论以利于自己。

The danger here comes from many directions, and some directions are in tension with others. The only constant is that we must seek accountability, norms, and guardrails for everyone, even as we empower “good” actors to keep “bad” actors in check.

这里的危险来自许多方向，有些方向彼此冲突。唯一不变的是，我们必须为所有人寻求问责、规范和防护，同时赋能“好”的行为者去制约“坏”的行为者。

## 4. 自动钢琴（Player piano）

### 经济冲击（Economic disruption）

The previous three sections were essentially about security risks posed by powerful AI: risks from the AI itself, risks from misuse by individuals and small organizations and risks of misuse by states and large organizations. If we put aside security risks or assume they have been solved, the next question is economic. What will be the effect of this infusion of incredible “human” capital on the economy? Clearly, the most obvious effect will be to greatly increase economic growth. The pace of advances in scientific research, biomedical innovation, manufacturing, supply chains, the efficiency of the financial system, and much more are almost guaranteed to lead to a much faster rate of economic growth. In *Machines of Loving Grace*, I suggest that a 10–20% sustained annual GDP growth rate may be possible.

前三节本质上讲的是强大 AI 带来的安全风险：AI 自身的风险、个人和小型组织滥用它的风险，以及国家和大型组织滥用它的风险。如果暂且放下安全风险，或者假定它们已经解决，下一个问题就是经济。这种不可思议的“人力”资本注入经济，会产生什么影响？显然，最明显的影响是大幅提高经济增长。科学研究、生物医学创新、制造业、供应链、金融体系效率等方面的进步速度，几乎必然会导致经济增长率大幅提高。在《仁慈造物之机》中，我提出，持续的年均 GDP 增长 10%–20% 是可能的。

But it should be clear that this is a double-edged sword: what are the economic prospects for most existing humans in such a world? New technologies often bring labor market shocks, and in the past humans have always recovered from them, but I am concerned that this is because these previous shocks affected only a small fraction of the full possible range of human abilities, leaving room for humans to expand to new tasks. AI will have effects that are much broader and occur much faster, and therefore I worry it will be much more challenging to make things work out well.

但显然，这是一把双刃剑：在这样一个世界里，大多数现有人类的经济前景如何？新技术往往带来劳动力市场冲击，过去人类总能从中恢复；但我担心，那是因为过去的冲击只影响了人类能力全部可能范围的一小部分，给人类留下了扩展到新任务的空间。AI 的影响将广泛得多，也快得多，因此我担心要让事情顺利解决会困难得多。

### 劳动力市场冲击（Labor market disruption）

There are two specific problems I am worried about: labor market displacement, and concentration of economic power. Let’s start with the first one. This is a topic that I [warned about very publicly in 2025](https://www.axios.com/2025/05/28/ai-jobs-white-collar-unemployment-anthropic), where I predicted that AI could displace half of all entry-level white collar jobs in the next 1–5 years, even as it accelerates economic growth and scientific progress. This warning started a public debate about the topic. Many CEOs, technologists, and economists agreed with me, but others assumed I was falling prey to a “lump of labor” fallacy and didn’t know how labor markets worked, and some didn’t see the 1–5-year time range and thought I was claiming AI is displacing jobs right now (which I agree it is likely not). So it is worth going through in detail why I am worried about labor displacement, to clear up these misunderstandings.

我担心的具体问题有两个：劳动力市场冲击，以及经济权力集中。先说第一个。这是一个我[在 2025 年非常公开地警告过](https://www.axios.com/2025/05/28/ai-jobs-white-collar-unemployment-anthropic)的话题，当时我预测 AI 可能在未来 1–5 年内取代一半的初级白领工作，即使它同时加速经济增长和科学进步。这个警告引发了公众讨论。许多 CEO、技术专家和经济学家同意我的看法，但另一些人认为我陷入了“劳动总量”谬误，不懂劳动力市场如何运作；还有一些人没看到 1–5 年的时间范围，以为我在说 AI 现在就在取代工作（我同意这很可能还没有）。所以值得详细说明我为什么担心岗位流失，以澄清这些误解。

As a baseline, it’s useful to understand how labor markets *normally* respond to advances in technology. When a new technology comes along, it starts by making pieces of a given human job more efficient. For example, early in the Industrial Revolution, machines, such as upgraded plows, enabled human farmers to be more efficient at some aspects of the job. This improved the productivity of farmers, which increased their wages.

作为基准，先了解劳动力市场*通常*如何应对技术进步是有用的。新技术出现时，首先会让某项人类工作中的某些环节更有效率。例如，工业革命早期，改良的犁等机器让农民在某些环节更有效率，提高了农民的生产率，从而提高了他们的工资。

In the next step, some parts of the job of farming could be done *entirely* by machines, for example with the invention of the [threshing machine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Threshing_machine) or [seed drill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seed_drill). In this phase, humans did a lower and lower fraction of the job, but the work they *did* complete became more and more leveraged because it is complementary to the work of machines, and their productivity continued to rise. As described by [Jevons’ paradox](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jevons_paradox), the wages of farmers and perhaps even the number of farmers continued to increase. Even when 90% of the job is being done by machines, humans can simply do 10x more of the 10% they still do, producing 10x as much output for the same amount of labor.

下一步，农业工作的某些部分可以*完全*由机器完成，例如[脱粒机](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Threshing_machine)或[播种机](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seed_drill)的发明。在这个阶段，人完成的工作比例越来越小，但他们*所做*的工作获得了更强的杠杆效应，因为它与机器的工作互补，生产率继续上升。正如[杰文斯悖论](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jevons_paradox)所描述的那样，农民的工资甚至农民人数都可能继续增加。即使 90% 的工作由机器完成，人类也可以把仍然由自己做的那 10% 放大十倍，以同样的劳动生产出十倍的产品。

Eventually, machines do everything or almost everything, as with modern [combine harvesters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Combine_harvester), tractors, and other equipment. At this point farming as a form of human employment really does go into steep decline, and this potentially causes serious disruption in the short term, but because farming is just one of many useful activities that humans are able to do, people eventually switch to other jobs, such as operating factory machines. This is true even though farming accounted for a huge proportion of employment *ex ante*. 250 years ago, 90% of Americans [lived on farms](https://www.nass.usda.gov/About_NASS/History_of_Ag_Statistics/index.php); in Europe, 50–60% of employment [was agricultural](https://ourworldindata.org/grapher/share-of-the-labor-force-employed-in-agriculture?time=1750..latest). Now those percentages are in the low single digits in those places, because workers switched to industrial jobs (and later, knowledge work jobs). The economy can do what previously required most of the labor force with only 1–2% of it, freeing up the rest of the labor force to build an ever more advanced industrial society. There’s no fixed “ [lump of labor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lump_of_labour_fallacy),” just an ever-expanding ability to do [more and more with less and less](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ephemeralization). People’s wages rise in line with the GDP exponential and the economy maintains full employment once disruptions in the short term have passed.

最终，机器完成所有或几乎所有工作，比如现代[联合收割机](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Combine_harvester)、拖拉机和其他设备。此时，农业作为一种人类就业形式确实急剧萎缩，短期内可能造成严重冲击；但由于农业只是人类能够从事的众多有用活动之一，人们最终会转向其他工作，比如操作工厂机器。即使农业在*事前*占就业的很大比例，情况也是如此。250 年前，90% 的美国人[住在农场](https://www.nass.usda.gov/About_NASS/History_of_Ag_Statistics/index.php)；在欧洲，50%–60% 的就业[是农业](https://ourworldindata.org/grapher/share-of-the-labor-force-employed-in-agriculture?time=1750..latest)。现在这些地方的这一比例已降到个位数，因为工人转向了工业工作（后来又转向知识工作）。经济可以用 1%–2% 的劳动力完成过去需要大部分劳动力才能完成的事，从而解放其余劳动力去建设更先进的工业社会。并不存在固定的“[劳动总量](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lump_of_labour_fallacy)”，只有不断扩展的、[以更少做更多](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ephemeralization)的能力。人们的工资随 GDP 的指数增长而上升，短期内冲击过去后，经济也能维持充分就业。

It’s possible things will go roughly the same way with AI, but I would bet pretty strongly against it. Here are some reasons I think AI is likely to be different:

AI 可能也会以大致相同的方式发展，但我敢相当肯定地打赌，不会。以下是我认为 AI 可能不同的几个原因：

- **Speed.** The pace of progress in AI is much faster than for previous technological revolutions. For example, in the last 2 years, AI models went from barely being able to complete a single line of code, to [writing all or almost all of the code](https://x.com/bcherny/status/2004887829252317325) for some people—including engineers at Anthropic.[^37]Soon, they may do the entire task of a software engineer end to end.[^38]It is hard for people to adapt to this pace of change, both to the changes in how a given job works and in the need to switch to new jobs. Even legendary programmers are increasingly [describing themselves as “behind.”](https://x.com/karpathy/status/2004607146781278521) The pace may if anything continue to speed up, as AI coding models increasingly accelerate the task of AI development. To be clear, speed in itself does not mean labor markets and employment won’t eventually recover, it just implies the short-term transition will be unusually painful compared to past technologies, since humans and labor markets are slow to react and to equilibrate.
- **Cognitive breadth.** As suggested by the phrase “country of geniuses in a datacenter,” AI will be capable of a very wide range of human cognitive abilities—perhaps all of them. This is very different from previous technologies like mechanized farming, transportation, or even computers.[^39]This will make it harder for people to switch easily from jobs that are displaced to similar jobs that they would be a good fit for. For example, the general intellectual abilities required for entry-level jobs in, say, finance, consulting, and law are fairly similar, even if the specific knowledge is quite different. A technology that disrupted only one of these three would allow employees to switch to the two other close substitutes (or for undergraduates to switch majors). But disrupting all three at once (along with many other similar jobs) may be harder for people to adapt to. Furthermore, it’s not *just* that most existing jobs will be disrupted. That part has happened before—recall that farming was a huge percentage of employment. But farmers could switch to the relatively similar work of operating factory machines, even though that work hadn’t been common before. By contrast, AI is increasingly matching the general cognitive profile of humans, which means it will also be good at the new jobs that would ordinarily be created in response to the old ones being automated. Another way to say it is that AI isn’t a substitute for specific human jobs but rather a general labor substitute for humans.
- **Slicing by cognitive ability.** Across a wide range of tasks, AI appears to be advancing from the bottom of the ability ladder to the top. For example, in coding our models have proceeded from the level of “a mediocre coder” to “a strong coder” to “a very strong coder.” [^40]We are now starting to see the same progression in white-collar work in general. We are thus at risk of a situation where, instead of affecting people with specific skills or in specific professions (who can adapt by retraining), AI is affecting people with certain intrinsic cognitive properties, namely lower intellectual ability (which is harder to change). It is not clear where these people will go or what they will do, and I am concerned that they could form an unemployed or very-low-wage “underclass.” To be clear, things somewhat like this have happened before—for example, computers and the internet are believed by some economists to represent “ [skill-biased technological change](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/chapter/handbook/abs/pii/S0169721811024105).” But this skill biasing was both not as extreme as what I expect to see with AI, and is believed to have contributed to an increase in wage inequality,[^41]so it is not exactly a reassuring precedent.
- **Ability to fill in the gaps.** The way human jobs often adjust in the face of new technology is that there are many aspects to the job, and the new technology, even if it appears to directly replace humans, often has gaps in it. If someone invents a machine to make widgets, humans may still have to load raw material into the machine. Even if that takes only 1% as much effort as making the widgets manually, human workers can simply make 100x more widgets. But AI, in addition to being a rapidly advancing technology, is also a rapidly *adapting* technology. During every model release, AI companies carefully measure what the model is good at and what it isn’t, and customers also provide such information after the launch. Weaknesses can be addressed by collecting tasks that embody the current gap, and training on them for the next model. Early in generative AI, users noticed that AI systems had certain weaknesses (such as AI image models generating hands with the wrong number of fingers) and many assumed these weaknesses were inherent to the technology. If they were, it would limit job disruption. But pretty much every such weakness gets addressed quickly— often, within just a few months.

- **速度。** AI 的进步速度比以往的技术革命快得多。例如，过去两年，AI 模型从几乎写不出一行代码，发展到为一些人[写出全部或几乎所有代码](https://x.com/bcherny/status/2004887829252317325)，包括 Anthropic 的工程师。[^37] 很快，它们可能端到端完成软件工程师的全部工作。[^38] 人们很难适应这种变化速度，无论是适应某项工作的方式变化，还是适应转行的需要。甚至传奇程序员也越来越[把自己描述为“落后”](https://x.com/karpathy/status/2004607146781278521)。随着 AI 编程模型日益加速 AI 开发任务，这种速度可能还会继续加快。明确地说，速度本身并不意味着劳动力市场和就业最终不会恢复，它只意味着与过去的技术相比，短期转型会异常痛苦，因为人和劳动力市场的反应与再平衡都很慢。
- **认知广度。** 正如“数据中心里的天才国度”这个说法所暗示的，AI 将能够胜任非常广泛的人类认知能力——也许是全部。这与以往的技术（如机械化农业、运输，甚至计算机）非常不同。[^39] 这将使人们更难从被取代的工作轻松转向他们适合的类似工作。例如，金融、咨询和法律等领域的初级工作所需的一般智力能力相当相似，即使具体知识差别很大。一项只颠覆这三者之一的技术，会让员工转向另外两个相近替代行业（或让本科生转专业）。但同时颠覆这三者（以及许多其他类似工作），人们可能更难适应。此外，这*不仅仅*是大多数现有工作会被颠覆。那部分以前也发生过——回想一下，农业曾占就业的很大比例。但农民可以转向操作工厂机器这种相对类似的工作，即使这种工作以前并不常见。相比之下，AI 越来越匹配人类的一般认知特征，这意味着它也会擅长那些通常因旧工作被自动化而创造出来的新工作。换一种说法：AI 不是特定人类工作的替代品，而是对人类一般劳动力的替代。
- **按认知能力切片。** 在广泛的任务中，AI 似乎正在从能力阶梯的底部向顶部推进。例如，在编程方面，我们的模型已经从“平庸的程序员”发展到“很强的程序员”，再到“非常强的程序员”。[^40] 我们现在开始在整个白领工作中看到同样的进程。因此，我们面临的风险是：AI 影响的不再是拥有特定技能或从事特定职业的人（他们可以通过再培训适应），而是具有某些内在认知特征的人，也就是智力较低的人（这更难改变）。不清楚这些人会去哪里、做什么，我担心他们可能形成一个失业或极低工资的“底层阶级”。明确地说，类似的事情以前也发生过——例如，一些经济学家认为计算机和互联网代表了“[技能偏向型技术变革](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/chapter/handbook/abs/pii/S0169721811024105)”。但这种技能偏向既没有我预期 AI 带来的那么极端，也被认为加剧了工资不平等，[^41]所以这并不是一个令人安心的先例。
- **填补空白的能力。** 人类工作面对新技术时往往能调整，是因为工作有许多方面，而新技术即使看起来直接替代人类，也常常留下空白。如果有人发明了制造小部件的机器，人类可能仍需要给机器上料。即使这只需要手工制造小部件 1% 的力气，人类也可以简单地制造 100 倍的小部件。但 AI 不仅是一项快速进步的技术，也是一项快速*适应*的技术。每次发布模型时，AI 公司都会仔细衡量模型擅长什么、不擅长什么，客户也会在上线后提供这类信息。弱点可以通过收集体现当前差距的任务，并在下一代模型上训练来解决。生成式 AI 早期，用户注意到 AI 系统有某些弱点（比如 AI 图像模型生成的手指数量不对），许多人以为这些弱点是技术固有的。如果是这样，那会限制工作颠覆。但几乎每一个这样的弱点都会很快得到解决——往往只需几个月。

It’s worth addressing common points of skepticism. First, there is the argument that economic diffusion will be slow, such that even if the underlying technology is *capable* of doing most human labor, the actual application of it across the economy may be much slower (for example in industries that are far from the AI industry and slow to adopt). Slow diffusion of technology is [definitely real](https://www.ft.com/content/3b93e647-2a8b-4fb4-831d-e27adf4db5f8) —I talk to people from a wide variety of enterprises, and there are places where the adoption of AI will take years. That’s why my prediction for 50% of entry level white collar jobs being disrupted is 1–5 years, even though I suspect we’ll have powerful AI (which would be, technologically speaking, enough to do *most or all* jobs, not just entry level) in much less than 5 years. But diffusion effects merely buy us time. And I am not confident they will be as slow as people predict. Enterprise AI adoption is growing at rates much faster than any previous technology, largely on the pure strength of the technology itself. Also, even if traditional enterprises are slow to adopt new technology, startups will spring up to serve as “glue” and make the adoption easier. If that doesn’t work, the startups may simply disrupt the incumbents directly.

值得回应一些常见的怀疑。首先，有人认为经济扩散会缓慢，因此即使底层技术*能够*完成大多数人类劳动，它在整个经济中的实际应用也可能慢得多（例如在远离 AI 行业、采用缓慢的行业）。技术扩散缓慢[确实存在](https://www.ft.com/content/3b93e647-2a8b-4fb4-831d-e27adf4db5f8)：我与各种企业的人交谈过，有些地方采用 AI 需要数年。这就是为什么我预测 50% 的初级白领工作会被取代是在 1–5 年内，尽管我怀疑我们会在远少于 5 年的时间里拥有强大 AI（从技术上讲，它足以完成*大多数或所有*工作，而不仅是初级工作）。但扩散效应只是为我们争取时间。而且我不相信它会像人们预测的那样慢。企业采用 AI 的速度比以往任何技术都快得多，主要靠技术本身的实力。此外，即使传统企业采用新技术缓慢，初创公司也会涌现出来充当“粘合剂”，让采用更容易。如果这不起作用，初创公司可能直接颠覆现有企业。

That could lead to a world where it isn’t so much that specific jobs are disrupted as it is that large enterprises are disrupted in general and replaced with much less labor-intensive startups. This could also lead to a world of “geographic inequality,” where an increasing fraction of the world’s wealth is concentrated in Silicon Valley, which becomes its own economy running at a different speed than the rest of the world and leaving it behind. All of these outcomes would be great for economic growth—but not so great for the labor market or those who are left behind.

这可能导致一个世界：与其说是特定工作被颠覆，不如说是大型企业整体被颠覆，被劳动力密集程度低得多的初创公司取代。这也可能导致“地理不平等”：越来越多的世界财富集中在硅谷，硅谷成为自身的经济体，以与世界其他地方不同的速度运行，把它们甩在后面。所有这些结果对经济增长都是好事——但对劳动力市场或被落下的人来说就不太好了。

Second, some people say that human jobs will move to the physical world, which avoids the whole category of “cognitive labor” where AI is progressing so rapidly. I am not sure how safe this is, either. A lot of physical labor is already being done by machines (e.g., manufacturing) or will soon be done by machines (e.g., driving). Also, sufficiently powerful AI will be able to accelerate the development of robots, and then control those robots in the physical world. It may buy some time (which is a good thing), but I’m worried it won’t buy much. And even if the disruption was limited only to cognitive tasks, it would still be an unprecedentedly large and rapid disruption.

第二，有人说人类工作将转向物理世界，从而避开 AI 进步如此迅速的整个“认知劳动”类别。我也不确定这有多安全。大量体力劳动已经由机器完成（如制造业），或很快将由机器完成（如驾驶）。此外，足够强大的 AI 将能够加速机器人开发，然后在物理世界中控制这些机器人。这也许能争取一些时间（这是好事），但我担心争取不到多少。而且，即使冲击仅限于认知任务，那也仍然是一场前所未有的巨大而迅速的冲击。

Third, perhaps some tasks inherently require or greatly benefit from a human touch. I’m a little more uncertain about this one, but I’m still skeptical that it will be enough to offset the bulk of the impacts I described above. AI is already widely used for customer service. Many people [report](https://undark.org/2025/11/04/chatbot-mental-health/) that it is easier to talk to AI about their personal problems than to talk to a therapist—that the AI is more patient. When my sister was struggling with medical problems during a pregnancy, she felt she wasn’t getting the answers or support she needed from her care providers, and she found Claude to have a better bedside manner (as well as succeeding better at diagnosing the problem). I’m sure there are some tasks for which a human touch really is important, but I’m not sure how many—and here we’re talking about finding work for nearly everyone in the labor market.

第三，也许有些任务本质上需要或非常受益于人的温度。我对这一点稍微不确定，但我仍然怀疑它足以抵消我上面描述的大部分影响。AI 已经被广泛用于客户服务。许多人[报告](https://undark.org/2025/11/04/chatbot-mental-health/)说，与 AI 谈论个人问题比与治疗师交谈更容易——AI 更有耐心。我妹妹怀孕期间遇到医疗问题时，觉得从护理人员那里得不到需要的答案或支持，而她发现 Claude 对病人的态度更好（也更成功地诊断出了问题）。我相信有些任务确实需要人的温度，但我不确定有多少——而我们这里谈的是要为劳动力市场中的几乎每个人找到工作。

Fourth, some may argue that comparative advantage will still protect humans. Under the [law of comparative advantage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Comparative_advantage), even if AI is better than humans at everything, any *relative* differences between the human and AI profile of skills creates a basis of trade and specialization between humans and AI. The problem is that if AIs are literally thousands of times more productive than humans, this logic starts to break down. Even tiny [transaction costs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transaction_cost) could make it not worth it for AI to trade with humans. And human wages may be very low, even if they technically have something to offer.

第四，有人可能认为比较优势仍会保护人类。根据[比较优势定律](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Comparative_advantage)，即使 AI 在所有事情上都比人类强，人类与 AI 技能轮廓之间的任何*相对*差异都会为人类与 AI 之间的贸易和专业化提供基础。问题在于，如果 AI 的生产率是人类的数千倍，这个逻辑就开始瓦解。即使很小的[交易成本](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transaction_cost)也可能让 AI 觉得与人类交易不值得。而人类的工资可能非常低，即使他们在技术上仍能提供一些东西。

It’s possible all of these factors can be addressed—that the labor market is resilient enough to adapt to even such an enormous disruption. But even if it can eventually adapt, the factors above suggest that the short-term shock will be unprecedented in size.

所有这些因素都可能得到解决——劳动力市场可能足够有韧性，能够适应如此巨大的冲击。但即使它最终能够适应，上述因素也表明，短期冲击的规模将是前所未有的。

### 防御措施（Defenses）

What can we do about this problem? I have several suggestions, some of which Anthropic is already doing. The first thing is simply to get accurate data about what is happening with job displacement in real time. When an economic change happens very quickly, it’s hard to get reliable data about what is happening, and without reliable data it is hard to design effective policies. For example, government data is currently lacking granular, high-frequency data on AI adoption across firms and industries. For the last year Anthropic has been operating and publicly releasing an [Economic Index](https://www.anthropic.com/economic-index) that shows use of our models almost in real time, broken down by industry, task, location, and even things like whether a task was being automated or conducted collaboratively. We also have an [Economic Advisory Council](https://www.anthropic.com/news/introducing-the-anthropic-economic-advisory-council) to help us interpret this data and see what is coming.

我们能对此做些什么？我有几点建议，其中一些 Anthropic 已经在做。

第一件事，是获取关于岗位流失情况的准确实时数据。当经济变化非常快时，很难获得可靠数据；没有可靠数据，就很难设计有效政策。例如，政府数据目前缺乏关于企业和行业采用 AI 的细粒度、高频数据。过去一年，Anthropic 一直在运营并公开发布[经济指数](https://www.anthropic.com/economic-index)，几乎实时显示我们模型的使用情况，按行业、任务、地点甚至任务是自动化还是协作完成来细分。我们还有一个[经济顾问委员会](https://www.anthropic.com/news/introducing-the-anthropic-economic-advisory-council)，帮助我们解读这些数据并预见未来。

Second, AI companies have a choice in how they work with enterprises. The very inefficiency of traditional enterprises means that their rollout of AI can be very path dependent, and there is some room to choose a better path. Enterprises often have a choice between “cost savings” (doing the same thing with fewer people) and “innovation” (doing more with the same number of people). The market will inevitably produce both eventually, and any competitive AI company will have to serve some of both, but there may be some room to steer companies towards innovation when possible, and it may buy us some time. Anthropic is actively thinking about this.

第二，AI 公司在如何与企业合作方面有选择。传统企业的低效率意味着它们部署 AI 的路径可能非常依赖具体情境，因此有一些空间选择更好的路径。企业常常在“节省成本”（用更少的人做同样的事）和“创新”（用同样多的人做更多的事）之间选择。市场最终不可避免地会同时产生两者，任何有竞争力的 AI 公司都必须兼顾，但在可能的情况下，也许有一些空间引导企业走向创新，这可能会为我们争取一些时间。Anthropic 正在积极思考这一点。

Third, companies should think about how to take care of their employees. In the short term, being creative about ways to reassign employees within companies may be a promising way to stave off the need for layoffs. In the long term, in a world with enormous total wealth, in which many companies increase greatly in value due to increased productivity and capital concentration, it may be feasible to pay human employees even long after they are no longer providing economic value in the traditional sense. Anthropic is currently considering a range of possible pathways for our own employees that we will share in the near future.

第三，公司应当考虑如何照顾员工。短期内，创造性地在公司内部重新分配员工，可能是避免裁员的有效方式。长期来看，在一个总财富巨大、许多公司因生产率和资本集中而价值大增的世界里，即使人类员工在传统意义上不再提供经济价值，继续支付他们工资也可能是可行的。Anthropic 目前正在为我们自己的员工考虑一系列可能的路径，并将在近期分享。

Fourth, wealthy individuals have an obligation to help solve this problem. It is sad to me that many wealthy individuals (especially in the tech industry) have recently adopted a cynical and nihilistic attitude that philanthropy is inevitably fraudulent or useless. Both private philanthropy like the [Gates Foundation](https://www.gatesfoundation.org/) and public programs like [PEPFAR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/President%27s_Emergency_Plan_for_AIDS_Relief) have saved tens of millions of lives in the developing world, and helped to create economic opportunity in the developed world. All of Anthropic’s co-founders have pledged to donate 80% of our wealth, and Anthropic’s staff have individually pledged to donate company shares worth billions at current prices—donations that the company has committed to matching.

第四，富人有义务帮助解决这个问题。令我难过的是，许多富人（尤其是科技行业）最近采取了一种愤世嫉俗、虚无主义的态度，认为慈善不可避免是欺诈或无用的。像[盖茨基金会](https://www.gatesfoundation.org/)这样的私人慈善，以及像 [PEPFAR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/President%27s_Emergency_Plan_for_AIDS_Relief)这样的公共项目，都在发展中国家挽救了数千万人的生命，并帮助在发达国家创造了经济机会。Anthropic 的所有联合创始人都承诺捐出我们财富的 80%，Anthropic 的员工也个人承诺捐出按当前价格价值数十亿美元的公司股份——公司已承诺配捐。

Fifth, while all the above private actions can be helpful, ultimately a macroeconomic problem this large will require government intervention. The natural policy response to an enormous economic pie coupled with high inequality (due to a lack of jobs, or poorly paid jobs, for many) is progressive taxation. The tax could be general or could be targeted against AI companies in particular. Obviously tax design is complicated, and there are many ways for it to go wrong. I don’t support poorly designed tax policies. I think the extreme levels of inequality predicted in this essay justify a more robust tax policy on basic moral grounds, but I can also make a pragmatic argument to the world’s billionaires that it’s in their interest to support a good version of it: if they don’t support a good version, they’ll inevitably get a bad version designed by a mob.

第五，尽管上述私人行动都有帮助，但如此巨大的宏观经济问题最终需要政府干预。对于巨大的经济蛋糕加上高度不平等（由于许多人缺乏工作或工作报酬低），自然的政策反应是累进税。税收可以是一般性的，也可以专门针对 AI 公司。显然，税收设计很复杂，有很多出错的方式。我不支持设计糟糕的税收政策。我认为本文预测的极端不平等程度，基于基本道德理由，足以支持更强有力的税收政策；但我也可以向世界上的亿万富翁提出一个务实的论点：支持一个好的版本符合他们的利益——如果他们不支持好的版本，他们不可避免地会得到一个由暴民设计的糟糕版本。

Ultimately, I think of all of the above interventions as ways to buy time. In the end AI will be able to do everything, and we need to grapple with that. It’s my hope that by that time, we can use AI itself to help us restructure markets in ways that work for everyone, and that the interventions above can get us through the transitional period.

归根结底，我把上述所有干预都视为争取时间的方式。最终，AI 将能够做所有事情，我们必须面对这一点。我希望到那时，我们可以利用 AI 本身来帮助重组市场，使其对所有人都可行，而上述干预能让我们度过过渡期。

### 经济权力集中（Economic concentration of power）

Separate from the problem of job displacement or economic inequality *per se* is the problem of *economic concentration of power.* Section 1 discussed the risk that humanity gets disempowered by AI, and Section 3 discussed the risk that citizens get disempowered by their governments by force or coercion. But another kind of disempowerment can occur if there is such a huge concentration of wealth that a small group of people effectively controls government policy with their influence, and ordinary citizens have no influence because they lack economic leverage. Democracy is ultimately backstopped by the idea that the population as a whole is necessary for the operation of the economy. If that economic leverage goes away, then the implicit social contract of democracy may stop working. [Others have written about this](https://intelligence-curse.ai/), so I needn’t go into great detail about it here, but I agree with the concern, and I worry it is already starting to happen.

与岗位流失或经济不平等*本身*不同，另一个问题是*经济权力集中*。第一节讨论了人类被 AI 剥夺权力的风险，第三节讨论了公民被政府以武力或胁迫剥夺权力的风险。但还有一种剥夺权力的方式：如果财富如此高度集中，以至于一小群人实际上凭影响力控制政府政策，而普通公民由于缺乏经济杠杆，也就失去了影响力。民主最终依托于一个观念：全体人民对经济运行是必要的。如果这种经济杠杆消失，民主的隐性社会契约可能停止运作。[其他人已经写过这一点](https://intelligence-curse.ai/)，我不必在此详述，但我同意这种担忧，而且我担心它已经开始发生。

To be clear, I am not opposed to people making a lot of money. There’s a strong argument that it incentivizes economic growth under normal conditions. I am sympathetic to concerns about impeding innovation by killing the golden goose that generates it. But in a scenario where GDP growth is 10–20% a year and AI is rapidly taking over the economy, yet single individuals hold appreciable fractions of the GDP, innovation is *not* the thing to worry about. The thing to worry about is a level of wealth concentration that will break society.

明确地说，我不反对人们赚很多钱。有充分理由认为，在正常条件下，它能激励经济增长。我对扼杀下金蛋的鹅、阻碍创新的担忧抱有同情。但在 GDP 每年增长 10%–20%、AI 迅速接管经济，而个人却持有可观 GDP 份额的情景下，创新*不是*需要担心的事。需要担心的是将撕裂社会的财富集中程度。

The most famous example of extreme concentration of wealth in US history is the [Gilded Age](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gilded_Age), and the wealthiest industrialist of the Gilded Age was [John D. Rockefeller](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_D._Rockefeller). Rockefeller’s wealth amounted to ~2% of the US GDP at the time.[^42]

美国历史上财富极度集中最著名的例子是[镀金时代](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gilded_Age)，镀金时代最富有的工业家是[约翰·D·洛克菲勒](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_D._Rockefeller)。洛克菲勒的财富约相当于当时美国 GDP 的 2%。[^42]

A similar fraction today would lead to a fortune of $600B, and the richest person in the world today (Elon Musk) already exceeds that, at [roughly $700B](https://www.reuters.com/business/autos-transportation/elon-musk-becomes-first-person-worth-700-billion-after-court-ruling-pay-package-2025-12-20/). So we are already at historically unprecedented levels of wealth concentration, even *before* most of the economic impact of AI. I don’t think it is too much of a stretch (if we get a “country of geniuses”) to imagine AI companies, semiconductor companies, and perhaps downstream application companies generating ~$3T in revenue per year,[^43]being valued at ~$30T, and leading to personal fortunes well into the trillions. In that world, the debates we have about tax policy today simply won’t apply as we will be in a fundamentally different situation.

今天类似的份额将意味着 6000 亿美元的财富，而当今世界首富（埃隆·马斯克）已经超过这一数字，[约为 7000 亿美元](https://www.reuters.com/business/autos-transportation/elon-musk-becomes-first-person-worth-700-billion-after-court-ruling-pay-package-2025-12-20/)。因此，甚至在 AI 的大部分经济影响显现*之前*，我们就已经处于历史上前所未有的财富集中水平。如果我们得到“天才国度”，不难想象 AI 公司、半导体公司以及下游应用公司每年创造约 3 万亿美元收入，[^43]估值约 30 万亿美元，并产生数万亿美元的个人财富。在那个世界里，我们今天关于税收政策的辩论根本不适用，因为我们将处于完全不同的境地。

Related to this, the coupling of this economic concentration of wealth with the political system already concerns me. AI datacenters already represent a substantial fraction of US economic growth,[^44] and are thus strongly tying together the financial interests of large tech companies (which are increasingly focused on either AI or AI infrastructure) and the political interests of the government in a way that can produce perverse incentives. We already see this through the reluctance of tech companies to criticize the US government, and the government’s support for extreme anti-regulatory policies on AI.

与此相关，这种经济财富集中与政治体系的结合已经让我担忧。AI 数据中心已经占美国经济增长的相当一部分，[^44]因此把大型科技公司（越来越专注于 AI 或 AI 基础设施）的财务利益与政府的政治利益紧密绑在一起，可能产生扭曲的激励。我们已经看到这一点：科技公司不愿批评美国政府，而政府支持极端反监管的 AI 政策。

### 防御措施（Defenses）

What can be done about this? First, and most obviously, companies should simply choose not to be part of it. Anthropic has always strived to be a policy actor and not a political one, and to maintain our authentic views whatever the administration. We’ve spoken up in favor of [sensible AI regulation](https://www.nytimes.com/2025/06/05/opinion/anthropic-ceo-regulate-transparency.html) and [export controls](https://www.wsj.com/opinion/trump-can-keep-americas-ai-advantage-china-chips-data-eccdce91?gaa_at=eafs&gaa_n=AWEtsqespyCL3hcx_9DpJWbIPX1vrtS1raPgFoBNK8ltnrjwedpX2NuvVu1K_yZ1arw%3D&gaa_ts=696c6c70&gaa_sig=wef9kKocpL9PU07UoiPS6kj_o_Nwy_VSufM6gltIvdjQFhb8HRLtpSzp4Z8WDG6v3leg0ODX4HOJjWblvZe2pw%3D%3D) that are in the public interest, even when these are at odds with government policy.[^45]

对此能做些什么？

首先，也最明显的是，公司应当选择不参与其中。Anthropic 一直努力做一个政策参与者，而不是政治参与者，无论哪届政府，都保持我们真实的观点。我们曾公开支持[合理的 AI 监管](https://www.nytimes.com/2025/06/05/opinion/anthropic-ceo-regulate-transparency.html)和[出口管制](https://www.wsj.com/opinion/trump-can-keep-americas-ai-advantage-china-chips-data-eccdce91?gaa_at=eafs&gaa_n=AWEtsqespyCL3hcx_9DpJWbIPX1vrtS1raPgFoBNK8ltnrjwedpX2NuvVu1K_yZ1arw%3D&gaa_ts=696c6c70&gaa_sig=wef9kKocpL9PU07UoiPS6kj_o_Nwy_VSufM6gltIvdjQFhb8HRLtpSzp4Z8WDG6v3leg0ODX4HOJjWblvZe2pw%3D%3D)，即使这些与政府政策相悖，因为我们认为它们符合公共利益。[^45]

Many people have told me that we should stop doing this, that it could lead to unfavorable treatment, but in the year we’ve been doing it, Anthropic’s valuation has increased by over 6x, an almost unprecedented jump at our commercial scale.

许多人告诉我，我们应该停止这样做，这可能导致不利对待；但在我们这样做的一年里，Anthropic 的估值增长了 6 倍以上，以我们的商业规模而言，这是几乎前所未有的跃升。

Second, the AI industry needs a healthier relationship with government—one based on substantive policy engagement rather than political alignment. Our choice to engage on policy substance rather than politics is sometimes read as a tactical error or failure to “read the room” rather than a principled decision, and that framing concerns me. In a healthy democracy, companies should be able to advocate for good policy for its own sake. Related to this, a public backlash against AI is brewing: this could be a corrective, but it’s currently unfocused. Much of it targets issues that aren’t actually problems (like [datacenter](https://newsletter.semianalysis.com/p/from-tokens-to-burgers-a-water-footprint?hide_intro_popup=true) [water usage](https://andymasley.substack.com/p/the-ai-water-issue-is-fake)) and proposes solutions (like datacenter bans or poorly designed wealth taxes) that wouldn’t address the real concerns. The underlying issue that deserves attention is ensuring that AI development remains accountable to the public interest, not captured by any particular political or commercial alliance, and it seems important to focus the public discussion there.

第二，AI 行业需要与政府建立更健康的关系——一种基于实质性政策参与、而非政治结盟的关系。我们选择参与政策实质而非政治，有时被解读为战术错误或未能“读懂气氛”，而不是有原则的决定，这种解读让我担忧。在健康的民主国家，公司应当能够为良好政策本身而倡导。与此相关，一场针对 AI 的公众反弹正在酝酿：这可能是一种纠正，但目前没有聚焦。许多反弹针对的并不是真正的问题（比如[数据中心](https://newsletter.semianalysis.com/p/from-tokens-to-burgers-a-water-footprint?hide_intro_popup=true)[用水](https://andymasley.substack.com/p/the-ai-water-issue-is-fake)），提出的解决方案（比如禁止数据中心或设计糟糕的财富税）也不会解决真正的担忧。真正值得关注的问题是，确保 AI 发展仍然对公共利益负责，不被任何特定的政治或商业联盟俘获，把公共讨论引向这里似乎很重要。

Third, the macroeconomic interventions I described earlier in this section, as well as a resurgence of private philanthropy, can help to balance the economic scales, addressing both the job displacement and concentration of economic power problems at once. We should look to the history of our country here: even in the Gilded Age, industrialists such as [Rockefeller](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S096262981500027X) and [Carnegie](https://www.carnegie.org/about/our-history/gospelofwealth/) felt a strong obligation to society at large, a feeling that society had contributed enormously to their success and they needed to give back. That spirit seems to be increasingly missing today, and I think it is a large part of the way out of this economic dilemma. Those who are at the forefront of AI’s economic boom should be willing to give away both their wealth and their power.

第三，我在本节前面描述的宏观经济干预，以及私人慈善的复兴，可以帮助平衡经济天平，同时解决岗位流失和经济权力集中两个问题。我们应当回顾美国的历史：即使在镀金时代，[洛克菲勒](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S096262981500027X)和[卡内基](https://www.carnegie.org/about/our-history/gospelofwealth/)这样的工业家也对社会怀有强烈的义务感，觉得社会为他们的成功做出了巨大贡献，他们需要回馈。这种精神在今天似乎越来越缺失，我认为这是走出经济困境的重要部分。站在 AI 经济繁荣最前沿的人，应当愿意交出财富和权力。

## 5. 无尽的黑暗之海（Black seas of infinity）

### 间接效应（Indirect effects）

This last section is a catchall for unknown unknowns, particularly things that could go wrong as an indirect result of positive advances in AI and the resulting acceleration of science and technology in general. Suppose we address all the risks described so far, and begin to reap the benefits of AI. We will likely get a “ [century of scientific and economic progress compressed into a decade](https://www.darioamodei.com/essay/machines-of-loving-grace),” and this will be hugely positive for the world, but we will then have to contend with the problems that arise from this rapid rate of progress, and those problems may come at us fast. We may also encounter other risks that occur indirectly as a consequence of AI progress and are hard to anticipate in advance.

最后一节是一个笼统的类别，用来讨论未知的未知，尤其是那些可能作为 AI 积极进展以及由此带来的科技加速的间接结果而出错的事情。假设我们解决了迄今为止描述的所有风险，并开始收获 AI 的好处。我们很可能得到一个“[把一百年的科学和经济进步压缩到十年](https://www.darioamodei.com/essay/machines-of-loving-grace)”的时代，这对世界极为有利；但随后我们必须应对这种快速发展带来的问题，而这些问题可能来得很快。我们还可能遇到其他风险，它们是 AI 进步的间接后果，难以事先预料。

By the nature of unknown unknowns it is impossible to make an exhaustive list, but I’ll list three possible concerns as illustrative examples for what we should be watching for:

由于未知的未知的性质，不可能列出完整清单，但我会举三个可能的担忧作为例子，说明我们应该留意什么：

- **Rapid advances in biology.** If we do get a century of medical progress in a few years, it is possible that we will greatly increase the human lifespan, and there is a chance we also gain radical capabilities like the ability to increase human intelligence or radically modify human biology. Those would be big changes in what is possible, happening very quickly. They could be positive if responsibly done (which is my hope, as described in *Machines of Loving Grace*), but there is always a risk they go very wrong—for example, if efforts to make humans smarter also make them more unstable or power-seeking. There is also the issue of “ [uploads](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mind_uploading) ” or “whole brain emulation,” digital human minds instantiated in software, which might someday help humanity transcend its physical limitations, but which also carry [risks I find disquieting](https://qntm.org/mmacevedo).
- **AI changes human life in an unhealthy way.** A world with billions of intelligences that are much smarter than humans at everything is going to be a very weird world to live in. Even if AI doesn’t actively aim to attack humans (Section 1), and isn’t explicitly used for oppression or control by states (Section 3), there is a lot that could go wrong short of this, via normal business incentives and nominally consensual transactions. We see early hints of this in the concerns about AI psychosis, [AI driving people to suicide](https://www.cbsnews.com/news/chatgpt-lawsuit-colordo-man-suicide-openai-sam-altman/), and concerns about romantic relationships with AIs. As an example, could powerful AIs invent some new religion and convert millions of people to it? Could most people end up “addicted” in some way to AI interactions? Could people end up being “puppeted” by AI systems, where an AI essentially watches their every move and tells them exactly what to do and say at all times, leading to a “good” life but one that lacks freedom or any pride of accomplishment? It would not be hard to generate dozens of these scenarios if I sat down with the creator of [*Black Mirror*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_Mirror) and tried to brainstorm them. I think this points to the importance of things like improving [Claude’s Constitution](https://www.anthropic.com/constitution), over and above what is necessary for preventing the issues in Section 1. Making sure that AI models *really* have their users’ long-term interests at heart, in a way thoughtful people would endorse rather than in some subtly distorted way, seems critical.
- **Human purpose.** This is related to the previous point, but it’s not so much about specific human interactions with AI systems as it is about how human life changes in general in a world with powerful AI. Will humans be able to find purpose and meaning in such a world? I think this is a matter of attitude: as I said in *Machines of Loving Grace*, I think human purpose does not depend on being the best in the world at something, and humans can find purpose even over very long periods of time through stories and projects that they love. We simply need to break the link between the generation of economic value and self-worth and meaning. But that is a transition society has to make, and there is always the risk we don’t handle it well.

- **生物学的快速进步。** 如果我们真的在几年内取得一个世纪的医学进步，人类的寿命可能大幅延长，我们也有可能获得诸如提高人类智力或从根本上改造人类生物学之类的激进能力。这些将是可能性的巨大变化，而且发生得非常快。如果负责任地进行，它们可能是积极的（这是我的希望，正如《仁慈造物之机》所述），但总有可能出大问题——例如，让人类更聪明的努力也让他们更不稳定或更追求权力。还有“[上传](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mind_uploading)”或“全脑仿真”的问题，即在软件中实例化数字人类心智，这也许有朝一日能帮助人类超越身体限制，但也带有[我觉得不安的风险](https://qntm.org/mmacevedo)。
- **AI 以不健康的方式改变人类生活。** 一个拥有数十亿个在所有事情上都比人类聪明得多的智能的世界，将是一个非常奇怪的世界。即使 AI 不主动攻击人类（第一节），也没有被国家明确用于压迫或控制（第三节），在正常商业激励和名义上双方同意的交易中，仍有很多可能出错的地方。我们在对 AI 精神病、[AI 驱使人们自杀](https://www.cbsnews.com/news/chatgpt-lawsuit-colordo-man-suicide-openai-sam-altman/)的担忧，以及对与 AI 建立恋爱关系的担忧中，已经看到早期迹象。例如，强大 AI 会不会发明某种新宗教，让数百万人皈依？大多数人会不会以某种方式对 AI 互动“上瘾”？人们会不会最终被 AI 系统“操纵”，AI 本质上监视他们的一举一动，并时刻告诉他们该做什么、说什么，从而过上一种“好”生活，却缺乏自由或成就感？如果我坐下来和《[黑镜](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_Mirror)》的创作者一起头脑风暴，不难想出几十种这样的场景。我认为，这说明改进 [Claude 的宪法](https://www.anthropic.com/constitution)之类的事情很重要，其重要性超出防止第一节问题所需。确保 AI 模型*真正*把用户的长期利益放在心上，以一种有思想的人会认可、而不是某种微妙扭曲的方式，似乎至关重要。
- **人类的目的。** 这与上一点有关，但与其说是人类与 AI 系统的具体互动，不如说是在拥有强大 AI 的世界里人类生活总体上如何变化。人类能在这样的世界里找到目的和意义吗？我认为这是一个态度问题：正如我在《仁慈造物之机》中所说，人类的目的不取决于在某件事上做到世界最好；即使在很长的时间里，人类也能通过自己热爱的故事和项目找到目的。我们只需要打破经济价值创造与自我价值、意义之间的联系。但这是社会必须完成的转变，而我们总有不妥善处理的风险。

My hope with all of these potential problems is that in a world with powerful AI that we trust not to kill us, that is not the tool of an oppressive government, and that is genuinely working on our behalf, we can use AI itself to anticipate and prevent these problems. But that is not guaranteed—like all of the other risks, it is something we have to handle with care.

对于所有这些潜在问题，我的希望是：在一个我们相信不会杀死我们、不是压迫性政府的工具、真正为我们服务的强大 AI 的世界里，我们可以利用 AI 本身来预见和防止这些问题。但这没有保证——像所有其他风险一样，我们必须小心处理。

## 人类的考验（Humanity’s test）

Reading this essay may give the impression that we are in a daunting situation. I certainly found it daunting to write, in contrast with *Machines of Loving Grace*, which felt like giving form and structure to surpassingly beautiful music that had been echoing in my head for years. And there is much about the situation that genuinely is hard. AI brings threats to humanity from multiple directions, and there is genuine tension between the different dangers, where mitigating some of them risks making others worse if we do not thread the needle extremely carefully.

读这篇文章，可能会让人觉得我们处境艰难。我写它时确实觉得艰难，这与《仁慈造物之机》形成对比——写那篇文章就像把多年来在脑海中回响的绝美音乐赋予形式和结构。而眼下的局势确实有很多真正困难的地方。AI 从多个方向给人类带来威胁，不同危险之间存在真实的张力；如果不极其小心地穿针引线，缓解其中一些风险可能会让另一些更糟。

Taking time to carefully build AI systems so they do not autonomously threaten humanity is in genuine tension with the need for democratic nations to stay ahead of authoritarian nations and not be subjugated by them. But in turn, the same AI-enabled tools that are necessary to fight autocracies can, if taken too far, be turned inward to create tyranny in our own countries. AI-driven terrorism could kill millions through the misuse of biology, but an overreaction to this risk could lead us down the road to an autocratic surveillance state. The labor and economic concentration effects of AI, in addition to being grave problems in their own right, may force us to face the other problems in an environment of public anger and perhaps even civil unrest, rather than being able to call on the better angels of our nature. Above all, the sheer *number* of risks, including unknown ones, and the need to deal with all of them at once, creates an intimidating gauntlet that humanity must run.

眼下的困难之处，在于几种风险彼此拉扯：

- 花时间仔细构建 AI 系统，使其不会自主威胁人类，与民主国家需要领先于威权国家、不被它们征服之间存在真实的张力。
- 但反过来，对抗专制所需的同样 AI 工具，如果走得太远，也可能被转向内部，在我们自己的国家制造暴政。
- AI 驱动的恐怖主义可能通过滥用生物学杀死数百万人；但对这一风险的过度反应，可能把我们引向专制的监控国家。
- AI 的劳动力与经济集中效应，除了本身是严重问题，还可能迫使我们在公众愤怒甚至内乱的环境中面对其他问题，而无法呼唤我们本性中更好的天使。
- 最重要的是，风险的*数量*之多——包括未知风险——以及必须同时应对所有风险，构成了人类必须跑完的令人生畏的难关。

Furthermore, the last few years should make clear that the idea of stopping or even substantially slowing the technology is fundamentally untenable. The formula for building powerful AI systems is incredibly simple, so much so that it can almost be said to emerge spontaneously from the right combination of data and raw computation. Its creation was probably inevitable the instant humanity invented the transistor, or arguably even earlier when we first learned to control fire. If one company does not build it, others will do so nearly as fast. If all companies in democratic countries stopped or slowed development, by mutual agreement or regulatory decree, then authoritarian countries would simply keep going. Given the incredible economic and military value of the technology, together with the lack of any meaningful enforcement mechanism, I don’t see [how we could possibly convince them to stop](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Security_dilemma).

此外，过去几年应当让人明白，停止甚至大幅放慢这项技术的想法根本站不住脚。构建强大 AI 系统的配方极其简单，以至于几乎可以说，它会在数据与原始算力的正确组合下自发涌现。它的诞生，也许在人类发明晶体管的那一刻就已不可避免，或者更早，在我们学会控制火的时候。如果一家公司不造，其他公司几乎会以同样快的速度造出来。如果民主国家的所有公司通过共同协议或监管法令停止或放慢开发，威权国家只会继续推进。鉴于这项技术惊人的经济和军事价值，加上缺乏任何有意义的执行机制，我看不出[我们怎么可能说服它们停下来](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Security_dilemma)。

I do see a path to a *slight* moderation in AI development that is compatible with a [realist view of geopolitics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Realism_/(international_relations/)). That path involves slowing down the march of autocracies towards powerful AI for a few years by denying them the resources they need to build it,[^46] namely chips and semiconductor manufacturing equipment. This in turn gives democratic countries a buffer that they can “spend” on building powerful AI more carefully, with more attention to its risks, while still proceeding fast enough to comfortably beat the autocracies. The race between AI companies within democracies can then be handled under the umbrella of a common legal framework, via a mixture of industry standards and regulation.

我确实看到一条道路，可以在符合[现实主义地缘政治观](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Realism_/(international_relations/))的前提下，*略微*放慢 AI 发展。这条道路是：通过拒绝提供构建强大 AI 所需的资源，[^46]即芯片和半导体制造设备，把专制国家迈向强大 AI 的步伐拖慢几年。这反过来给民主国家一个缓冲，可以把这段时间“花”在更仔细地构建强大 AI 上，更加关注其风险，同时仍以足够快的速度推进，轻松击败专制国家。民主国家内部 AI 公司之间的竞赛，则可以在共同法律框架下，通过行业标准和监管的混合来管理。

Anthropic has advocated very hard for this path, by pushing for chip export controls and judicious regulation of AI, but even these seemingly common-sense proposals have largely been rejected by policymakers in the United States (which is the country where it’s most important to have them). There is so much money to be made with AI—literally trillions of dollars per year—that even the simplest measures are finding it difficult to overcome the [political economy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Political_economy) inherent in AI. This is the trap: AI is so powerful, such a glittering prize, that it is very difficult for human civilization to impose any restraints on it at all.

Anthropic 一直大力倡导这条道路，推动芯片出口管制和对 AI 的审慎监管；但即使是这些看似常识性的提议，也基本被美国政策制定者拒绝（而美国恰恰是最需要这些政策的国家）。AI 能赚太多钱了——字面意义上每年数万亿美元——以至于即使最简单的措施也难以克服 AI 内在的[政治经济学](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Political_economy)。这就是陷阱：AI 如此强大，是如此耀眼的奖品，以至于人类文明很难对它施加任何限制。

I can imagine, as Sagan did in *Contact*, that this same story plays out on thousands of worlds. A species gains sentience, learns to use tools, begins the exponential ascent of technology, faces the crises of industrialization and nuclear weapons, and if it survives those, confronts the hardest and final challenge when it learns how to shape sand into machines that think. Whether we survive that test and go on to build the beautiful society described in *Machines of Loving Grace*, or succumb to slavery and destruction, will depend on our character and our determination as a species, our spirit and our soul.

我可以想象，正如萨根在《接触》中所做的那样，同样的故事在成千上万个世界上演。一个物种获得意识，学会使用工具，开始技术的指数级上升，面对工业化和核武器的危机；如果它挺过这些，就会在学会把沙子塑造成会思考的机器时，面对最艰难、最终的挑战。

我们是挺过这场考验，继续建设《仁慈造物之机》中描述的美好社会，还是屈服于奴役和毁灭，将取决于我们作为一个物种的品格和决心，取决于我们的精神和灵魂。

Despite the many obstacles, I believe humanity has the strength inside itself to pass this test. I am encouraged and inspired by the thousands of researchers who have devoted their careers to helping us understand and steer AI models, and to shaping the character and constitution of these models. I think there is now a good chance that those efforts bear fruit in time to matter. I am encouraged that at least some companies have [stated they’ll pay](https://openai.com/index/preparing-for-future-ai-capabilities-in-biology/) meaningful commercial costs to block their models from contributing to the threat of bioterrorism. I am encouraged that a few brave people have resisted the prevailing political winds and [passed](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/billTextClient.xhtml?bill_id=202520260SB53) [legislation](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/bills/2025/A6453/amendment/A) that puts the first early seeds of sensible guardrails on AI systems. I am encouraged that the [public understands that AI carries risks and wants those risks addressed](https://www.pewresearch.org/internet/2025/04/03/views-of-risks-opportunities-and-regulation-of-ai/#6d2b9b266433bfda6c8fc2f498738a4c). I am encouraged by the indomitable spirit of freedom around the world and the determination to resist tyranny wherever it occurs.

尽管障碍重重，我相信人类内心有力量通过这场考验。让我感到鼓舞和启发的，是成千上万的研究人员，他们毕生致力于帮助我们理解和引导 AI 模型，塑造这些模型的品格和宪法。我认为，这些努力现在有很好的机会及时结出果实。

让我鼓舞的是，至少有一些公司[表示愿意承担](https://openai.com/index/preparing-for-future-ai-capabilities-in-biology/)可观的商业成本，阻止它们的模型助长生物恐怖主义威胁；少数勇敢的人顶住当前的政治风向，[通过](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/billTextClient.xhtml?bill_id=202520260SB53)[立法](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/bills/2025/A6453/amendment/A)，为 AI 系统设置了第一批合理防护的种子；[公众理解 AI 带有风险，并希望这些风险得到解决](https://www.pewresearch.org/internet/2025/04/03/views-of-risks-opportunities-and-regulation-of-ai/#6d2b9b266433bfda6c8fc2f498738a4c)；世界各地不屈的自由精神，以及在任何地方都抵抗暴政的决心。

But we will need to step up our efforts if we want to succeed. The first step is for those closest to the technology to simply tell the truth about the situation humanity is in, which I have always tried to do; I’m doing so more explicitly and with greater urgency with this essay. The next step will be convincing the world’s thinkers, policymakers, companies, and citizens of the imminence and overriding importance of this issue—that it is worth expending thought and political capital on this in comparison to the thousands of other issues that dominate the news every day. Then there will be a time for courage, for enough people to buck the prevailing trends and stand on principle, even in the face of threats to their economic interests and personal safety.

但如果想成功，我们需要加大努力。第一步，是让最接近这项技术的人如实说明人类所处的境况——我一直努力这样做；在这篇文章里，我做得更明确、更紧迫。

下一步，是说服世界上的思想家、政策制定者、公司和公民，让他们相信这个问题迫在眉睫、至关重要——与每天占据新闻的成千上万个其他问题相比，它值得投入思想和政治资本。

然后，将迎来勇气的时刻：需要有足够多的人逆流而上、坚持原则，即使面临自身经济利益和人身安全的威胁。

The years in front of us will be impossibly hard, asking more of us than we think we can give. But in my time as a researcher, leader, and citizen, I have seen enough courage and nobility to believe that we can win—that when put in the darkest circumstances, humanity has a way of gathering, seemingly at the last minute, the strength and wisdom needed to prevail. We have no time to lose.

横亘在我们面前的未来岁月，将艰难得不可思议，向我们索取的东西会超出我们以为自己能付出的极限。但在我作为研究者、领导者和公民的岁月里，我见过足够多的勇气和高贵，因此我相信我们能赢——相信在最黑暗的处境中，人类总有办法在看似最后一刻汇聚起取胜所需的力量和智慧。我们已经没有时间可以浪费了。
